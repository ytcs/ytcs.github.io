Which of six requirements can each of twelve discrete, lattice and modified-continuum families of quantum gravity keep? The requirements are Lorentz invariance, a massless universal spin-2 graviton with locality, positivity, chiral matter, horizon thermodynamics with a dynamical law, and a computed number (section 1). The answer is given per family, in Table 1. In each family at least one requirement is excluded under stated hypotheses, or is available only at a cost, and no family has been shown to keep all six. Most of Table 1 is empty: each family has been tested against one or two requirements, and the empty cells are not findings. The families are described in section 2; string theory, loop quantum gravity, spin foams and causal dynamical triangulations are not among them. Eight further pages, on cosmology and the dark sector, test whether structures of the gravity sector fix cosmological numbers, which is the sixth requirement; they belong to no family and are indexed, with the routes to Newton's constant, in Table 3.

Poisson sprinklings, the point sets behind most rows, are the standard causal-set model, from the programme of Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin [[BLMS]](#ref-blms), and the causal-set d'Alembertian and action are due to Benincasa and Dowker [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd). The pages of Table 2 prove, among other statements, that no non-negative weighting of horizon pairs gives an area law in three to twelve dimensions, and that Haar-random lattices are Poincaré-invariant and not Poisson.

## 1. The six requirements

*Lorentz invariance* means that the substrate selects no frame and that gravitons, photons and matter share one limiting speed.

*A graviton and locality* means a massless spin-2 graviton that couples with equal strength to the energy and momentum of every species, together with an action that is local or whose non-locality is controlled. The column also takes the accuracy with which a wave operator defined on the substrate reproduces the continuum d'Alembertian, and the compatibility of finite-dimensional local algebras with positive-energy translations.

*Positivity* means non-negative weights in sums over configurations and, for an entropy built from horizon pairs, a non-negative amount per pair; positive states, so that probabilities are non-negative and the energy is bounded below; and a propagator that is a non-negative superposition of free propagators (a positive spectral weight, the usual criterion for the absence of ghosts).

*Chiral matter* means one-handed fermions with one species per field and no doublers, the extra species that a naive lattice discretisation produces.

*Horizon thermodynamics and a dynamical law* means an entropy proportional to the horizon area, a temperature, and a law of change. The law of change is an action, a sum over histories or a rule that selects a manifold-like geometry; the column also takes a rule that completes or predicts field values on a fixed sprinkling. It takes in addition the boundary terms of an action that carry the area term, and the entropy of the vacuum on a region of a field theory. The pages test the area dependence of horizon counts, boundary terms and local rules of change; none tests a temperature.

*A computed number* means Newton's constant in units of the discreteness length, or a quantity of cosmology or the dark sector, obtained and not fitted.

These six are the columns of Table 1. They follow what a calculation can test in each family. The eight cosmology and dark-sector pages are on the map because they ask whether structures of the gravity sector, such as a global constraint on the vacuum energy, a horizon integrality condition or a modulus coupling, fix a cosmological number.

## 2. The families

A *Poisson sprinkling* of a manifold is a random set of points of density $$\ell^{-d}$$ in $$d$$-dimensional spacetime, where $$\ell$$ is the discreteness length. The causal order of the manifold is kept, and the resulting partial order is a causal set. One *realisation* is one sampled point set. Table 1 splits this family by what is built on it, in the rows that begin with "Poisson" and, for sums over orders, "Causal sets". The causal-set d'Alembertian at a point $$x$$ is a sum over the causal past of $$x$$ in which a point $$y$$ has a weight $$w_m$$ that depends on the number $$m$$ of sprinkled points between $$y$$ and $$x$$ (the layer of $$y$$) [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd). Its mean over the sprinkling, the *mean-field* operator, is Lorentz invariant and is described by a *kernel*, the layer weights written as a function of the proper time of the interval.

A *horizon count* refers to a region $$P$$ below a spacelike slice, whose boundary is a horizon. It adds, over every pair $$x\prec y$$ with $$x$$ in $$P$$ and $$y$$ outside $$P$$ and below the slice, a weight $$w_m$$ that depends on the number $$m$$ of other elements in the future of $$x$$ below the slice ([horizon counts and the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)). The causal-set action sums interval counts in the same way. Two kinds of *rule of change* act on a causal set. A *completion rule* predicts the value of a field at a sprinkled element from its values at finitely many other elements ([local rules of change on a sprinkling](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)). A sum over orders weights partial orders by an action; almost all finite partial orders have three layers of sizes close to $$n/4$$, $$n/2$$ and $$n/4$$ (the Kleitman–Rothschild orders), so the question is whether the action suppresses them. Fermion fields live on the points or, for Kähler–Dirac fields, on chains of the order.

*Quantum counts* ask whether the number operators of a quantum field in a Poincaré-invariant state, or in any positive-energy vacuum, can reproduce a sprinkling, and whether finite-dimensional local algebras are compatible with positive-energy translations. *Mixed-Poisson and signed sprinklings* let the density be random, or the weights of configurations be signed or complex.

*Invariant random lattices* are lattices placed at random so that their *law*, the probability distribution of the point set, is Poincaré-invariant, although no single realisation is: Haar-random lattices, and the orbit lattice of the integral quadratic form $$Q_7=7x_0^2-x_1^2-x_2^2-x_3^2$$ of signature $$(1,3)$$. A translation-invariant operator acts on plane waves by a multiplier, its *symbol*. A *lattice with an exact discrete Lorentz group* has exact translations and a Zariski-dense subgroup of the Lorentz group acting on it, such as the integer Lorentz group; Zariski-dense means contained in no proper subgroup cut out by polynomial equations. *Invariant random sets of null lines or null hyperplanes* are random sets whose law is Poincaré-invariant.

*Lattice gravitons* are the gravitons of Hamiltonian lattice theories with lattice translation symmetry. A *preferred frame* or a compact residual symmetry group is what is left when a substrate selects a frame. *Local qubit and rotor models* have emergent linearised diffeomorphisms. Three continuum families with a modified propagator or action complete the list: propagators with positive spectral weight, generalised free fields with a ghost (a field whose two-point function has a negative spectral weight) and induced gravity, in which Newton's constant is generated by the quantum fluctuations of matter fields.

The thirteenth group is not a family. It consists of eight pages on cosmology and the dark sector: linear constraints on the vacuum energy, the sign of vacuum-matter energy exchange, structures that leave the cosmological constant undetermined, an apparent negative neutrino mass in BAO data, the coupling of a dark-dimension modulus to baryons, an instability in Freezing Gravity (a dark-energy model with a freely prescribed expansion history), the low multipoles of holographic cosmology, and a positivity bound on non-Gaussianity. Section 6 treats them.

## 3. The map

A parenthesis names the one qualifier that changes the claim most, or the kind of evidence. An empty cell means that the combination is not examined here, not that the requirement holds.

Four notes apply to Table 1. The Lorentz entry of the first row concerns the Poisson law, which is Poincaré-invariant by construction, and does not test whether all species share one limiting speed; the two rows of chiral matter that use a frame give up Lorentz invariance by construction, and their Lorentz cells are empty because the question is not examined for them. The routes to a number that are not tied to one family are in section 6.

## 5. How the results depend on each other

Several results rest on the non-compactness of the Lorentz group. No Lorentz-invariant probability measure exists on the hyperboloid of future unit timelike vectors, so a frame can be neither averaged away nor read off a Lorentz-invariant point set ([frame leakage](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/), Proposition 6 and Corollary 7). Invariant locally finite measures also fail to exist on the spaces of null lines and null hyperplanes, which empties invariant random sets of such lines (Theorem 4 of [Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/)). A neighbour rule that sees frames only modulo an arithmetic lattice selects a finite non-empty set with probability zero (Theorem 6 there).

Non-compactness does not remove every invariant object. The space of unimodular lattices carries a finite measure invariant under $$SL(d,\mathbb R)$$, and so under the Lorentz group, so that Haar-random lattices exist, are Poincaré-invariant in law and are not Poisson (Theorem 1 there; Proposition 9 of [quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/)).

A different mechanism, the Zariski density of an arithmetic Lorentz group, acts on lattices. Exact lattice translations together with such a group make the orbit of every non-torsion momentum dense in the Brillouin torus (the space of lattice momenta modulo $$2\pi$$), which leaves flat bands ([arithmetic Lorentz group](/quantum-gravity/discrete-lorentz-group/), Corollary 1 and Theorem 1). Its Proposition 1 says that a bounded invariant function on finitely many shells has no dispersion on any open set; [kernel-scale kinematics](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/), Proposition 4, applies it to the stabiliser of the orbit lattice, which is Zariski dense, and concludes that a kernel on finitely many shells has no symbol in a single realisation.

Positivity enters in several forms. A propagator with positive Stieltjes weight has spectral dimension at least the dimension of space ([spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/)). The mean-field d'Alembertian in four dimensions has no such weight ([causal-set d'Alembertian](/quantum-gravity/causal-box-spectrum/), which cites the spectral-dimension result), and [mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/) extends the statement to interval-local matrix kernels (Theorem 17) and asks what is lost when the process, not the operator, is allowed to be signed. For horizon counts, an area-law response needs weights of both signs ([horizon counts](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)); [horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/) cites that result and asks for exactness at every gradient order, and [dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/) and [boundary terms](/quantum-gravity/boundary-terms/) cite it too.

The d'Alembertian results and the horizon-count results share a threshold in the tail exponent of the kernel. The Mellin transform of a kernel, its integral against a power of the interval volume or proper time, fixes both the low-momentum symbol of the d'Alembertian and the gradient expansion of the horizon count, and exactness to all orders means zeros of that transform. A d'Alembertian kernel bounded by $$e^{-\lambda\tau^{\beta}}$$ in proper time $$\tau$$, whose symbol equals the continuum one to all orders, needs $$\beta\le1/2$$, and such kernels exist for every $$\beta<1/2$$ (Theorems 6 and 7 of [causal-set d'Alembertian](/quantum-gravity/causal-box-spectrum/)). A horizon-count kernel with an area law at every gradient order cannot decay like $$e^{-c\sqrt x}$$ or faster in the linear size $$x$$, and kernels with tails $$e^{-cx^{\kappa}}$$ exist for every $$\kappa<1/2$$ (Theorems 1 and 4 of [horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/)). The d'Alembertian page constructs its flat operators itself, notes that Theorem 4 of the kernel page is a variant with simple zeros, and quotes Theorem 1 of the kernel page for the boundary exponent 1/2, which is excluded there for kernels with the required zeros and open for the d'Alembertian.

On a lattice, [the conserved lattice momentum](/quantum-gravity/lattice-diffeomorphisms/) is excluded when its symbol is analytic and low-energy graviton scattering does not vanish. [Universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/) builds on it and excludes an exactly cone-shaped band for real-analytic periodic symbols, and [emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/) cites both. Proposition 5 of the arithmetic-Lorentz-group page shows that no three-point vertex that conserves lattice momentum modulo $$2\pi$$ is covariant under the induced action of a non-monomial Lorentz transformation. For counts, [quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/) shows that Poincaré-invariant, locally normal bosonic states of finite mean density have mixed-Poisson counts. [Integer counts](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/) cites that result and shows that integer counts of non-zero density cannot sit in a positive-energy vacuum when their two-point function is real (Corollary 2 for translation-invariant classical point processes); its application to condensate states, the coherent states of quantum sprinklings with one constant wave function, is a sketch.

The remaining pages form clusters. The matter pages share one question, whether a frame is needed: the order-only page identifies a global spinor space with a global frame, the chirality and reflection-positivity pages study operators with and without one, and the frame-leakage page, which all three cite, computes the cost of a frame in a regulator. Five of the dark-sector pages cite each other: [linear constraints on the cosmological constant](/quantum-gravity/linear-lambda-constraints/), [the sign of vacuum-matter energy exchange](/quantum-gravity/vacuum-matter-sign/), [negative neutrino mass in BAO data](/quantum-gravity/neutrino-mimic/), [matter-induced instability in Freezing Gravity](/quantum-gravity/matter-induced-instability/) and [structures that leave the cosmological constant undetermined](/quantum-gravity/dark-sector-moduli/). The last also cites the dimensionless-constants page, which ties the cluster to the causal-set pages. Three pages cite no other page, and no other page of Table 2 cites them: [dark-dimension modulus coupling](/quantum-gravity/dark-dimension-coupling/), [low multipoles of holographic cosmology](/quantum-gravity/holographic-cosmology/) and [the positivity wedge](/quantum-gravity/positivity-wedge/).

Table 3 lists the routes to a measured or computed number, and the constraints from data, that have a page. The first four columns give the quantity, the route with its main hypotheses, what is found and the page.

| Cosmological constant | Global constraint linear in the stress tensor with causal-vector or sequestering weights; flat universe with pressure between zero and a third of the density, or energy conditions | **excluded**: a positive value | [Linear constraints](/quantum-gravity/linear-lambda-constraints/) |
| Cosmological constant | Vacuum energy of a free scalar on flat FRW; Kodama and Verlinde conditions; logarithmic coefficient of causal-set counts | The conditions leave the value undetermined | [Undetermined cosmological constant](/quantum-gravity/dark-sector-moduli/) |
| Cosmological constant | Volume term in the mean horizon count of a Poisson sprinkling; finite weightings, conformally static backgrounds, $$d\ge3$$ | **excluded**: such a term | [Dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/) |

No row of Table 3 derives a measured number. The results are stated with cases left outside them. For the linear constraints, these are weights set by a preferred frame with an energy-to-trace ratio above $$-1/4$$, stiff matter (pressure equal to density) and constraints that are not linear. For the undetermined cosmological constant, they are a light spectator field during inflation and routes that fix the dark mass by linking the dark and visible confinement scales. For the vacuum-matter sign, they are a vacuum field that starts away from the minimum of its potential, so that it does not track it, or has no minimum.

## 7. Limits of the tables and open questions

Table 1 does not say that the means fix an entropy per horizon pair: the entropy unit enters as a free factor beside the weight normalisation ([dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/)). The horizon column does not say that a horizon temperature is tested; no page tests one. The sign of the weighted horizon count on a given marked order, such as a three-element chain, is not fixed by the sign of its mean ([horizon counts](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)).

Several questions are undecided. For lattice gravitons, it is open whether a lattice amplitude exists whose soft factor is gauge invariant up to relative order $$(Ea)^{r}$$ ([universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/)). For chiral matter on a Poisson sprinkling, the chirality pages leave $$\gamma_5$$-Hermitian completions of a retarded block and chirally asymmetric Wilson terms outside their theorems, and whether the disorder average of reflection positivity holds for the unsymmetrised ensemble is open ([chirality obstructions](/quantum-gravity/frame-free-chirality/), [Wilson terms and reflection positivity](/quantum-gravity/frames-and-reflection-positivity/)). For the d'Alembertian, a kernel with a non-locality scale much larger than the discreteness scale may reduce the single-realisation fluctuation, and the dependence of the bound on that scale is open ([kernel-scale kinematics](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/)); no weights are known that realise a heavy-tailed kernel ([horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/)). For laws of change, those that read common predecessors, interval contents or links are not covered, and suppression of three-layer orders at Kleitman–Rothschild proportions is undecided for actions of depth at least one ([local rules of change](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)). For counts, scalar volume counts that obey Einstein locality but whose two-point function is neither real nor real on separated light-ray intervals are undecided ([integer counts](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/)), and so is the four-dimensional mean operator of a signed combination of a Poisson process and a Haar-random lattice ([mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/)).

Some statements fall outside the hypotheses of single theorems without being open questions. Strongly interacting local models, non-analytic symbols, smaller gauge groups and infinite-dimensional local spaces are outside the emergent-graviton theorems ([emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/)). A ghost removed from the physical algebra leaves the Euclidean propagator, and so the spectral dimension, unchanged, and lies outside the entropy theorem for the Lorentz-invariant vacuum ([spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/), [ghost entropy](/quantum-gravity/indefinite-metric-entropy/)). Graviton tensor structure, time-dependent generators and measures other than the flat one on momentum space lie outside the spectral-dimension theorem.

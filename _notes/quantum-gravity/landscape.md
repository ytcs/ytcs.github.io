Which of six requirements can each of twelve discrete, lattice and modified-continuum families of quantum gravity keep? The requirements are Lorentz invariance, a massless universal spin-2 graviton with locality, positivity, chiral matter, horizon thermodynamics with a dynamical law, and a computed number (section 1). Table 1 gives the answer per family. In each family at least one requirement is excluded, or available only at a cost and no family has been shown to keep all six. Most of the grid is empty: most families have been tested against one or two requirements, the Poisson sprinkling against all six; an empty cell is not a finding. String theory, loop quantum gravity, spin foams and causal dynamical triangulations are not among the families (section 2). Eight further pages, on cosmology and the dark sector, test whether structures of the gravity sector fix or constrain cosmological quantities. None derives a number; Table 3 lists them.

Poisson sprinklings, the point sets behind most rows, are the causal-set model of Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin [[BLMS]](#ref-blms); the causal-set d'Alembertian and action are due to Benincasa and Dowker [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd). Table 2 includes two results. Among weights that depend only on the type of a horizon pair (the number of other points in the future of its lower point, below the slice), with finitely many types or an exponentially decaying kernel, none is non-negative and gives an area law for the expected count at linear order in curvature in three to twelve dimensions. Haar-random lattices are Poincaré-invariant and not Poisson.

## 1. The six requirements

*Lorentz invariance* means that the substrate selects no frame and that gravitons, photons and matter share one limiting speed.

*A graviton and locality* means a massless spin-2 graviton that couples with equal strength to the energy and momentum of every species, with an action that is local or whose non-locality is controlled. The column also takes the accuracy of a wave operator on the substrate against the continuum d'Alembertian, and the compatibility of finite-dimensional local algebras with positive-energy translations.

*Positivity* means non-negative weights in sums over configurations and, for an entropy built from horizon pairs, a non-negative amount per pair; positive states, so that probabilities are non-negative and the energy is bounded below; and a propagator that is a non-negative superposition of free propagators (a positive spectral weight, which excludes ghosts).

*Chiral matter* means one-handed fermions with one species per field and no doublers, the extra species that a naive lattice discretisation produces.

*Horizon thermodynamics and a dynamical law* means an entropy proportional to the horizon area, a temperature, and a law of change: an action, a sum over histories, a rule that selects a manifold-like geometry, or a rule that completes field values on a fixed sprinkling. The column also takes the boundary terms that carry the area term and the entropy of the vacuum on a region of a field theory. The pages test the area dependence of horizon counts, boundary terms and rules of change, not a temperature.

*A computed number* means Newton's constant in units of the discreteness length, or a quantity of cosmology or the dark sector, obtained and not fitted.

The eight cosmology and dark-sector pages test whether structures of the gravity sector, such as a global constraint on the vacuum energy or the coupling of a modulus, fix or constrain a cosmological quantity. Table 3 says which constrain and which leave a quantity undetermined.

## 2. The families

A *Poisson sprinkling* is a random set of points of density $$\ell^{-d}$$ in $$d$$-dimensional spacetime, with $$\ell$$ the discreteness length; the causal order of the manifold makes it a causal set. One *realisation* is one sampled point set. The *interval count* of a pair $$x\prec y$$ is the number of sprinkled points between them. The causal-set d'Alembertian at $$x$$ is a sum over the causal past of $$x$$ in which a point $$y$$ has a weight $$w_m$$ that depends on the interval count $$m$$ of the pair [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd). Its mean over the sprinkling, the *mean-field* operator, is Lorentz-invariant and is described by a *kernel*, the weights as a function of the proper time of the interval.

A *horizon count* refers to a region $$P$$ below a spacelike slice, whose boundary is a horizon. It adds, over every pair $$x\prec y$$ with $$x$$ in $$P$$ and $$y$$ outside $$P$$ below the slice, a weight $$w_m$$ that depends on the number $$m$$ of other elements in the future of $$x$$ below the slice, the type of the pair ([horizon counts](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)). A *completion rule* predicts the value of a field at a sprinkled element from its values at finitely many other elements ([local rules of change](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)). In a sum over orders, almost all finite partial orders have three layers (levels) of sizes close to $$n/4$$, $$n/2$$ and $$n/4$$, the Kleitman–Rothschild orders, so the question is whether the action suppresses them.

*Quantum counts* ask whether the number operators of a quantum field in a Poincaré-invariant state or a positive-energy vacuum can reproduce a sprinkling. *Mixed-Poisson and signed sprinklings* let the density be random, or the weights of configurations signed or complex.

*Invariant random lattices* are placed at random so that their *law*, the probability distribution of the point set, is Poincaré-invariant although no single realisation is: Haar-random lattices, and the orbit lattice of the integral quadratic form $$Q_7=7x_0^2-x_1^2-x_2^2-x_3^2$$, whose *shells* are the sets of lattice vectors with equal $$Q_7$$. A translation-invariant operator acts on plane waves by a multiplier, its *symbol*. A *lattice with an exact discrete Lorentz group* has exact translations and a Zariski-dense subgroup of the Lorentz group acting on it (contained in no proper subgroup cut out by polynomial equations), such as the integer Lorentz group. *Invariant random sets of null lines or hyperplanes* have Poincaré-invariant law.

*Lattice gravitons* are the gravitons of Hamiltonian lattice theories. A *preferred frame* or a compact residual symmetry group is what is left when a substrate selects a frame. *Local qubit and rotor models* have emergent linearised diffeomorphisms. Three continuum families complete the list: propagators with positive spectral weight, generalised free fields with a ghost (a negative spectral weight), and induced gravity, in which Newton's constant is generated by the fluctuations of matter fields.

The thirteenth group is not a family. Its eight pages treat linear constraints on the vacuum energy, the sign of vacuum-matter energy exchange, structures that leave the cosmological constant undetermined, an apparent negative neutrino mass in baryon-acoustic-oscillation (BAO) distance data, the coupling of a dark-dimension modulus to baryons, an instability in Freezing Gravity (a dark-energy model with a freely prescribed expansion history) when the dark-energy equation of state is below $$-1$$, the low multipoles of holographic cosmology, and a positivity bound on non-Gaussianity (section 6).

## 5. How the results depend on each other

Several results rest on the non-compactness of the Lorentz group. No Lorentz-invariant probability measure exists on the hyperboloid of future unit timelike vectors, so a frame can be neither averaged away nor read off a Lorentz-invariant point set ([frame leakage](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/), Proposition 6 and Corollary 7). Invariant locally finite measures also fail to exist on the spaces of null lines and null hyperplanes, which empties invariant random sets of such lines (Theorem 4 of [Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/)). Haar-random lattices escape this because the space of unimodular lattices (of unit covolume) carries a finite $$SL(d,\mathbb R)$$-invariant measure (Theorem 1 there).

The Zariski density of an arithmetic Lorentz group acts on lattices. Exact lattice translations together with such a group make the orbit of every non-torsion momentum dense in the Brillouin torus (lattice momenta modulo $$2\pi$$), which leaves flat bands ([arithmetic Lorentz group](/quantum-gravity/discrete-lorentz-group/), Corollary 1 and Theorem 1). Its Proposition 1 says that a bounded invariant function on finitely many shells has no dispersion on any open set; [kernel-scale kinematics](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/), Proposition 4, applies it to the stabiliser of the orbit lattice (the Lorentz transformations preserving it).

A propagator with positive Stieltjes weight has spectral dimension at least the dimension of space ([spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/)). The mean-field d'Alembertian in four dimensions has no such weight ([causal-set d'Alembertian](/quantum-gravity/causal-box-spectrum/), which cites that result), and [mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/) extends the statement to matrix-valued kernels that depend only on the interval (Theorem 17). For horizon counts, an area-law response needs weights of both signs ([horizon counts](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)), which [horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/), [dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/) and [boundary terms](/quantum-gravity/boundary-terms/) cite.

The d'Alembertian and horizon-count results share a threshold in the tail exponent of the kernel. The Mellin transform of a kernel (its integral against a power of the interval volume or proper time) fixes both the low-momentum symbol of the d'Alembertian and the gradient expansion of the horizon count, and exactness to all orders means zeros of that transform. A d'Alembertian kernel bounded by $$e^{-\lambda\tau^{\beta}}$$ in proper time $$\tau$$, with symbol equal to the continuum one to all orders, needs $$\beta\le1/2$$, and such kernels exist for every $$\beta<1/2$$ (Theorems 6 and 7 of the d'Alembertian page). Under the gradient-order condition of the horizon-count kernel page, a kernel integrable near zero with the area law at every gradient order cannot decay like $$e^{-c\sqrt x}$$ or faster in the linear size $$x$$, and kernels with tails $$e^{-cx^{\kappa}}$$ exist for every $$\kappa<1/2$$ (Theorems 1 and 4 there). The exponent 1/2 itself is excluded in the second case and open in the first.

[Conserved lattice momentum](/quantum-gravity/lattice-diffeomorphisms/) is cited by [universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/), and both by [emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/); [quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/) is cited by [integer counts](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/). The matter pages share one question, whether a frame is needed: the order-only page identifies a global spinor space with a frame, the chirality and reflection-positivity pages study operators with and without one, and the frame-leakage page, which all three cite, computes its cost. Five of the dark-sector pages are connected by links among them: [linear constraints](/quantum-gravity/linear-lambda-constraints/), [vacuum-matter sign](/quantum-gravity/vacuum-matter-sign/), [negative neutrino mass in BAO data](/quantum-gravity/neutrino-mimic/), [Freezing Gravity](/quantum-gravity/matter-induced-instability/) and [the undetermined cosmological constant](/quantum-gravity/dark-sector-moduli/), the last of which also cites the dimensionless-constants page. ## 7. Open questions

- Lattice gravitons: whether a lattice amplitude exists whose soft factor is gauge invariant up to high order in the product of energy and lattice spacing ([universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/)).
- Chiral matter on a Poisson sprinkling: operators that complete a retarded block to be Hermitian for the chirality operator, and Wilson terms without chiral symmetry, lie outside the theorems; reflection positivity of the disorder average of the unsymmetrised ensemble is open ([chirality obstructions](/quantum-gravity/frame-free-chirality/), [Wilson terms and reflection positivity](/quantum-gravity/frames-and-reflection-positivity/)).
- The d'Alembertian: whether a kernel with a non-locality scale much larger than the discreteness scale reduces the single-realisation fluctuation, and how the bound depends on that scale ([kernel-scale kinematics](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/)); which weights realise a heavy-tailed kernel ([horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/)).
- Laws of change: those that read common predecessors, interval contents or links are not covered, and suppression of three-layer orders at Kleitman–Rothschild proportions is undecided for actions of depth at least one ([local rules of change](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)).
- Counts: scalar volume counts that obey Einstein locality and whose two-point function is neither real nor real on separated light-ray intervals ([integer counts](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/)).

Which combinations of six requirements can a discrete, lattice-based or modified-continuum theory of quantum gravity satisfy? The requirements are Lorentz invariance, a massless universal spin-2 graviton with locality, positivity of weights and states, chiral matter, horizon thermodynamics with a dynamical law, and a computed number (section 1). Within each of the twelve families examined here, at least one requirement is excluded under hypotheses listed on the page that proves it, or is available only at a cost named there, and no family has been shown to satisfy all six. Each exclusion is narrow, and several routes have not been tested. The families are described in section 2; string theory, loop quantum gravity, spin foams and causal dynamical triangulations are not among them.

The Poisson rows come from the causal-set programme of Bombelli, Lee, Meyer and Sorkin [[BLMS]](#ref-blms) and the causal-set action of Benincasa and Dowker [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd); the speed bound from GW170817 [[GW170817]](#ref-gw170817) sets the observational scale for the first requirement. What the wiki adds are the proofs and computations on the 30 pages indexed in Table 2.

## 1. The six requirements and where each is tested

Lorentz invariance means that gravitons, photons and matter share one limiting speed and the substrate selects no frame. Gravitational waves and gamma rays from GW170817 agree on the speed within a few parts in $$10^{15}$$ [[GW170817]](#ref-gw170817); that bound compares the graviton with the photon only. The second requirement is a massless spin-2 graviton that couples universally to energy and momentum, together with locality, meaning that the action is local or its non-locality is controlled; the column also takes results on whether a wave operator defined on the substrate reproduces the continuum d'Alembertian, and with what fluctuation. The lattice, local-model, kernel and count-algebra pages test it. Positivity means non-negative weights in sums over configurations and positive states, so that probabilities are non-negative and the energy is bounded below. Chiral matter means one-handed fermions with one species per field and no doublers. Horizon thermodynamics and a dynamical law means an entropy proportional to the horizon area, a temperature, and a law of change (an action, a rule or a sum over histories) that selects a manifold-like geometry; the pages test the area dependence of horizon counts, the boundary terms of the action and local rules of change, and none tests a temperature. The last requirement is a computed number, Newton's constant in units of the discreteness scale or a dark-sector quantity, obtained and not fitted.

These six are the columns of Table 1. The list is a modelling decision: it follows what a calculation can test in each family.

## 2. The families

A *Poisson sprinkling* of a manifold is a random set of points of density $$\ell^{-d}$$ in $$d$$-dimensional spacetime, where $$\ell$$ is the discreteness length. The causal order of the manifold is kept, and the resulting partial order is a causal set. Table 1 splits this family by what is built on it. The causal-set d'Alembertian is a sum over the causal past of a point with weights that depend on the number of sprinkled points between the two [[Benincasa–Dowker]](#ref-bd). A horizon count weights pairs of elements that straddle a horizon by their type, the number of further elements in the future of the lower one below a spacelike slice ([horizon counts and the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)). The causal-set action sums such interval counts, and rules of change complete or revise an order ([local rules of change on a sprinkling](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)). Fermion fields live on the points or, for Kähler–Dirac fields, on chains of the order ([fermions that see only the causal order](/quantum-gravity/order-only-fermions/), [chirality obstructions for frame-free Weyl operators](/quantum-gravity/frame-free-chirality/), [frames, Wilson terms and reflection positivity](/quantum-gravity/frames-and-reflection-positivity/)).

*Quantum counts* ask whether number operators of a quantum field in a Poincaré-invariant state, or of any positive-energy vacuum, can reproduce a sprinkling, and whether finite-dimensional local algebras are compatible with positive-energy translations ([quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/), [integer counts in a positive-energy vacuum](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/)). *Mixed-Poisson and signed sprinklings* let the density be random or the weights of configurations be signed or complex ([mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/)).

*Invariant random lattices* are lattices placed at random so that their law is Poincaré-invariant: Haar-random lattices, and the orbit lattice of the integral quadratic form $$Q_7=7x_0^2-x_1^2-x_2^2-x_3^2$$ of signature $$(1,3)$$ ([Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/), [kernel-scale kinematics of a single realisation](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/)). A *lattice with an exact discrete Lorentz group* has exact translations and a Zariski-dense subgroup of the Lorentz group acting on it, such as the integer Lorentz group ([lattices with an arithmetic Lorentz group](/quantum-gravity/discrete-lorentz-group/)). *Invariant random sets of null lines or null hyperplanes* are random sets of lines or hyperplanes whose law is Poincaré-invariant.

*Lattice gravitons* are the gravitons of Hamiltonian lattice theories with lattice translation symmetry ([a conserved lattice momentum](/quantum-gravity/lattice-diffeomorphisms/), [exact universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/)). A *preferred frame* or a compact residual symmetry group is what is left when a substrate selects a frame ([frame leakage](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/)). *Local qubit and rotor models* have emergent linearised diffeomorphisms ([dispersion of emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/)). Three continuum families complete the list: propagators with positive spectral weight ([positive spectral weight and spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/)), generalised free fields with a ghost ([entropy in indefinite-metric theories](/quantum-gravity/indefinite-metric-entropy/)) and induced gravity ([the induced Newton constant](/quantum-gravity/induced-gravity-number/)).

## 5. How the results depend on each other

The Lorentz group is not compact, and several results rest on that. No Lorentz-invariant probability measure exists on the hyperboloid of future unit timelike vectors, so a frame can be neither averaged away nor read off a Lorentz-invariant point set ([frame leakage](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/), Proposition 6 and Corollary 7). Invariant Radon measures also fail to exist on the spaces of null lines and null hyperplanes, which empties invariant random sets of such lines (Theorem 4 of [Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/)); a separate argument shows that a neighbour rule that sees frames only modulo an arithmetic lattice selects a finite non-empty set with probability zero (Theorem 6 there). It does not remove everything: the space of unimodular lattices carries a finite measure invariant under $$SL(d,\mathbb R)$$, and so under the Lorentz group, so Haar-random lattices exist, are Poincaré-invariant in law and are not Poisson (Theorem 1 there; [quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/), Proposition 9). The exact lattice translations of a Zariski-dense discrete Lorentz group make orbits of non-torsion momenta dense in the Brillouin torus, which leaves flat bands ([lattices with an arithmetic Lorentz group](/quantum-gravity/discrete-lorentz-group/), Corollary 1 and Theorem 1). The same density gives the orbit lattice, whose stabiliser is Zariski dense, no symbol in a single realisation ([kernel-scale kinematics of a single realisation](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/), Proposition 4, which uses Proposition 1 there).

Positivity enters in two forms that the pages treat separately. A propagator with positive Stieltjes weight has spectral dimension at least the dimension of space ([positive spectral weight and spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/)). The page on the [causal-set d'Alembertian](/quantum-gravity/causal-box-spectrum/) cites it and shows that the mean-field operator in four dimensions has no positive spectral representation; [mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/) extends the statement to interval-local matrix kernels (Theorem 17) and asks what is lost when the process, not the operator, is allowed to be signed. For horizon counts, [horizon counts and the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/) finds that an area-law response needs weights of both signs; [heavy-tailed horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/) cites it and asks for exactness at every gradient order, and [causal-set counts and dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/) and [boundary and corner terms](/quantum-gravity/boundary-terms/) cite it. The d'Alembertian and horizon-count pages all work with the Mellin transform of the layer weights; the pages do not derive one group of results from the other.

Three results concern a conserved momentum or a positive-energy vacuum. On a lattice, [a conserved lattice momentum](/quantum-gravity/lattice-diffeomorphisms/) excludes an analytic conserved momentum together with low-energy graviton scattering, and [exact universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/), which cites it, excludes an exactly cone-shaped band for real-analytic periodic symbols; [dispersion of emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/) cites both. Proposition 5 of the arithmetic Lorentz group page points the same way for Lorentz transformations: no three-point vertex that conserves lattice momentum modulo $$2\pi$$ is covariant under the induced action of a non-monomial transformation. For counts, [quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/) shows that invariant positive states have mixed-Poisson counts, and [integer counts in a positive-energy vacuum](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/), which cites it, shows that integer counts of non-zero density cannot sit in a positive-energy vacuum when their two-point function is real (Corollary 2 for translation-invariant classical point processes). The application of the count result to condensate states is a sketch there.

The remaining pages form clusters. The matter pages share one question, whether a frame is needed: the order-only page identifies a global spinor space with a global frame, the chirality and reflection-positivity pages study operators with and without one, and the frame-leakage page, which they cite, computes the cost of a frame in a regulator. Six of the dark-sector pages are connected by citations: [linear constraints on the cosmological constant](/quantum-gravity/linear-lambda-constraints/), [the sign of vacuum-matter energy exchange](/quantum-gravity/vacuum-matter-sign/), [negative neutrino mass in BAO data](/quantum-gravity/neutrino-mimic/), [matter-induced instability in Freezing Gravity](/quantum-gravity/matter-induced-instability/), [structures that leave the cosmological constant undetermined](/quantum-gravity/dark-sector-moduli/) and [dark-dimension modulus coupling](/quantum-gravity/dark-dimension-coupling/). The structures page also cites the dimensionless-constants page, which ties them to the causal-set pages. [Low multipoles of holographic cosmology](/quantum-gravity/holographic-cosmology/) and [the positivity wedge](/quantum-gravity/positivity-wedge/) cite no other page.

## 7. What the map does not say, and what is open

Some readings of the tables go beyond the pages, and the pages give the replacement. Haar-random lattices are Poincaré-invariant point processes of finite density that are not Poisson ([Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/)); the mixed-Poisson form is proved for Poincaré-invariant, locally normal bosonic states of finite mean density ([quantum sprinklings](/quantum-gravity/quantum-sprinklings/)). Two lattice-graviton constructions are excluded, a conserved lattice momentum with an analytic symbol and an exactly universal soft coupling for finite-dimensional real-analytic periodic symbols; for the second, the algebraic condition holds only up to a defect of relative order $$(Ea)^{r}$$, with $$E$$ the energy, $$a$$ the lattice spacing and $$r+2$$ the lowest degree at which the squared band frequency differs from $$\lvert k\rvert^2$$, and whether an amplitude with that property exists is open ([exact universal graviton coupling](/quantum-gravity/spin-2-soft-locking/)). On horizons, the entropy unit enters as a free factor beside the weight normalisation ([causal-set counts and dimensionless constants](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/)), no page tests a temperature, and the sign of the weighted horizon count on one sprinkling is not fixed by its mean ([horizon counts and the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)). A random lattice can carry a hand, a parity-odd law of the embedded point set, which a Poisson process cannot; no page builds a fermion operator on that lattice, so it is not an entry of the chiral-matter column ([Lorentz-invariant point sets](/quantum-gravity/invariant-point-sets/), Propositions 13 and 14).

Directions that the hypotheses leave open are as follows. For chiral matter on a Poisson sprinkling, the chirality pages leave γ₅-Hermitian completions of a retarded block and chirally asymmetric Wilson terms outside their theorems, and whether the disorder average of reflection positivity holds for the unsymmetrised ensemble is open ([chirality obstructions for frame-free Weyl operators](/quantum-gravity/frame-free-chirality/), [frames, Wilson terms and reflection positivity](/quantum-gravity/frames-and-reflection-positivity/)). For the d'Alembertian, a kernel with a non-locality scale much larger than the discreteness scale may reduce the single-realisation fluctuation; the dependence of the bound on that scale is open ([kernel-scale kinematics of a single realisation](/quantum-gravity/per-realisation-kinematics/)), and no weights are known that realise a heavy-tailed kernel ([heavy-tailed horizon-count kernels](/quantum-gravity/nonlocal-weights/)). For laws of change, those that read common predecessors, interval contents or links are not covered, and suppression of three-layer orders at Kleitman–Rothschild proportions is undecided for actions of depth at least one ([local rules of change on a sprinkling](/quantum-gravity/no-local-dynamics/)). For counts, scalar volume counts that obey Einstein locality but whose two-point function is neither real nor real on separated light-ray intervals are undecided ([integer counts in a positive-energy vacuum](/quantum-gravity/count-algebras/)), and so is the mean operator of a signed combination of a Poisson process and a lattice ([mixed-Poisson and signed substrates](/quantum-gravity/signed-substrates/)).

Further directions lie outside the hypotheses of single pages. Strongly interacting local models, non-analytic symbols, smaller gauge groups and infinite-dimensional local spaces are outside the emergent-graviton theorems ([dispersion of emergent gravitons](/quantum-gravity/emergent-spin-2/)). A ghost removed from the physical algebra, as in the fakeon prescription, leaves the Euclidean propagator and so the spectral dimension unchanged, and it lies outside the entropy theorem for the Lorentz-invariant vacuum ([positive spectral weight and spectral dimension](/quantum-gravity/positivity-dimension-lemma/), [entropy in indefinite-metric theories](/quantum-gravity/indefinite-metric-entropy/)). Graviton tensor structure, time-dependent generators and other measures lie outside the spectral-dimension theorem.

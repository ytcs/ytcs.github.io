Two numbers of the dark sector have resisted derivation: the vacuum energy density $$\rho_\Lambda=\Lambda/8\pi G$$ of the cosmological constant, about $$(2.24\ {\rm meV})^4$$, and the ratio $$\Omega_c/\Omega_b=5.36$$ of the dark-matter to baryon densities (both from the Planck 2018 parameters [[Planck VI]](#ref-planck6), section 5 and the proof of Theorem 1). We ask whether the structures in which one could hope to derive them fix a value, and find that in each case examined they do not. The common reason is a transformation under which the defining conditions of the structure are invariant and which moves the number sought. The conditions then fix at most the number up to that transformation: a ratio, a geometric progression, a sign, or a coset of renormalisation constants. This is stated as an orbit lemma (Lemma 1), which is elementary. The content lies in the verification of the invariance in specific structures. We prove it for the log-coefficients of causal-set counts (Proposition 1); for the vacuum energy of a massive field on a flat Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) background in an adiabatic state, where the renormalised energy density has no term of the right shape and the cosmological constant is a coordinate on the space of counterterms (Theorem 1); for large-gauge and horizon-level quantisation in loop quantum gravity (Propositions 3 and 4); for a group-field-theory condensate (Proposition 5); and for clock Hamiltonians, where the induced vacuum energy is non-positive for passive clock states (Proposition 6). For the density ratio, the dark-matter mass is an independent modulus in every construction that relates the number densities only (Proposition 7), and a construction that fixes the mass from gauge couplings is limited by the precision of ultraviolet thresholds (Proposition 8). Section 6 lists the routes that these statements do not exclude.

The renormalisation ambiguity of the stress tensor of a quantum field on a curved background is due to Wald [[Wald]](#ref-wald) and was put on the footing used below by Hollands and Wald [[HW1]](#ref-hw1), [[HW2]](#ref-hw2). Adiabatic regularisation is due to Parker and Fulling [[PF]](#ref-pf), its decoupling properties are discussed by Ferreiro and Navarro-Salas [[FNS]](#ref-fns), and the exact energy density of a massive field in de Sitter space is in Bunch and Davies [[BD]](#ref-bd). The stochastic spectator field is from Starobinsky and Yokoyama [[SY]](#ref-sy). For the Kodama state and its level we follow Randono [[Randono]](#ref-randono) and Witten [[Witten]](#ref-witten); horizon state counting with a finite level is in Engle, Noui, Perez and Pranzetti [[ENPP]](#ref-enpp). The Page–Wootters construction is in [[PW83]](#ref-pw83). The group-field-theory background is from Gielen and Polaczek [[GP]](#ref-gp), de Cesare, Pithis and Sakellariadou [[dCPS]](#ref-dcps) and Granata and Oriti [[GO]](#ref-go). For the density ratio, Bai and Schwaller [[BS]](#ref-bs) and Ritter and Volkas [[RV]](#ref-rv) relate the dark and visible confinement scales through infrared fixed points, and Farrar [[Farrar]](#ref-farrar) derives $$\Omega_{\rm DM}/\Omega_b$$ from a single confinement scale; that proposal is disputed in [[KT]](#ref-kt) and [[MS]](#ref-ms). This page adds the statements and proofs below, which cover each structure in one framework, the exponent argument that locates every fit-free route to $$\rho_\Lambda$$ in the counterterm space, and the sensitivity bound of Proposition 8.

## 1. The orbit lemma

A structure often produces conditions of the form "a functional $$F$$ vanishes", "$$F$$ is extremal" or "two functionals of the same weight are equal". Suppose a group $$G$$ acts on the space $$X$$ of data, the functional transforms as $$F(g\cdot x)=\chi(g)F(x)$$ with $$\chi(g)\ne0$$, and the number sought is a coordinate $$q:X\to\mathbb R$$ with $$q(g\cdot x)=\psi(g)\,q(x)$$.

<div class="lemma" markdown="1">
**Lemma 1** (orbit lemma). Let $$S\subset X$$ be the zero set of $$F$$, or the set of critical points of $$F$$ (for differentiable $$F$$), or the set of extrema of $$F$$ (when $$\chi>0$$), or the set where two functionals $$F_1,F_2$$ with the same $$\chi$$ satisfy $$F_1=cF_2$$. Then $$S$$ is invariant under $$G$$, and the set of values $$q(S)\subset\mathbb R$$ is invariant under multiplication by $$\psi(G)$$. In particular, if $$\psi(G)\supseteq\{e^t:t\in\mathbb R\}$$ and $$S\cap\{q>0\}\ne\emptyset$$, then $$q(S)\supseteq(0,\infty)$$; if $$\psi(G)=\{\lambda^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$$, then $$q(S)$$ is a union of geometric progressions of ratio $$\lambda$$; if $$\psi(G)=\{\pm1\}$$, then $$q(S)$$ is symmetric under a change of sign.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* If $$F(x)=0$$ then $$F(g\cdot x)=\chi(g)F(x)=0$$. If $$x$$ is a critical point, the chain rule gives $$dF_{g\cdot x}\circ dg_x=\chi(g)\,dF_x=0$$, and $$dg_x$$ is invertible. For extrema, $$\chi>0$$ preserves the order of values. For $$F_1=cF_2$$, $$F_1(gx)=\chi F_1(x)=c\chi F_2(x)=cF_2(gx)$$. Hence $$x\in S$$ implies $$g\cdot x\in S$$, and $$q(g\cdot x)=\psi(g)q(x)$$ shows $$\psi(g)q(x)\in q(S)$$. $$\square$$
</div>

The lemma is a statement about invariant sets and is not deep. It becomes informative when a structure is shown to have such a group, because it then removes every condition built from $$F$$ alone. It says nothing about structures whose admissible data are discrete and rigid, such as a data set selected by an extremal property among a discrete family that no symmetry of the problem moves; for those, there is no orbit to speak of. It also does not apply to conditions that are not built from a covariant functional, such as integrality conditions $$a x+b\in\mathbb Z$$ with fixed $$b$$, which Proposition 3 treats separately.

## 2. Counting statistics that cannot see the discreteness scale

In a causal set, a Poisson sprinkling of density $$\rho$$ into a Lorentzian region $$D$$ gives a random point set with the induced causal order. Functionals of the order, such as the number of pairs of points related by a given number of intermediate points, have expectation values $$C(\rho,g,D)$$. The discreteness length is $$\ell\propto\rho^{-1/4}$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 1** (homothety). For a constant $$\lambda>0$$, $$C(\rho,\lambda^2g,D)=C(\lambda^4\rho,g,D)$$ for every functional of the causal order. Suppose $$C$$ has an expansion $$C=\sum_ka_k(g)\rho^{k/4}+c(g)\ln\rho+d(g)+o(1)$$ as $$\rho\to\infty$$. Then $$a_k(\lambda^2g)=\lambda^ka_k(g)$$, $$c(\lambda^2g)=c(g)$$ and $$d(\lambda^2g)=d(g)+4c(g)\ln\lambda$$. In particular, the coefficient $$c$$ of $$\ln\rho$$ depends on $$(g,D)$$ only through the dimensionless shape of $$D$$; for a diamond of proper height $$\tau$$ in de Sitter space of Hubble rate $$H$$ it is a function of $$H\tau$$ alone.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* A constant rescaling of the metric does not change the causal relations, and it multiplies the volume measure by $$\lambda^4$$. The Poisson process with intensity $$\rho\,dV_{\lambda^2g}=\rho\lambda^4dV_g$$ is the process of density $$\lambda^4\rho$$ for $$g$$, so the law of the sprinkled causal set is the same and (the first claim) follows. Insert the expansion on both sides, using $$\ln(\lambda^4\rho)=\ln\rho+4\ln\lambda$$, and compare coefficients of $$\rho^{k/4}$$ and $$\ln\rho$$ (an asymptotic expansion in this scale is unique). The shape statement follows since $$\lambda$$ is arbitrary. $$\square$$
</div>

A condition that equates two logarithmic coefficients, for instance one computed from the action of a causal diamond and one from a count of horizon molecules, is therefore an equation $$F(H\tau)=0$$ for the dimensionless shape of the diamond. It contains neither $$\rho$$ nor the Planck length, so it cannot fix $$\Lambda\ell^2$$. If the height is tied to the horizon, $$\tau=k/H$$, the condition reads $$F(k)=0$$, which holds for every $$\Lambda$$ or for none. If it is tied to the discreteness scale, $$\tau=n\ell$$, a solution has $$\Lambda\ell^2=O(n^{-2})$$, which is of order one for $$n$$ of order one and cannot be $$10^{-122}$$ unless $$n\sim10^{61}$$. The scale of the cosmological constant would have to be supplied by a second relation involving $$\ell$$ explicitly. Other results on what causal-set kinematics can and cannot fix are collected on the page on [pure numbers from causal-set kinematics](/quantum-gravity/no-number-from-kinematics/).

## 3. The cosmological constant as a coordinate on the counterterm space

Consider a free scalar field of mass $$m$$ on a spatially flat FRW background with scale factor $$a(t)$$ and Hubble rate $$H=\dot a/a$$, coupled to the curvature with coupling $$\xi$$, in a state of the adiabatic-vacuum type. Its energy density has an ultraviolet divergence, and the renormalised value $$\rho_{\rm ren}$$ is defined only up to the following freedom.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 2** (renormalisation ambiguity). Any two prescriptions for the renormalised stress tensor that obey the axioms of Wald (locality, covariance, conservation, standard normalisation) differ by a finite linear combination of local, conserved tensors built from the metric and $$m$$, of dimension at most four: $$m^4g_{ab}$$, $$m^2G_{ab}$$ and the two independent conserved tensors that are quadratic in the curvature, with coefficients that may depend on $$\ln(m/\mu)$$ [[Wald]](#ref-wald), [[HW1]](#ref-hw1), [[HW2]](#ref-hw2). Call this span the counterterm space $$\mathcal C$$. In a flat FRW background the energy-density components of these tensors are $$m^4$$, $$m^2H^2$$ and two combinations of $$H^4$$, $$H^2\dot H$$, $$\dot H^2$$ and $$H\ddot H$$, each with a coefficient that can be changed freely. A property of $$\rho_{\rm ren}$$ that is invariant under $$\rho_{\rm ren}\to\rho_{\rm ren}+c$$ for all $$c\in\mathcal C$$ is a property of the class of $$\rho_{\rm ren}$$ in the quotient $$\mathcal F/\mathcal C$$. Being "Lambda-like", that is, constant in time to within a given tolerance, is not such a property: adding $$c_0m^4$$ with $$\vert c_0\vert $$ large makes any class Lambda-like.
</div>

The last statement is immediate, since $$m^4$$ does not depend on time. It is the case of Lemma 1 in which $$G$$ is the group of translations by elements of $$\mathcal C$$ and $$q$$ is the coefficient of $$m^4$$. A statement about the value of $$\rho_\Lambda$$ in this setting must therefore be a statement about a particular choice of representative in each class.

A natural choice exists. Renormalise by adiabatic subtraction through fourth adiabatic order: the mode energy per wavenumber is expanded in the adiabatic parameter $$\epsilon\sim H/\omega_k$$ with $$\omega_k=\sqrt{k^2/a^2+m^2}$$, and the terms through $$\epsilon^4$$ are subtracted before the $$k$$-integral [[PF]](#ref-pf). The subtracted terms are local in the metric and of dimension at most four, so this is a representative in each class. Among the representatives whose counterterm coefficients are independent of $$m$$, it is the one for which $$\rho_{\rm ren}\to0$$ as $$m\to\infty$$ at fixed geometry, since a nonzero coefficient of $$m^4$$ or $$m^2H^2$$ does not vanish in that limit. We call it the decoupling section.

<div class="theorem" markdown="1">
**Theorem 1** (decoupling section). Consider a free scalar of mass $$m$$ on flat FRW, in an adiabatic vacuum of order $$N\ge6$$, renormalised by adiabatic subtraction through fourth order, and let $$H(t)\le H_{\max}\ll m$$ on the interval considered. Statement (a) holds in the sense of an expansion valid through adiabatic order $$N$$.

(a) $$\rho_{\rm ren}$$ has an asymptotic expansion $$\rho_{\rm ren}\sim\sum_{n\ge3}\rho_{2n}$$ in which $$\rho_{2n}$$ is a local polynomial in $$a^{-1}\,d^ja/dt^j$$ with $$2n$$ time derivatives in total, divided by $$m^{2n-4}$$. In particular, no term of lower derivative order than six appears, and there is no term of the form $$m^4\ln(m/H)$$.

(b) For $$\xi=1/6$$ in de Sitter space the expansion is $$\rho_{\rm ren}=-H^6/(2520\pi^2m^2)\,(1+O(H^2/m^2))$$ plus a remainder that is exponentially small in $$m/H$$.

(c) For the history of a flat $$\Lambda$$CDM universe over $$0\le z\le1100$$ and a field with $$m\ge H(z=1100)=3.4\times10^{-29}$$ eV, any contribution to the energy density of the form $$H^4f(H/m)$$ with $$\lvert f\rvert\le C$$ satisfies $$\lvert\rho\rvert\le C\,H(1100)^4=5\times10^{-104}\,C\,\rho_\Lambda$$. The sixth-order term for $$\xi=1/6$$ on this history has $$\lvert\rho_6\rvert=1.7\times10^{-134}\rho_\Lambda$$ today at $$m=H(1100)$$ and smaller values for larger $$m$$, and $$\rho_6(z=1100)/\rho_6(z=0)=8.4\times10^{25}$$, where $$\rho_\Lambda$$ is the observed value.
</div>

The idea is that a field of mass $$m\gg H$$ is adiabatic: the vacuum is the instantaneous vacuum of the local Hamiltonian, and the renormalised quantity that survives the subtraction is the error of the adiabatic approximation, which is suppressed by powers of $$H/m$$.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* (a) The mode functions of the adiabatic vacuum of order $$N$$ have the Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin (WKB) expansion to order $$N$$, so the per-mode energy $$\rho_k$$ equals the sum of its adiabatic terms $$\rho_k^{(0)}+\rho_k^{(2)}+\rho_k^{(4)}+\rho_k^{(6)}+\dots$$ up to $$O(\epsilon^{N+1})$$. The subtraction removes the terms through order four, and the order-$$2n$$ term falls as $$\omega_k^{1-2n}$$ at large $$k$$, so that its integral with measure $$k^2dk$$ diverges for $$n\le2$$ (quartically, quadratically and logarithmically for $$n=0,1,2$$) and converges for $$n\ge3$$. Hence $$\rho_{\rm ren}=\sum_{n=3}^{N/2}\int\rho_k^{(2n)}k^2dk/2\pi^2a^3+O(\epsilon^{N+1})$$, where the uniform bound $$\epsilon\le H_{\max}/m$$ controls the remainder. The $$k$$-integral of a term with $$2n$$ derivatives scales as $$m^{4-2n}$$ by dimensional analysis, and the logarithm $$\ln(m/\mu)$$ of the counterterm ideal does not occur because the subtraction is performed at the level of the integrand, with no dependence on $$\mu$$.

(b) For $$\xi=1/6$$, the exact renormalised energy density in the de Sitter vacuum is [[BD]](#ref-bd)

$$
\rho_{\rm ren}=\frac{1}{64\pi^2}\Big[m^4\big(\psi(\tfrac32+\nu)+\psi(\tfrac32-\nu)-\ln\tfrac{m^2}{H^2}\big)-\tfrac23m^2H^2+\tfrac1{15}H^4\Big],\quad \nu^2=\tfrac14-\tfrac{m^2}{H^2}, \tag{1}
$$

where $$\psi$$ is the digamma function. For $$m>H/2$$, $$\nu=iy$$ with $$y=\sqrt{m^2/H^2-1/4}$$, and the combination in the parentheses is $$2\,\mathrm{Re}\,\psi(\tfrac32+iy)-\ln(m^2/H^2)$$. The asymptotic series of the digamma function in $$1/y$$ turns the expression in the brackets into a power series in $$H^2/m^2$$ whose first three terms cancel against $$-\tfrac23m^2H^2+\tfrac1{15}H^4$$ and the logarithm, leaving $$-\tfrac{64}{2520}H^6/m^2$$ as the leading term; the cancelled terms ($$m^4$$ times a logarithm, $$m^4$$, $$m^2H^2$$ and $$H^4$$) are those removed by the adiabatic subtraction. We verified this in two ways. A symbolic WKB expansion of the mode equation $$\chi''+(k^2+m^2a^2)\chi=0$$ to sixth adiabatic order, with the $$k$$-integral done exactly, gives $$\rho_6=-H^6/(2520\pi^2m^2)$$ in de Sitter space, and its ratio to the exact value (1) is $$1.0076$$, $$1.00084$$ and $$1.000075$$ at $$m/H=10,30,100$$. And the exact formula (1) evaluated at 80-digit precision gives: the bracket times $$m^2/H^6$$ is $$-0.02559014$$ at $$m/H=10$$, $$-0.02541802$$ at $$30$$, and $$-0.0253968256$$ at $$10^4$$, against $$-64/2520=-0.0253968254$$. The difference between the exact value and the optimally truncated asymptotic series is exponentially small: the remainder is $$-5.9\times10^{-9}$$, $$2.0\times10^{-14}$$, $$1.2\times10^{-19}$$ and $$5.8\times10^{-25}$$ at $$m/H=3,5,7,9$$, compared with $$e^{-2\pi m/H}=6.5\times10^{-9}$$, $$2.3\times10^{-14}$$, $$7.9\times10^{-20}$$ and $$2.8\times10^{-25}$$ (the ratio drifts by a power-law prefactor).

(c) With $$H_0$$ and the density parameters of Planck [[Planck VI]](#ref-planck6) ($$H_0=67.36$$ km s$$^{-1}$$ Mpc$$^{-1}$$, $$\Omega_m=0.3153$$, radiation $$\Omega_r=9.2\times10^{-5}$$, $$\Omega_\Lambda=0.6846$$), $$\hbar H_0=1.437\times10^{-33}$$ eV and, with $$M_P=2.435\times10^{27}$$ eV, $$\rho_\Lambda=3\Omega_\Lambda H_0^2M_P^2=2.51\times10^{-11}\ {\rm eV}^4$$, whose fourth root is $$2.24$$ meV. Then $$\rho_\Lambda/H_0^4=5.9\times10^{120}$$ and $$H(1100)/H_0=2.36\times10^4$$ (our computation), so $$H(1100)^4=3.1\times10^{17}H_0^4=5.3\times10^{-104}\rho_\Lambda$$. Today and with $$m=H(1100)$$ the de Sitter estimate of the term (b) is $$H_0^6/(2520\pi^2m^2)=1.2\times10^{-134}\rho_\Lambda$$; the evaluation of the sixth-order term on the $$\Lambda$$CDM history by the same symbolic expansion gives the figures in (c). $$\square$$
</div>

Steps (a) and (b) are the substance. Step (a) uses two inputs from the literature on adiabatic regularisation [[PF]](#ref-pf), [[FNS]](#ref-fns): that the per-mode energy of an adiabatic vacuum equals its adiabatic expansion through order $$N$$, and that the adiabatic terms of each order are local polynomials in the scale factor and its derivatives; we cite these for general $$\xi$$ and $$a(t)$$ and verify the consequence in de Sitter space only. Step (b) is a check of (a) in the one case with a closed form. The sizes in (c) rest on the fact that a quantum field on a fixed background contains no Newton constant, so every energy density it produces is $$H^4$$ times a function of $$m/H$$, and the observed $$\rho_\Lambda$$ is $$6\times10^{120}H_0^4$$.

The same fact gives an exponent argument. Suppose that $$\rho_\Lambda=m^aH_0^{4-a}$$ for the mass $$m$$ of a known particle. Then $$a=\ln(\rho_\Lambda/H_0^4)/\ln(m/H_0)$$, which is $$4.05$$ for $$m=1$$ meV, $$3.83$$ for $$50$$ meV and $$3.13$$ for the electron mass (our computation). The local terms of the adiabatic expansion are $$m^{4-2n}H^{2n}$$, that is $$a=4-2n\in\{4,2,0,-2,\dots\}$$: only even powers of $$H$$ occur. The exponents required lie between $$3.1$$ and $$4.05$$, and the only local term near them is $$a=4$$, the cosmological counterterm $$c_0m^4$$. Its coefficient would have to be $$c_0=\rho_\Lambda/m^4$$, which is $$25$$ for $$m=1$$ meV, $$4\times10^{-6}$$ for $$50$$ meV and $$10^{-33}$$ for the electron. The closeness of $$\rho_\Lambda^{1/4}=2.24$$ meV to neutrino mass scales is, in this sense, a statement about a coefficient in $$\mathcal C$$.

Three remarks specify the scope. A term of definite degree $$d$$ in $$H$$ changes by a factor $$(2.36\times10^4)^d$$ over $$0\le z\le1100$$, and an equation of state within $$\vert 1+w\vert <10^{-2}$$ over that range allows a change by a factor of at most $$1101^{0.03}=1.23$$, which means $$d<0.021$$ (our computation); the sixth-order term changes by the factor $$8.4\times10^{25}$$ quoted in (c). The statement concerns a free field in the adiabatic vacuum; excitations of the field are particles and are not part of it. For interacting fields the local ambiguity is as in Proposition 2 with coefficients that depend on the couplings, and the decoupling statement is a consequence of the cited results, not proved here. Non-local terms at second order in a perturbation of the mass: the zero-momentum retarded response of $$\langle\phi^2\rangle$$ to a time-dependent mass shift has the kernel $$I(T)=\int_1^\infty\sqrt{x^2-1}\,x^{-1}\sin(2xT)\,dx$$ in units of $$m$$, whose large-$$T$$ behaviour comes from the branch point at $$x=1$$ and is $$(\sqrt\pi/4)\,T^{-3/2}\cos(2T+\text{phase})$$; the tail oscillates at frequency $$2m$$ with zero mean, and a feature of width $$w$$ in the history excites it with weight $$2\pi mw/\sinh(\pi mw)$$, which is exponentially small for cosmological $$w\gtrsim1/H$$. This kernel was not rederived here beyond the asymptotics.

### Light fields during inflation

Theorem 1 requires $$m>H$$ over the interval. A field lighter than the Hubble rate at the epoch of inflation is not covered. The stationary distribution of a light spectator field $$\chi$$ with potential $$V$$ in de Sitter space of rate $$H_I$$ is $$P(\chi)\propto\exp[-8\pi^2V(\chi)/3H_I^4]$$ [[SY]](#ref-sy); for $$V=\tfrac12m^2\chi^2$$ the mean energy density is $$\langle V\rangle=\tfrac12m^2\langle\chi^2\rangle=3H_I^4/16\pi^2$$, independent of $$m$$. Setting this equal to $$\rho_\Lambda$$ requires $$H_I=(16\pi^2\rho_\Lambda/3)^{1/4}=6.03$$ meV (our computation). The variance of $$\chi$$ approaches its stationary value as $$1-\exp(-2m^2N/3H_I^2)$$ after $$N$$ e-folds, so equilibrium needs $$N\gtrsim3H_I^2/2m^2$$, which for $$m\lesssim H_0$$ is at least $$2.6\times10^{61}$$ e-folds; the de Sitter entropy at that rate is $$8\pi^2M_P^2/H_I^2=1.3\times10^{61}$$, so $$N$$ would exceed the entropy by a factor $$2.05$$. A bound $$N\lesssim S_{\rm dS}$$ on the duration of inflation, if imposed, leaves a fraction $$1-e^{-0.49}=0.39$$ of the stationary variance (for $$m=H_0$$). In either case the value of $$\rho_\Lambda$$ is traded for the value of $$H_I$$, and the actual field value in our patch is a random draw from the distribution, so the exchange is one input for one output. Mass-dependent details (a thawing equation of state of a quadratic field with $$m\approx H_0$$) were not computed here.

## 4. Quantisation and counting structures that give a label

### The Kodama state

The Kodama state of gravity with cosmological constant $$\Lambda$$ is a wave functional of the self-dual connection $$A_+=\Gamma+iK$$ (the spin connection and extrinsic curvature) on a closed three-manifold $$\Sigma$$. In its generalisation to a real Immirzi parameter $$\gamma$$ [[Randono]](#ref-randono) it is the exponential of $$i$$ times the Chern–Simons functional with coefficients proportional to $$1/\Lambda$$ for the Euler (imaginary-part) and $$1/\gamma\Lambda$$ for the Pontryagin (real-part) combination. Under a large gauge transformation $$g:\Sigma\to SL(2,\mathbb C)$$ the Chern–Simons functional shifts by the Wess–Zumino term.

<div class="lemma" markdown="1">
**Lemma 2** (real shift). For $$g:\Sigma\to SL(2,\mathbb C)$$ on a closed oriented three-manifold, $$CS(A^g)-CS(A)$$ is a real constant times the degree of $$g$$ in $$\pi_3(SL(2,\mathbb C))=\mathbb Z$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The shift is a multiple of $$\int_\Sigma g^*\mathrm{tr}(\theta^3)$$ with $$\theta=g^{-1}dg$$, since the remaining terms are exact. The form $$\mathrm{tr}(\theta^3)$$ is closed on $$SL(2,\mathbb C)$$, so the integral depends only on the homotopy class of $$g$$; $$SL(2,\mathbb C)$$ deformation retracts onto $$SU(2)$$, and a representative of degree $$n$$ is an $$SU(2)$$-valued map. For $$\theta=\theta^aT_a$$ with $$T_a=-i\sigma_a/2$$, $$\mathrm{tr}(T_aT_bT_c)=-\epsilon_{abc}/4$$ is real, so the integral is real, proportional to $$n$$. $$\square$$
</div>

The consequence is that the Euler part, the one with the "pure $$\Lambda$$" coefficient, is invariant under large gauge transformations in the Lorentzian theory for every $$\Lambda$$. The cosmological constant enters the large-gauge phase through the Pontryagin coefficient only, which is proportional to $$1/\gamma\Lambda$$. For a real $$\gamma$$, the state picks up the phase $$\exp(2\pi ik_\gamma n)$$ under a transformation of degree $$n$$ with

$$
k_\gamma=\frac{3}{2\gamma\Lambda\ell_P^2}=\frac{S_{\rm dS}}{2\pi\gamma},\qquad S_{\rm dS}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}, \tag{2}
$$

which is the level of the Chern–Simons theory in [[Randono]](#ref-randono), who notes that the coupling constants can be adjusted to make $$k_\gamma$$ an integer. For the self-dual state ($$\gamma=i$$) the shift in the exponent is real, so a transformation of degree $$n$$ multiplies the modulus by $$e^{nS_{\rm dS}}$$ and the state is not invariant up to a phase for any $$\Lambda$$ [[Witten]](#ref-witten). With $$H_0=67.36$$ km s$$^{-1}$$ Mpc$$^{-1}$$ and $$\Omega_\Lambda=0.6846$$, $$\Lambda=3\Omega_\Lambda H_0^2/c^2=1.089\times10^{-52}\ {\rm m}^{-2}$$, $$\Lambda\ell_P^2=2.84\times10^{-122}$$ and $$S_{\rm dS}=3.3\times10^{122}$$, so $$k_\gamma=5.3\times10^{121}$$ for $$\gamma=1$$; consecutive integers differ in $$\Lambda$$ by the relative amount $$1/k_\gamma\approx2\times10^{-122}$$ (our computation).

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 3** (a lattice of values, not a value). Let the large-gauge and horizon conditions be of the form $$a_ix+b_i\in\mathbb Z$$, $$i=1,\dots,r$$, in the variable $$x=S_{\rm dS}$$, with constants $$a_i\ne0$$ and $$b_i$$ independent of $$\Lambda$$. The set $$X$$ of allowed $$x$$ is empty, or an arithmetic progression (when all ratios $$a_i/a_j$$ are rational), or a single point (when some ratio is irrational). In the treatments of the Kodama state that we are aware of, the conditions that involve $$\Lambda$$ are the integrality of $$k_\gamma$$ in (2) and of rational multiples of it (the level of an isolated-horizon Chern–Simons theory, the maximal spin $$j_{\max}=k_\gamma/2$$, a larger gauge group), which are of this form with $$b_i=0$$ and $$a_i\in\mathbb Q/\gamma$$, so the allowed set is a progression and does not contain a preferred element.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* Each condition defines a shifted lattice $$(\mathbb Z-b_i)/a_i$$. If $$x,x'\in X$$ then $$x-x'\in\bigcap_ia_i^{-1}\mathbb Z$$. This intersection is $$\{0\}$$ if some ratio $$a_i/a_j$$ is irrational, and a lattice $$\ell\mathbb Z$$ with $$\ell$$ the least common multiple otherwise, so $$X$$ has at most one point in the first case and is an arithmetic progression or empty in the second. The Kodama conditions are homogeneous in $$S_{\rm dS}$$ by (2), with $$b_i=0$$ and rational ratios, which is the form assumed; the list of conditions is taken from the cited treatments and was not re-derived. $$\square$$
</div>

In the one-point case, hitting a value $$x^*\sim10^{122}$$ requires fixing the offset $$b_2$$ to a relative precision of about $$10^{-123}$$: the 122 digits of $$1/\Lambda$$ are moved into an order-one parameter. This is the content of "label, not value" for this structure. The proposition does not exclude a second, non-homogeneous topological integer with an irrational ratio; by Lemma 2 it cannot come from the $$SL(2,\mathbb C)$$ part of the gauge group.

### The q-deformed horizon

If the level $$k$$ is tied to the horizon, the horizon states are counted by the Verlinde formula for $$SU(2)_k$$: $$n$$ punctures with spins $$j_1,\dots,j_n$$ carry the number of states

$$
V_k(j)=\frac2K\sum_{l=1}^{K-1}\sin^2\!\frac{\pi l}{K}\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{\sin\big((2j_i+1)\pi l/K\big)}{\sin(\pi l/K)},\qquad K=k+2, \tag{3}
$$

and the classical ($$k\to\infty$$) number of invariants is $$C(j)$$. A puncture of spin $$j$$ has area $$a_j=8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2\sqrt{j(j+1)}$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 4** (q-transparency). (i) If $$\sum_ij_i\le k$$, then $$V_k(j)=C(j)$$. (ii) If $$\sum_ij_i=k+1$$ and $$C(j)\ge1$$, then $$V_k(j)=C(j)-1$$. (iii) If the area of the horizon satisfies $$A\le8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2(k+1)$$, every configuration of punctures that fits has $$V_k=C$$. With the level of the bulk theory tied to the cosmological constant by $$k=c/(\gamma\Lambda\ell_P^2)$$ and the area of the de Sitter horizon $$A_{\rm dS}=12\pi/\Lambda$$, (iii) holds for all $$k$$ when $$c\ge3/2$$, which includes the level (2) ($$c=3/2$$) and the value $$c=3$$ that follows from matching $$a(j_{\max}=k/2)$$ to $$A_{\rm dS}$$. The entropy condition $$S_{\rm Verlinde}(A_{\rm dS})=A_{\rm dS}/4$$ is then the classical one and contains no information on $$\Lambda$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The idea is that the sum (3) is a discrete Fourier average of the character product, and averaging over $$K$$-th roots of unity keeps only the Fourier modes divisible by $$K$$. Let $$z=e^{2i\theta}$$ and $$\prod_i\chi_{j_i}=\sum_mc_mz^m$$ with $$\chi_j=\sum_{m=-j}^jz^m$$, $$c_{-m}=c_m$$ and $$c_m=0$$ for $$\vert m\vert >J\equiv\sum_ij_i$$. With $$\sin^2\theta=(2-z-z^{-1})/4$$ and $$\sum_{l=0}^{K-1}z_l^m=K\,[K\mid m]$$ for $$z_l=e^{2\pi il/K}$$ (the $$l=0$$ term vanishes), (3) becomes
$$V_k=\sum_{r\in K\mathbb Z}(c_r-c_{r+1}),$$
using the symmetry of $$c$$. The classical count is $$C=c_0-c_1$$ by the Weyl integration formula. For $$J\le k=K-2$$ only $$r=0$$ contributes, which proves (i); for $$J=K-1$$ the term $$r=-K$$ gives $$-c_{K-1}=-c_J=-1$$, which proves (ii). For (iii), $$a_j>8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2j$$, so $$\sum_ia_{j_i}\le A\le8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2(k+1)$$ forces $$\sum_ij_i<k+1$$, and a non-zero invariant has integer $$\sum_ij_i$$, so $$\sum_ij_i\le k$$. With $$A_{\rm dS}=12\pi\gamma\ell_P^2k/c$$ the condition $$A_{\rm dS}\le8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2(k+1)$$ is $$12k/c\le8(k+1)$$, which holds for $$c\ge3/2$$. $$\square$$
</div>

We checked (i) and (ii) by evaluating (3) numerically for random configurations with $$k\le8$$: 1267 configurations with $$\sum j\le k$$ had $$V_k=C$$ with no exception, and 220 with $$\sum j=k+1$$ all had $$C-V_k=1$$. The witness $$\{k/2,k/2,1\}$$ has $$C=1$$ and $$V_k=0$$ for $$k=2,4,10,20,40$$ (in each case $$\sum j=k+1$$), and its area is $$8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2(k+1+\sqrt2)$$ up to terms of order $$1/k$$, so the threshold in (iii) cannot be raised by more than $$\sqrt2$$ in the factor $$(k+1)$$. The proposition says that the horizon of the universe holds so few maximal quanta that the deformation cannot change any count, so the Verlinde condition is the classical one and leaves $$\Lambda$$ free. A value $$c<3/2$$ would open a window where the deformation is visible; the value of $$c$$ is a modelling input that we have taken from the two normalisations above.

### A group-field-theory condensate

In the mean-field treatment of a group field theory with a massless scalar clock, we take (from the cited treatments, whose equations we have not re-derived) a single mode $$\sigma=\rho\,e^{i\theta}$$ in relational time $$\phi_c$$ obeys $$\rho''=Q^2/\rho^3+E^2\rho-\sum_n\lambda_n\rho^{n-1}$$, with conserved charge $$Q=\rho^2\theta'$$, and the volume is $$V=v\rho^2$$ with $$d\phi_c/dt=Q/V$$ in units with $$\hbar=1$$, so that $$H=(Q/3V)(V'/V)$$ [[dCPS]](#ref-dcps), [[GO]](#ref-go). Substituting the conserved energy $$E_{\rm tot}=\rho'^2+Q^2/\rho^2-E^2\rho^2+\sum_n(2\lambda_n/n)\rho^n$$ gives the exact identity

$$
H^2=\frac{4Q^2}{9}\Big[\frac{E^2}{V^2}+\frac{vE_{\rm tot}}{V^3}-\frac{v^2Q^2}{V^4}-\sum_n\frac{2\lambda_n}{n}\,v^{-2}\Big(\frac{V}{v}\Big)^{(n-6)/2}\Big], \tag{4}
$$

so that each interaction term is a perfect fluid with $$\rho_n\propto V^{(n-6)/2}$$, equation of state $$w_n=(4-n)/2$$, dust for $$n=4$$ and a cosmological constant for $$n=6$$ with $$\Lambda=3H^2\to(4/9)Q^2\vert \lambda_6\vert /v^2$$. A second massless scalar $$\eta$$ can serve as a clock instead of $$\chi$$. Gielen and Polaczek [[GP]](#ref-gp) showed that in the free theory with several scalars the clock and non-clock scalars are treated differently.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 5** (clock dependence; derivation within the model). Let the same couplings $$\lambda_n$$, the same volume parameter $$v$$ and the same classical data $$(\pi_\chi,\pi_\eta)$$ be described by a condensate peaked on $$\chi$$ (frame $$\chi$$) or on $$\eta$$ (frame $$\eta$$), where the charge $$Q$$ in (4) is the momentum $$\pi_c$$ of the clock. Then the cosmological constant and the dust density of the interaction terms satisfy $$\Lambda_\eta/\Lambda_\chi=\rho_{\rm dust,\eta}/\rho_{\rm dust,\chi}=(\pi_\eta/\pi_\chi)^2$$, while the stiff term, fixed by matching to general relativity, is proportional to $$\pi_\chi^2+\pi_\eta^2$$ in both frames.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* In frame $$c$$, (4) holds with $$Q=\pi_c$$. The interaction term with $$n=6$$ gives $$\Lambda_c=(4/9)\pi_c^2\vert \lambda_6\vert /v^2$$ and the term with $$n=4$$ gives a contribution $$(2/9)\pi_c^2\vert \lambda_4\vert /(vV)$$ to $$H^2$$ (dust); both contributions are proportional to $$\pi_c^2$$ with all other factors equal. Taking the ratio of the two frames gives the stated law. The coefficient $$E^2$$ in frame $$c$$ includes the non-clock momentum; requiring that the stiff term be the general-relativistic one, $$(4\pi G/3)(\pi_\chi^2+\pi_\eta^2)/V^2$$, fixes it, as in [[GP]](#ref-gp). $$\square$$
</div>

Hence the dust and cosmological constant of the interaction terms are not functions of clock-independent data $$(V,\pi_\chi^2+\pi_\eta^2)$$; their ratio between two frames is $$(\pi_\eta/\pi_\chi)^2$$, and an invariant statement can use only ratios among the coefficients with the same power of $$\pi_c$$, such as the ratio of the dust density squared to the product of the clock-stiff density and $$\Lambda$$. The proposition depends on the matching rule that identifies the non-clock momentum, and on the use of the same interaction in both frames; both are modelling choices in the cited treatments. A frame-independent value of $$\Lambda$$ would need a rule that selects the clock, and we know of none in the model.

### Clock Hamiltonians

In a Page–Wootters construction [[PW83]](#ref-pw83) a finite-dimensional clock with Hamiltonian $$H_C(v)$$ contributes to the minisuperspace Hamiltonian constraint of a flat universe with volume variable $$v=a^3$$. In the Born–Oppenheimer regime, with $$v$$ slow, conditioning on a clock reading in the adiabatic branch $$n$$ gives that branch an induced energy density $$\rho_{{\rm ind},n}(v)=\lambda_n(v)/v$$, where $$\lambda_n$$ is the corresponding eigenvalue of $$H_C(v)$$. (This definition is the model assumption.) A term $$c\,v^{\gamma}$$ in $$\lambda_n$$ is then a fluid with $$\rho\propto v^{\gamma-1}$$, and since $$\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}=v^{-(1+w)}$$ for an equation of state $$w$$, it has $$w=-\gamma$$. Take $$H_C(v)=H_0+v^\beta V$$ with a non-degenerate $$H_0$$ having eigenvalues $$E_m$$ and $$V_{nn}=0$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 6** (sign of the induced vacuum energy). By Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation theory in $$u=v^\beta$$, $$\lambda_n=E_n+u^2\Lambda_n+O(u^3)$$ with $$\Lambda_n=\sum_{m\ne n}\vert V_{nm}\vert ^2/(E_n-E_m)$$. The term $$u^2/v=v^{2\beta-1}$$ is a cosmological constant ($$w=-1$$) iff $$\beta=1/2$$, with energy density $$\Lambda_n$$. (i) $$\Lambda_n$$ depends only on the gaps of $$H_0$$, and $$\sum_n\Lambda_n=0$$. (ii) For any population $$p_n$$ of the branches that is passive, meaning non-increasing in $$E_n$$ (every thermal population at positive temperature is of this kind), the mean induced energy is $$\sum_np_n\Lambda_n=\tfrac12\sum_{n,m}(p_n-p_m)\vert V_{nm}\vert ^2/(E_n-E_m)\le0$$, with strict inequality if some $$V_{nm}\ne0$$ links levels of different population. It is non-negative for populations that are non-decreasing in energy. In particular, a clock in its ground branch has $$\Lambda_0=-\sum_{m\ne0}\vert V_{0m}\vert ^2/(E_m-E_0)<0$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The expansion is the second-order energy shift. (i) A shift $$H_0\to H_0+c$$ changes every $$\lambda_n$$ by $$c$$, which changes only the constant (dust-like, $$w=0$$) term. The sum vanishes because the summand $$\vert V_{nm}\vert ^2/(E_n-E_m)$$ is antisymmetric under $$n\leftrightarrow m$$. (ii) Symmetrise the double sum: $$\sum_{n,m}p_n\vert V_{nm}\vert ^2/(E_n-E_m)=\tfrac12\sum_{n,m}(p_n-p_m)\vert V_{nm}\vert ^2/(E_n-E_m)$$, and each pair has $$(p_n-p_m)/(E_n-E_m)\le0$$ for a passive population. $$\square$$
</div>

Numerically, for 2000 random clocks of two to six levels with random couplings and passive populations, the mean shift was positive in none; for 500 random populations that are increasing in energy it was positive in all 500; the second-order formula agrees with the exact eigenvalues to $$2\times10^{-3}$$ (relative, at $$u=10^{-4}$$ and level separations above $$0.3$$). The statement therefore excludes a positive cosmological constant from a thermal clock in this reading. The argument is the passivity argument of Theorem 1(c) on the page on [the sign of energy exchange between vacuum and matter](/quantum-gravity/vacuum-matter-sign/): mixing levels of a passive population lowers the energy. It does not exclude a positive value from a clock that stores work, and the induced term adds to a bare $$\Lambda$$ that nothing in the construction fixes. Whether a physical clock has a coupling with $$\beta=1/2$$, for a localised clock with weight zero, was not computed.

### A multiple-point condition

The multiple-point principle of Froggatt and Nielsen [[FN]](#ref-fn) requires the effective potential of the Standard Model to have two degenerate minima, one at the electroweak scale and one at a large field value $$\phi_*$$. The condition fixes couplings; the top-quark mass is the one checked against the measured value, and the Standard Model is close to this situation [[Buttazzo]](#ref-buttazzo). A bare vacuum term $$\Lambda_b$$ enters the potential additively and drops out of every difference of values and every derivative, so degeneracy imposes no condition on it. If one asks instead that the difference of the two minima be the observed $$\rho_\Lambda$$, then with $$V=\tfrac14\lambda_{\rm eff}(\phi)\phi^4$$ at large field the effective quartic coupling at $$\phi_*$$ must equal $$4\rho_\Lambda/\phi_*^4$$. For an illustrative $$\phi_*=5\times10^{17}$$ GeV and $$\rho_\Lambda=2.5\times10^{-47}\ {\rm GeV}^4$$ this is $$1.5\times10^{-117}$$ (our computation; $$\phi_*$$ is an assumed input). The cosmological-constant problem is then the problem of fixing $$\lambda_{\rm eff}(\phi_*)$$ to this precision. We did not rerun the renormalisation-group analysis.

## 5. The ratio of dark-matter to baryon density

Write $$R=\Omega_c/\Omega_b=r\,\mu$$, where $$r=n_X/n_B$$ is the ratio of the number densities of dark-matter and baryon particles and $$\mu=m_X/m_B$$ the ratio of their masses (with $$m_B$$ the mean baryon mass). The Planck 2018 values $$\Omega_ch^2=0.1200\pm0.0012$$ and $$\Omega_bh^2=0.02237\pm0.00015$$ [[Planck VI]](#ref-planck6) give $$R=5.364\pm0.065$$, with the correlation between the two densities ignored; $$\sigma(\ln R)=0.012$$ (our computation).

Constructions that relate $$r$$ to a count, for instance by a conserved asymmetry, a symmetry or a group-theory ratio, leave $$m_X$$ as a parameter of the Lagrangian. Lemma 1 makes the consequence precise. Let $$\theta$$ denote the data of a construction and suppose its selection conditions are built from a functional $$F$$ that is invariant under the dark scaling $$D_t:m_X\mapsto e^tm_X$$ with all other data fixed.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 7** (dark-mass modulus). If $$F\circ D_t=F$$ and $$R$$ is $$C^1$$ with weight $$w=\partial_t\ln R(D_t\theta)\ne0$$ on an open set $$U$$, then no condition built from $$F$$ alone fixes $$R$$ on $$U$$: the solution set is $$D_t$$-invariant and $$\ln R$$ moves strictly monotonically along each orbit. If $$r$$ does not depend on $$m_X$$, $$w=1$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* This is Lemma 1 with $$G=\{D_t\}$$, $$\chi=1$$ and $$q=R$$, together with $$\partial_t\ln R=\partial_t\ln\mu+\partial_t\ln r=1+\partial_t\ln r$$. Along an orbit in the solution set, $$\ln R(D_t\theta)=\ln R(\theta)+\int_0^tw$$ is strictly monotone when $$w\ne0$$ on $$U$$. $$\square$$
</div>

The proposition restates, in the language of Lemma 1, that a construction in which $$m_X$$ is an independent parameter does not predict $$R$$. Its use is to isolate the invariance hypothesis: scaling the overall mass scale of the whole theory has weight zero on $$R$$, so the overall scale is not the obstruction; the obstruction is the independence of $$m_X$$ from $$m_B$$. The cases without a dark modulus are those that fix $$\mu$$ by an additional relation. If the relation is $$\mu=1$$ (an exact $$\mathbb Z_2$$ copy of the visible sector, or two species of equal mass) then $$R=r$$ is a number: $$R=1$$ is $$67.6\sigma$$ below the measured value and $$R=2$$, for a doubling of the number of states, is $$52.1\sigma$$ below it. An exact mirror sector also has a modulus, the temperature ratio of the two sectors, which appears in $$r$$; its value is limited by nucleosynthesis and by the effective number of relativistic species.

The remaining way to fix $$\mu$$ is to link the dark and visible confinement scales. If the dark baryon mass is proportional to the dark confinement scale $$\Lambda_d$$ and the dark gauge coupling equals a unified coupling at a scale $$M_U$$ that is fixed by the measured visible couplings, then $$\mu$$ has no free modulus. The statement that this route does not predict $$\mu$$ at the required precision is the following.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 8** (threshold floor). Let the dark coupling at $$M_U$$ be $$\alpha_d^{-1}(M_U)=\alpha_U^{-1}+\delta$$, with $$\delta$$ an unknown ultraviolet threshold correction, and let the one-loop running have coefficient $$b_d>0$$. Then $$\partial\ln\mu/\partial\delta=-2\pi/b_d$$, and an uncertainty $$\vert \delta\vert \ge\delta_0$$ gives $$\sigma(\ln\mu)\ge2\pi\delta_0/b_d$$. Reaching the precision of the measured ratio, $$\sigma(\ln R)=0.012$$, needs $$\delta_0\le0.012\,b_d/2\pi=0.0086$$ for $$b_d=4.5$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* At one loop $$\ln\Lambda_d=\ln M_U-2\pi\alpha_d^{-1}(M_U)/b_d$$; the visible confinement scale and $$M_U$$ do not depend on $$\delta$$, and $$\mu\propto\Lambda_d$$. Differentiate. $$\square$$
</div>

The sensitivity is present unless the construction suppresses it. Bai and Schwaller [[BS]](#ref-bs) relate the dark and visible confinement scales through infrared fixed points of the two gauge couplings, and Ritter and Volkas [[RV]](#ref-rv) find that the results depend on the initial conditions of the couplings in the ultraviolet, so a residual sensitivity of the type in the proposition remains. Constructions that relate the dark and visible couplings at a high scale are in [[LV]](#ref-lv). Reaching $$\sigma(\ln R)=0.012$$ with $$b_d\approx4.5$$ needs a threshold knowledge of $$0.009$$ in $$\alpha^{-1}$$, a relative precision of order $$10^{-4}$$ for $$\alpha^{-1}$$ of order $$40$$. Whether an infrared fixed point can make $$\partial\ln\mu/\partial\delta$$ small at two loops was not computed.

### Unification as a fixer of the dark-matter mass

A colourless multiplet of $$SU(2)$$ with zero hypercharge does not contribute to the one-loop running of $$\alpha_1$$ or $$\alpha_3$$, since the one-loop coefficient of a gauge coupling receives contributions proportional to the Dynkin index of the multiplet under that factor and these vanish. The scale at which $$\alpha_1=\alpha_3$$ is therefore independent of the multiplet's mass at one loop. With the inputs $$\alpha_{\rm em}^{-1}(M_Z)=127.95$$, $$\sin^2\theta_W=0.2312$$, $$\alpha_s(M_Z)=0.118$$ and the Standard Model coefficients $$(b_1,b_2,b_3)=(41/10,-19/6,-7)$$, we find (our computation) $$\alpha_1=\alpha_3$$ at $$2.4\times10^{14}$$ GeV with $$\alpha^{-1}=40.4$$, $$\alpha_1=\alpha_2$$ at $$1.0\times10^{13}$$ GeV and $$\alpha_2=\alpha_3$$ at $$9.7\times10^{16}$$ GeV. If a grand-unified gauge boson has mass of the order of this crossing scale, the dimension-six estimate $$\tau_p\sim M_X^4/(\alpha_G^2m_p^5)$$ with $$\alpha_G^{-1}=40$$ gives $$\tau_p\sim10^{29}$$ years at $$M_X=2.4\times10^{14}$$ GeV, to be compared with the Super-Kamiokande bound $$\tau/B(p\to e^+\pi^0)>2.4\times10^{34}$$ years at $$90\%$$ confidence level [[SK]](#ref-sk). The estimate is naive; its prefactor depends on hadronic matrix elements, and models with suppressed gauge-boson couplings change the relation between $$M_X$$ and the crossing scale. This is an observation about Standard Model running and not a theorem, and it is not claimed to exclude a mass for dark matter fixed by unification: it shows that a minimal electroweak multiplet cannot move the crossing scale, so that any such fixing needs the proton-decay constraint to be avoided by additional structure.

## 6. What these statements do not exclude

Each item drops a hypothesis, and states what remains.

An integration constant fixed by a global condition. The cosmological constant of Theorem 1 is a coordinate on the counterterm space, and a global condition (a sequestering or unimodular constraint) can assign it a value outside quantum field theory on a fixed background. The properties of the linear versions are on the page on [linear global constraints](/quantum-gravity/linear-lambda-constraints/).

A selection of the history. A principle that selects the number of degrees of freedom, or the state, by a measure on histories is not a condition built from a covariant functional of the kind in Lemma 1.

A stochastic spectator from inflation. It fixes $$\rho_\Lambda$$ from $$H_I=6.03$$ meV (section 3), replacing one input by another and requiring $$N\gtrsim2.6\times10^{61}$$ e-folds for $$m\lesssim H_0$$.

Rigid, selected data. Lemma 1 applies to data moved by a symmetry. A discrete rigid datum selected by an extremal property is not moved by one, and the lemma says nothing about it.

A non-topological, non-homogeneous second integer. For the Kodama and Verlinde conditions of Propositions 3 and 4, a second condition with an irrational ratio, or a bulk level $$k$$ not tied to the area ($$c<3/2$$), would escape; neither is supplied by the pure Lorentzian theory.

A clock that stores work. Proposition 6 gives a non-positive mean induced energy for passive populations only.

Free dark-mass modulus fixed by another relation. A symmetry equating the dark and visible scales (mirror) leaves the temperature-ratio modulus; a single confinement scale for dark matter and baryons [[Farrar]](#ref-farrar) gives $$\Omega_{\rm DM}/\Omega_b=5.3$$ within about $$15\%$$ for a sexaquark mass in $$1860$$–$$1880$$ MeV and is disputed on the grounds of the abundance of the relic [[KT]](#ref-kt), [[MS]](#ref-ms); a common ultraviolet coupling fixes $$\mu$$ at the precision of Proposition 8.

## 7. Open points

The decoupling statement of Theorem 1(a) for interacting fields and for states other than the adiabatic vacuum is taken from the literature and was checked here only for a free conformally coupled field in de Sitter space. The coefficient of $$H^6/m^2$$ for other couplings and for a general scale factor, and the numerical size of the order-six term for $$\Lambda$$CDM over $$0\le z\le1100$$, were not recomputed. Proposition 5 is a derivation within the mean-field treatment, and it is open whether a condensate state exists that is covariant under the choice of clock. The first calculation that would decide the clock-Hamiltonian route is the weight $$\beta$$ with which a physical clock, such as the cosmic microwave background photons or a neutrino flavour oscillation, couples to the volume. The ratio $$R$$ in a common-coupling model with an infrared fixed point at two loops is also open; Proposition 8 gives the quantity to be computed. The place of these results among the other approaches is on the [landscape page](/quantum-gravity/landscape/).

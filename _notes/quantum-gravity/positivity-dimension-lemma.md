Several approaches to quantum gravity report a spectral dimension, the dimension seen by a diffusing probe, that falls from four at long distances to about two at short distances. We ask whether this can happen for the diffusion generated by the inverse of a propagator with a positive Källén–Lehmann representation. Under four hypotheses it cannot. Let $$G(p^2)$$ be a Euclidean propagator, invariant under rotations, with a massless pole, nonnegative spectral weight and no constant term, and let a particle diffuse on flat momentum space $$\mathbb R^D$$ with the inverse propagator as a time-independent generator. Then the spectral dimension is at least $$D$$ at every diffusion time, and equals $$D$$ at all times only for the free propagator (Theorem 2). Here positive weight is a property of the Euclidean propagator; whether it coincides with unitarity depends on how a ghost pole is quantised (section 4).

A spectral dimension below $$D$$ therefore needs one of four things: negative weight, for which Proposition 4 gives a sum rule when the weight has finite total variation; a generator that is not rotation invariant, as in Hořava gravity; a measure on momentum space other than the flat one; or a diffusion time rescaled by the scale. For asymptotic safety the consequence is that the inverse of a positive-weight fluctuation propagator cannot give the value two (section 5).

Sotiriou, Visser and Weinfurtner [[SVW]](#ref-svw) show how a dispersion relation determines the spectral dimension and how to invert the relation. The value two in asymptotic safety was obtained by Lauscher and Reuter [[LR]](#ref-lr) from a generator built from the running cosmological constant. Calcagni, Eichhorn and Saueressig [[CES]](#ref-ces) replaced the identification of the scale in that construction by one with the diffusion time, and observed that heat kernels of such operators need not be positive. Hořava [[Hořava]](#ref-horava-ds) found $$d_s=1+D_s/z$$ for anisotropic scaling with exponent $$z$$ in $$D_s$$ space dimensions. What is added here is an inequality valid at every scale with its hypotheses stated, a proof by a virial identity that needs no theory of Bernstein functions, the identity itself for arbitrary generators, and a sum rule for signed weights.

## 1. The spectral dimension

Consider a particle diffusing on $$\mathbb R^D$$ with generator $$-\psi(-i\nabla)$$, where $$\psi(\xi)\ge0$$ is the *symbol*: for the ordinary Laplacian $$\psi(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^2$$. The probability density to be back at the starting point after diffusion time $$\sigma$$ is the *return amplitude*

$$
P(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb R^D}d^D\xi\;e^{-\sigma\psi(\xi)},\qquad d_s(\sigma)=-2\,\frac{d\ln P}{d\ln\sigma}=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma, \tag{1}
$$

where $$\langle g\rangle_\sigma=\int g\,e^{-\sigma\psi}d^D\xi\big/P(\sigma)$$. The factor $$2$$ is chosen so that the free case gives the dimension: $$P\propto\sigma^{-D/2}$$ and $$d_s=D$$. The quantity $$d_s(\sigma)$$ is the spectral dimension at the scale $$\sigma^{1/2}$$.

For a field theory, $$\psi$$ is the inverse Euclidean propagator. If the propagator is $$G(p^2)$$ we take $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ with $$F(x)=1/G(x)$$, $$x=p^2$$. This is the usual definition of the spectral dimension of a quantum field theory from its two-point function. The hypotheses are as follows.

(H1) The measure is flat: momentum space is $$\mathbb R^D$$ with Lebesgue measure, with no reduced density of states.

(H2) The diffusion is autonomous: $$\psi$$ does not depend on $$\sigma$$.

(H3) The symbol is rotation invariant, $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$; for a Lorentz-invariant theory this is the property of the Euclidean continuation.

(H4) The propagator has the Stieltjes form with a massless pole, a nonnegative weight and no constant term,

$$
G(x)=\frac ax+\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{\rho(d\mu)}{x+\mu},\qquad a>0,\quad\rho\ge0,\quad\int\frac{\rho(d\mu)}{1+\mu}<\infty, \tag{2}
$$

and $$F$$ satisfies the growth condition $$F(x)\ge c\,x^{\varepsilon}-C$$ for some $$c,\varepsilon>0$$, which makes $$P(\sigma)$$ finite for every $$\sigma$$.

The representation (2) is the Källén–Lehmann form of the Euclidean propagator in the standard quantisation of a unitary theory, with $$\rho(\mu)$$ the weight of states of mass squared $$\mu$$; we call $$\rho\ge0$$ positive weight. A constant term $$b>0$$ added to (2) makes $$F$$ bounded by $$1/b$$ and $$P$$ infinite, so $$d_s$$ is undefined; this is why the constant is excluded.

## 2. A virial identity for any symbol

Dropping (H3) and (H4) leaves an exact formula for any symbol.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 1** (virial identity). Let $$\psi:\mathbb R^D\to[0,\infty)$$ be locally Lipschitz with $$\psi(\xi)\ge c\lvert\xi\rvert^{\varepsilon}-C$$ for some $$c,\varepsilon>0$$, and $$\lvert\psi\rvert+\lvert\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\rvert\le C'(1+\lvert\xi\rvert)^m$$ almost everywhere (so that the integrations by parts below have no boundary term). Then $$P(\sigma)<\infty$$ for all $$\sigma>0$$ and

$$
d_s(\sigma)=D+2\sigma\,\Big\langle\psi-\tfrac12\,\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\Big\rangle_\sigma. \tag{3}
$$

Consequently, if $$t\mapsto\psi(t\xi)/t^2$$ is nonincreasing on $$t>0$$ for every $$\xi$$, then $$d_s(\sigma)\ge D$$ for all $$\sigma$$; if it is nondecreasing, then $$d_s(\sigma)\le D$$ for all $$\sigma$$.
</div>

Twice the average of $$\psi$$ is the spectral dimension. A scaling argument shows that twice the average of $$\tfrac12\xi\cdot\nabla\psi$$ is the dimension $$D$$ itself, so (3) measures how much faster or slower than quadratically the symbol grows along rays.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* Finiteness of $$P$$ follows from the lower bound on $$\psi$$, and differentiating under the integral gives $$d_s=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma$$, as in (1), by dominated convergence with the polynomial bounds. The vector field $$\xi\,e^{-\sigma\psi}$$ is Lipschitz with divergence $$(D-\sigma\,\xi\cdot\nabla\psi)e^{-\sigma\psi}$$, and its flux through the sphere of radius $$R$$ is at most $$R^{D}e^{-\sigma(cR^\varepsilon-C)}\to0$$. Hence $$\int(D-\sigma\xi\cdot\nabla\psi)e^{-\sigma\psi}d^D\xi=0$$, that is $$D=\sigma\langle\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_\sigma$$. Subtracting this from $$d_s=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma$$ gives (3). The derivative of $$\psi(t\xi)/t^2$$ at $$t=1$$ is $$\xi\cdot\nabla\psi-2\psi$$, and applying this at the point $$t\xi$$ shows that monotonicity for all $$t$$ fixes the sign of $$\psi-\frac12\xi\cdot\nabla\psi$$ at every point.
</div>

For a rotation-invariant symbol $$\psi=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ the integrand is $$F-xF'=-x^2(F/x)'$$, so $$d_s\ge D$$ whenever $$F(x)/x$$ is nonincreasing. Neither concavity nor monotonicity of $$F$$ is needed. A concave $$F$$ with $$F(0)=0$$, which is the case for the reciprocal of a Stieltjes function, is a special case.

## 3. Positive spectral weight

<div class="theorem" markdown="1">
**Theorem 2** (positivity and the spectral dimension). Under (H1) to (H4), $$d_s(\sigma)\ge D$$ for every $$\sigma>0$$. More precisely,

$$
d_s(\sigma)-D=2\sigma\Big\langle F(x)^2\int\rho(d\mu)\frac{\mu}{(x+\mu)^2}\Big\rangle_\sigma\ \ge0, \tag{4}
$$

so equality holds at some $$\sigma$$, and then at all $$\sigma$$, exactly when $$\rho=0$$, that is for the free propagator $$G=a/x$$.
</div>

Positivity of the weight makes $$xG(x)$$ nondecreasing, which is the statement that $$F(x)/x=1/(xG)$$ is nonincreasing; what remains is to check the hypotheses of Proposition 1.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* From (2), $$xG(x)=a+\int\rho(d\mu)\,x/(x+\mu)$$, which is nondecreasing, with derivative $$(xG)'=\int\rho(d\mu)\,\mu/(x+\mu)^2\ge0$$. Hence $$F/x=1/(xG)$$ is nonincreasing, and $$F-xF'=(G+xG')/G^2=(xG)'F^2\ge0$$. This gives $$F'\le F/x=1/(xG)\le1/a$$, the last step because $$xG\ge a$$. The function $$F$$ is therefore increasing with $$0<F(x)\le x/a$$, and $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ is locally Lipschitz with $$0\le\psi\le\lvert\xi\rvert^2/a$$ and $$0\le\xi\cdot\nabla\psi=2xF'\le2x/a$$. Together with the growth condition of (H4), this verifies the hypotheses of Proposition 1; (3) reduces to (4). If $$d_s(\sigma)=D$$ at some $$\sigma$$, the nonnegative integrand vanishes almost everywhere, so $$(xG)'=0$$ and $$\rho=0$$.
</div>

The pole enters through $$a>0$$: it bounds $$F/x$$ by $$1/a$$ near the origin, which gives the bounds above, it fixes the equality case, and it determines the infrared limit in Proposition 3(b). The reciprocal of a Stieltjes function is a complete Bernstein function [[Schilling–Song–Vondraček]](#ref-ssv), hence concave with $$F(0)=0$$, which gives the same inequality through $$F\ge xF'$$; (4) shows that the weaker monotonicity of $$xG$$ is enough. The inequality is strict away from the free case, and a positive propagator can overshoot $$D$$, as the second row of the table shows.

*Examples.* We computed $$d_s(\sigma)$$ in $$D=4$$ from $$d_s=2\sigma\langle F\rangle_\sigma$$ by numerical quadrature. In the table the mass scale of the massive pole is $$M=1$$, so $$\sigma$$ is measured in units of $$M^{-2}$$.

| $$G(x)$$ | $$\sigma=10^{-8}$$ | $$10^{-4}$$ | $$10^{-2}$$ | $$1$$ | $$10$$ | $$10^{2}$$ | $$10^{4}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$1/x$$ | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 |
| $$1/x+1/(x+1)$$ | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0050 | 4.3523 | 4.6784 | 4.1169 | 4.0012 |
| $$1/x-1/(x+1)$$ | 2.0001 | 2.0088 | 2.0865 | 2.7009 | 3.4290 | 3.8941 | 3.9988 |
| $$e^{-x}/x$$ | 0.2464 | 0.5037 | 0.9282 | 2.2596 | 3.3375 | 3.8905 | 3.9988 |

For the second row the two sides of (4), with $$\rho=\delta_1$$ and $$(xG)'=1/(x+1)^2$$, agree to twelve digits; at $$\sigma=10^{-3}$$ and $$1$$ they equal $$4.9975\times10^{-4}$$ and $$0.35226$$. The second row has positive weight, and $$d_s\ge4$$ everywhere with a maximum of $$4.736$$ (at $$\sigma\approx5.7$$). The third row has a ghost of weight $$-1$$ and goes from $$2$$ at short distances to $$4$$. The fourth row, an entire form factor, has no Stieltjes representation and goes to values below one without a plateau at two. For $$40$$ randomly drawn propagators $$G=1/x+\sum_iw_i/(x+\mu_i)$$ with one to four terms, weights $$w_i$$ in $$[0.05,5]$$ and masses $$\mu_i$$ between $$10^{-3}$$ and $$10^{3}$$, the smallest value of $$d_s$$ over $$25$$ values of $$\sigma$$ between $$10^{-6}$$ and $$10^{6}$$ is $$4.00000$$.

### Limits

The limits of $$d_s$$ at the two ends follow from the behaviour of $$F$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 3** (limits). (a) Let $$F\ge0$$ be locally bounded with $$P(\sigma)<\infty$$ for all $$\sigma$$, and suppose $$F(x)/x^\gamma\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)$$ as $$x\to\infty$$ for some $$\gamma>0$$. Then $$d_s(\sigma)\to D/\gamma$$ as $$\sigma\to0^+$$. (b) Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2, $$d_s(\sigma)\to D$$ as $$\sigma\to\infty$$. (c) Under (H4), $$\gamma\le1$$ whenever the limit in (a) exists, so $$D/\gamma\ge D$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* (a) Substitute $$x=\sigma^{-1/\gamma}y$$ in the radial form $$P=\frac12\Omega_D\int x^{D/2-1}e^{-\sigma F(x)}dx$$ and in $$\sigma\langle F\rangle$$, with $$\Omega_D$$ the area of the unit sphere. Pointwise $$\sigma F(\sigma^{-1/\gamma}y)\to\kappa y^\gamma$$. Choose $$x_1$$ with $$F(x)\ge\frac\kappa2x^\gamma$$ for $$x\ge x_1$$. For $$\sigma\le1$$ and $$y\ge x_1$$ both the denominator and the numerator integrand are bounded by a constant times $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-\kappa y^\gamma/4}$$, using $$te^{-t}\le e^{-t/2}$$; on $$y<x_1$$ they are bounded by a constant times $$y^{D/2-1}$$. All are integrable, so by dominated convergence $$2\sigma\langle F\rangle\to2\langle\kappa y^\gamma\rangle$$ in the limit measure $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-\kappa y^\gamma}dy$$. By Proposition 1 applied to the pure power $$\psi=\kappa\lvert\xi\rvert^{2\gamma}$$ at $$\sigma=1$$ this average equals $$D/(2\gamma)$$. (b) Here $$F(x)=x/a\,(1+o(1))$$ as $$x\to0$$; choose $$x_1$$ with $$F\ge x/(2a)$$ on $$(0,x_1)$$. For $$\sigma\ge1$$ split the integrals at $$x_1$$. On $$x>x_1$$, $$F\ge F(x_1)$$ because $$F$$ is increasing, so $$e^{-\sigma F}\le e^{-\sigma F(x_1)/2}e^{-F/2}$$, and this part is bounded by $$e^{-\sigma F(x_1)/2}$$ times a constant, exponentially small against $$P\sim\sigma^{-D/2}$$; the same holds for the numerator of $$\langle F\rangle$$. On $$x<x_1$$ substitute $$x=y/\sigma$$: $$\sigma F(y/\sigma)\to y/a$$, and the integrands are dominated by $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-y/(2a)}$$ times a constant. In the limit $$2\sigma\langle F\rangle\to2\langle y/a\rangle=D$$, the mean of $$y/a$$ under $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-y/a}dy$$ being $$D/2$$. (c) Since $$F/x$$ is nonincreasing, $$F(x)\le xF(1)$$ for $$x\ge1$$, so $$\gamma\le1$$.
</div>

Part (c) is the statement about the ultraviolet value: a positive-weight propagator has $$G\gtrsim1/x$$ at large $$x$$, so it cannot fall off like $$x^{-2}$$. It can fall off more slowly, $$G\sim x^{-1+\eta/2}$$ with $$0<\eta<2$$, which needs infinite total weight and gives $$\gamma=1-\eta/2<1$$, that is $$d_s=D/\gamma>D$$ in the ultraviolet.

## 4. Where a reduction can come from

Dropping any of the four hypotheses allows $$d_s<D$$. We take them in turn: rotation invariance (H3), positivity (H4), and the measure and the clock (H1, H2).

### Anisotropic symbols

If the symbol is not rotation invariant, (3) allows $$d_s<D$$ whenever the symbol grows faster than quadratically in some direction. Hořava's gravity at a Lifshitz point [[Hořava 2009]](#ref-horava) has $$D=4$$ Euclidean dimensions $$(\omega,\vec k)$$ and, in the ultraviolet, the dispersion $$\psi=\omega^2+k^2+k^6$$ with anisotropic scaling $$z=3$$. Then $$\psi-\frac12\xi\cdot\nabla\psi=-2k^6<0$$, and (3) reads $$d_s=4-4\sigma\langle k^6\rangle_\sigma$$. Evaluating both sides of (3) by quadrature gives the same numbers, for example $$2.039076$$ at $$\sigma=10^{-2}$$ and $$3.994825$$ at $$\sigma=100$$. The values are

| $$\sigma$$ | $$10^{-8}$$ | $$10^{-4}$$ | $$10^{-2}$$ | $$1$$ | $$10$$ | $$10^{2}$$ | $$10^{4}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$d_s$$ | 2.0000 | 2.0018 | 2.0391 | 2.7038 | 3.7282 | 3.9948 | 4.0000 |

and the limits follow by scaling: in the ultraviolet the $$k^6$$ term dominates and $$P\propto\sigma^{-1/2}\sigma^{-1/2}$$ (one factor from $$\omega$$, one from the three momenta $$k$$ with $$\psi\sim k^6$$), so $$d_s=2$$, and $$P\propto\sigma^{-2}$$ in the infrared. For each fixed $$k$$ the propagator $$1/(\omega^2+k^2+k^6)$$ is a Stieltjes function of $$\omega^2$$ with positive weight; what the example gives up is rotation invariance, that is a preferred frame, whose low-energy leakage is discussed on the page on [frame leakage into low-energy physics](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/).

### Negative weight

Drop positivity but keep the other hypotheses. Let $$a>0$$ and let $$\rho$$ be a signed measure of finite total variation on $$(0,\infty)$$, so that $$G(x)=a/x+\int\rho(d\mu)/(x+\mu)$$ has a massless pole of residue $$a$$. Put $$W=a+\rho((0,\infty))$$, the total weight.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 4** (sum rule). With $$G$$ as above, $$xG(x)\to W$$ as $$x\to\infty$$. If $$W>0$$ and $$G>0$$ on $$(0,\infty)$$, then $$F=1/G$$ satisfies $$F(x)\sim x/W$$, $$P(\sigma)$$ is finite, and $$d_s(\sigma)\to D$$ as $$\sigma\to0$$. If $$W<0$$, then $$G<0$$ at large $$x$$ and $$P=\infty$$ for every $$\sigma$$. Hence, when $$G>0$$ on $$(0,\infty)$$, a spectral dimension in the ultraviolet that is defined and different from $$D$$ forces $$W=0$$, so that the negative part of $$\rho$$ has total mass at least $$a$$. For $$\rho=\sum_i\alpha_i\delta_{m_i^2}$$ this reads $$\sum_i\alpha_i=-a$$, the sum running over all massive poles, positive and negative. If $$W=0$$, $$G>0$$ on $$(0,\infty)$$ and $$\int\mu\lvert\rho\rvert<\infty$$, then $$x^2G(x)\to-\int\mu\,\rho(d\mu)$$, and when this limit is positive $$F\sim\kappa x^2$$ with $$\kappa=-1/\int\mu\rho(d\mu)$$ and $$d_s\to D/2$$, which is $$2$$ in $$D=4$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* $$xG(x)=a+\int\rho(d\mu)\,x/(x+\mu)$$ with $$\lvert x/(x+\mu)\rvert\le1$$ and pointwise limit $$1$$, so $$xG\to W$$ by dominated convergence. If $$W>0$$ and $$G>0$$, then $$F$$ is continuous and positive on $$(0,\infty)$$, $$F\sim x/a$$ at the origin and $$F\sim x/W$$ at infinity; the growth condition holds, so $$P$$ is finite, and Proposition 3(a) with $$\gamma=1$$ gives the limit. If $$W<0$$, then $$G<0$$ for large $$x$$, $$F\to-\infty$$ like $$x/\lvert W\rvert$$, and $$e^{-\sigma F}$$ grows too fast for $$P$$ to converge. So $$W=0$$, that is $$\rho((0,\infty))=-a$$, and the negative part of $$\rho$$ has mass at least $$a$$. For $$W=0$$, $$x^2G=x\,(xG)=-\int\rho\,\mu x/(x+\mu)\to-\int\mu\rho$$ when $$\int\mu\lvert\rho\rvert<\infty$$. The last statement is Proposition 3(a) with $$\gamma=2$$.
</div>

The model case is the propagator of quadratic gravity [[Stelle 1977]](#ref-stelle) restricted to the massive spin-two ghost, $$G=1/x-1/(x+M^2)=M^2/(x(x+M^2))$$ of the third row of the table, with $$F=x(x+M^2)/M^2$$, $$\gamma=2$$ and $$\kappa=1/M^2$$: it has $$a=1$$, $$\alpha_1=-1$$, $$W=0$$ and $$\int\mu\rho=-M^2$$, consistent with $$d_s=2$$ in the ultraviolet. As a second example, $$G=1/x+\tfrac12/(x+1)-\tfrac32/(x+2)=(2.5x+2)/(x(x+1)(x+2))$$ has a positive and a negative pole, $$W=0$$ and $$\kappa=1/2.5$$; the quadrature gives $$d_s=2.0001$$ at $$\sigma=10^{-8}$$ and $$2.0000$$ at $$10^{-12}$$.

Prescriptions for the ghost pole that act only in the Lorentzian continuation leave the Euclidean $$G$$, hence $$d_s$$, unchanged. The fakeon prescription of Anselmi [[Anselmi]](#ref-ans), applied to quantum gravity in [[Anselmi–Piva 2018]](#ref-apgrav), is of this kind, and the Lee–Wick formulation of [[Anselmi–Piva 2017]](#ref-aplw) defines the theory from a Euclidean one by a nonanalytic Wick rotation, so that the Euclidean propagator is the one with the ghost pole. Positivity of $$\rho$$ in (2) and unitarity of the Lorentzian theory are therefore different statements, and Theorem 2 does not say that $$d_s<D$$ is incompatible with unitarity.

### Density of states and the diffusion clock

Dropping (H1) or (H2) is a change of the definition of $$P$$. With a measure $$\nu(dx)\propto x^{D_{\rm eff}/2-1}dx$$ in place of the flat one and $$F=x$$, $$d_s=D_{\rm eff}$$. If the diffusion time is replaced by a function $$\sigma\to s(\sigma)$$ of itself, which is what happens when a scale is identified with an inverse diffusion time in a generator that depends on the scale, $$d_s$$ is multiplied by $$d\ln s/d\ln\sigma$$; for $$s=\sigma^{\gamma}$$ the factor is $$\gamma$$. In both cases $$d_s<D$$ is possible with a positive propagator, and the reduction is carried by the measure or the clock. A reported reduction $$d_s\to D'<D$$ has to be read together with the propagator, the measure and the clock it refers to.

## 5. The asymptotic-safety value two

Lauscher and Reuter [[LR]](#ref-lr) obtain $$d_s=2$$ in four dimensions from a generator built from the running of the cosmological constant. The eigenvalue $$x=p^2$$ of a mode is identified with the scale $$k^2$$ of the effective average action, which gives the generator $$\psi(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^2f(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$, with $$f(k^2)$$ the ratio of the running cosmological constant at scale $$k$$ to its macroscopic value. This is autonomous, on a flat measure and rotation invariant, so (H1) to (H3) hold. At the fixed point the cosmological constant runs as $$k^2$$, hence $$f\propto x$$, $$\psi\propto\lvert\xi\rvert^4$$, and Proposition 3(a) with $$\gamma=2$$ gives $$d_s=D/2$$ in every dimension $$D$$ (equation (3.15) of [[LR]](#ref-lr)); the value two is special to $$D=4$$. The generator is defined by the running of a coupling, not as the inverse of a propagator, so (H4) is not a statement about it.

The same paper also gives the dressed graviton propagator of the fixed-point regime, $$G^{-1}\propto(p^2)^{1-\eta/2}$$ with the anomalous dimension $$\eta=2-D$$ (their equation (2.6)), which in $$D=4$$ is $$G\propto x^{-2}$$. Taking the inverse of this propagator as the generator is a different construction, and it is the one to which Theorem 2 applies. With $$F=1/G\sim x^{\gamma}$$ and $$\gamma=1-\eta/2$$, Proposition 3(a) gives $$d_s=D/\gamma=2D/(2-\eta)$$, which is $$2$$ for $$\eta=2-D$$ in every $$D$$ and agrees with the generator above only in $$D=4$$. By Proposition 3(c) a positive-weight propagator with a massless pole has $$\gamma\le1$$, that is $$\eta\ge0$$, so the value $$2$$ cannot be reached by such a propagator.

The Lorentzian graviton spectral function has been computed non-perturbatively and is reported positive, with a massless one-graviton peak and a multi-graviton continuum [[FLPR]](#ref-flpr); the self-consistent computation in an on-shell scheme has unit total spectral weight and a close-to-quadratic decay of the continuum [[PRW]](#ref-prw). If the spectral representation of the transverse-traceless fluctuation propagator is the representation (2), with the pole and without a constant term, Theorem 2 gives $$d_s\ge4$$ at every scale for the diffusion it generates. With unit total weight, $$W=1$$, so $$F\sim x$$ in the ultraviolet and $$d_s\to4$$ at both ends (Proposition 3). The value $$2$$ of the Euclidean programme therefore does not belong to this propagator taken as the generator of an autonomous diffusion on a flat measure.

Calcagni, Eichhorn and Saueressig [[CES]](#ref-ces) use a different route. They identify the scale with the inverse diffusion time, $$k\sim\sigma^{-1/(\delta+2)}$$ for a metric running as $$k^\delta$$ (their equation (13)), so that the effective generator depends on $$\sigma$$ and $$d_s=2D/(2+\delta)$$ (their equation (17)). This is the clock change of section 4, outside (H2), and Theorem 2 makes no statement about it.

The reading above rests on three assumptions. The identification of a tensor propagator with a scalar function $$G(p^2)$$ covers the transverse-traceless part, which has a definite spectral representation, and not the spin-zero part or the gauge dependence. The positivity enters as a hypothesis taken from the cited computations. The generator of [[LR]](#ref-lr) is built from the background-field running of the cosmological constant, and no statement is made about its relation to the positivity of the fluctuation propagator.

## 6. A single ghost and a laboratory bound

Under its hypotheses Proposition 4 requires negative weight of total size at least $$a$$ for a reduction in the ultraviolet, and models of Stelle type supply it with massive ghosts, whose Newtonian potential is finite at the origin through cancellation between scalar and tensor massive modes [[MNS]](#ref-mns). Take a single ghost with $$G=1/x-1/(x+M^2)$$. For this propagator the third row of the table shows the transition at $$\sigma$$ of order $$M^{-2}$$ ($$d_s=3.43$$ at $$\sigma=10$$ and $$2.70$$ at $$\sigma=1$$ in units $$M=1$$), that is at lengths of order $$1/M$$. The static potential is the Fourier transform of $$1/k^2-1/(k^2+M^2)$$, so the Newtonian potential is multiplied by $$1-e^{-Mr}$$: finite at $$r=0$$, with a Yukawa correction of relative strength $$\alpha=-1$$ and range $$\lambda=1/M$$. A torsion-balance test of the inverse-square law with detector-attractor separations down to $$52\ \mu$$m limits, at $$95\%$$ confidence, gravitational-strength Yukawa interactions to ranges below $$38.6\ \mu$$m [[Lee et al.]](#ref-eotwash). The published constraint is on $$\lvert\alpha\rvert$$, and we apply it at $$\lvert\alpha\rvert=1$$; the curves for each sign are in the supplementary material of that paper and may differ slightly. This gives $$M\ge\hbar c/38.6\ \mu\text{m}=5.11$$ meV (from $$\hbar c=197.327$$ eV nm), so a reduction carried by one such ghost cannot set in at lengths above $$38.6\ \mu$$m. A ghost at $$M=2.24$$ meV, a scale of the order of the dark-energy density to the power one quarter, has $$\lambda=88\ \mu$$m and is excluded.

This is an empirical statement about a model, not part of Theorem 2. It assumes a single Yukawa term of strength $$-1$$. With several massive poles and the massless pole normalised to unit residue, the strengths sum to $$-1$$ (Proposition 4), and the bound on each depends on its own strength. For quadratic gravity the static potential is $$V=-(GM_\odot/r)\,(1+\tfrac13e^{-m_0r}-\tfrac43e^{-m_2r})$$ [[Stelle 1978]](#ref-stelle78), with a spin-two ghost of strength $$-4/3$$ and mass $$m_2$$ and a scalar of strength $$+1/3$$ and mass $$m_0$$. The constraint at $$\lvert\alpha\rvert=4/3$$ excludes at least the ranges excluded at $$\lvert\alpha\rvert=1$$, so the bound on $$m_2$$ is at least as strong when the scalar term does not cancel the tensor term, that is when $$m_0$$ and $$m_2$$ are not close.

## 7. Limits of the statement

Theorem 2 concerns the scalar function $$G(p^2)$$ with the pole and no constant term. A constant term $$b>0$$ makes the symbol bounded, $$P$$ infinite and $$d_s$$ undefined in the sense of (1); the two-dimensional Benincasa–Dowker propagator on the page on [the spectral representation of the causal-set d'Alembertian](/quantum-gravity/causal-box-spectrum/) has such a term, of size $$1/2$$ in the normalisation of that page, so its spectral dimension is not defined by (1) and would need a regularised definition. For a spin-two propagator with several tensor structures and gauge parameters there is no corresponding statement here, and whether non-perturbative information on the Lorentzian spectral function beyond the transverse-traceless mode changes the conclusion for the fluctuation propagator is not known to us.

Proposition 1 gives a criterion for non-radial generators with jump (Lévy) parts, but whether every symmetric Lévy exponent with a nondegenerate Gaussian part has $$d_s\ge D$$ is not proved here. Objects other than the two-point function, such as the spectral dimension of an ensemble of geometries or of a causal-set random walk, are averages of positive Markov chains and are not governed by a single autonomous generator; they are outside the statement. Whether a clock identification of the kind used in [[CES]](#ref-ces) can be phrased as a property of a measurable observable is open.

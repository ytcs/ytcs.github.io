Several approaches to quantum gravity report that the spectral dimension, the dimension seen by a diffusing probe, falls from four at long distances to about two at short distances. We ask whether this can happen when the propagator of the theory has the positive spectral representation of a unitary theory. For a Lorentz-invariant propagator, that is a Euclidean propagator invariant under rotations, with a massless pole, positive Källén–Lehmann weight and no constant term, it cannot: the spectral dimension of the diffusion generated by the inverse propagator on flat momentum space is at least the dimension $$D$$ of the space at every diffusion time, and equals $$D$$ at all times only for the free propagator. The assumptions are four: the measure on momentum space is flat, the diffusion is autonomous, the generator is rotation invariant, and the propagator is a Stieltjes function with a pole at zero and no constant term. Dropping the third gives an exact formula for the spectral dimension of an arbitrary generator, from which Hořava's value of two follows with positive weights and a preferred frame. Dropping the fourth gives a sum rule: a spectral dimension below $$D$$ in the ultraviolet needs negative weight of total size at least one. For asymptotic safety the lemma says that the value two cannot be carried by a positive-weight Lorentz-invariant fluctuation propagator.

The spectral dimension was introduced for fractal and lattice geometries and extended to theories with a dispersion relation; Sotiriou, Visser and Weinfurtner [[SVW]](#ref-svw) study how a dispersion relation determines it, including the inverse problem. In asymptotically safe gravity Lauscher and Reuter [[LR]](#ref-lr) obtain $$d_s=2D/(2-\eta)$$ from the anomalous dimension $$\eta$$ of the fixed point, which equals $$2$$ for $$\eta=2-D$$. Calcagni, Eichhorn and Saueressig [[CES]](#ref-ces) pointed out that heat kernels of quantum-improved operators need not be positive and modified the diffusion equation to repair this. Hořava [[Hořava]](#ref-horava-ds) found $$d_s=1+D_s/z$$ for anisotropic scaling with exponent $$z$$ in $$D_s$$ space dimensions, which is $$2$$ for $$D_s=3$$, $$z=3$$. Higher-derivative gravity of Stelle type [[Stelle]](#ref-stelle) has a massive spin-two ghost, and the Newtonian potential of such models is finite at the origin [[MNS]](#ref-mns). The Lorentzian graviton spectral function has been computed non-perturbatively and found positive [[FLPR]](#ref-flpr) [[PRW]](#ref-prw). What we add is the all-scale inequality with its hypotheses stated, a proof that needs no theory of Bernstein functions, the virial formula for general generators, and the sum rule for the signed case; the numbers in the examples were recomputed.

## 1. The spectral dimension

Consider a particle diffusing on $$\mathbb R^D$$ with generator $$-\psi(-i\nabla)$$, where $$\psi(\xi)\ge0$$ is the *symbol*: for the ordinary Laplacian $$\psi(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^2$$. The probability density to be back at the starting point after diffusion time $$\sigma$$ is the *return amplitude*

$$
P(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb R^D}d^D\xi\;e^{-\sigma\psi(\xi)},\qquad d_s(\sigma)=-2\,\frac{d\ln P}{d\ln\sigma}=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma, \tag{1}
$$

where $$\langle g\rangle_\sigma=\int g\,e^{-\sigma\psi}d^D\xi\big/P(\sigma)$$. The factor $$2$$ is chosen so that the free case gives the dimension: $$P\propto\sigma^{-D/2}$$ and $$d_s=D$$. The quantity $$d_s(\sigma)$$ is the spectral dimension at the scale $$\sigma^{1/2}$$.

For a field theory, $$\psi$$ is the inverse Euclidean propagator. If the propagator is $$G(p^2)$$ we take $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ with $$F(x)=1/G(x)$$, $$x=p^2$$. This is the usual definition of the spectral dimension of a quantum field theory from its two-point function, and it fixes what the hypotheses mean.

(H1) The measure is flat: momentum space is $$\mathbb R^D$$ with Lebesgue measure, with no reduced density of states.

(H2) The diffusion is autonomous: $$\psi$$ does not depend on $$\sigma$$.

(H3) The symbol is rotation invariant, $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$; for a Lorentz-invariant theory this is the property of the Euclidean continuation.

(H4) The propagator is a Stieltjes function with a massless pole and no constant term,

$$
G(x)=\frac ax+\int_{(0,\infty)}\frac{\rho(d\mu)}{x+\mu},\qquad a>0,\quad\rho\ge0,\quad\int\frac{\rho(d\mu)}{1+\mu}<\infty, \tag{2}
$$

together with a growth condition, $$F(x)\ge c\,x^{\varepsilon}-C$$ for some $$c,\varepsilon>0$$, which makes $$P(\sigma)$$ finite for every $$\sigma$$.

The representation (2) is the Källén–Lehmann form of the propagator of a unitary theory; $$\rho(\mu)$$ is the weight of states of mass squared $$\mu$$, which is nonnegative. A constant term $$b>0$$ added to (2) makes $$F$$ bounded by $$1/b$$ and $$P$$ infinite, so $$d_s$$ is undefined; this is why the constant is excluded.

## 2. A virial identity for any symbol

Dropping (H3) and (H4) leaves an exact formula for any symbol.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 1** (virial identity). Let $$\psi:\mathbb R^D\to[0,\infty)$$ be locally Lipschitz with $$\psi(\xi)\ge c\lvert\xi\rvert^{\varepsilon}-C$$ for some $$c,\varepsilon>0$$, and $$\lvert\psi\rvert+\lvert\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\rvert\le C'(1+\lvert\xi\rvert)^m$$ almost everywhere. Then $$P(\sigma)<\infty$$ for all $$\sigma>0$$ and

$$
d_s(\sigma)=D+2\sigma\,\Big\langle\psi-\tfrac12\,\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\Big\rangle_\sigma. \tag{3}
$$

Consequently, if $$t\mapsto\psi(t\xi)/t^2$$ is nonincreasing on $$t>0$$ for every $$\xi$$, then $$d_s(\sigma)\ge D$$ for all $$\sigma$$; if it is nondecreasing, then $$d_s(\sigma)\le D$$ for all $$\sigma$$.
</div>

The idea is that the spectral dimension is twice the average of the symbol, while a scaling argument says that the dimension itself is twice the average of the radial derivative of the symbol; the difference measures how much faster or slower than quadratically the symbol grows.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* Finiteness of $$P$$ follows from the lower bound on $$\psi$$, and differentiating under the integral gives $$d_s=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma$$, as in (1), by dominated convergence with the polynomial bounds. The vector field $$\xi\,e^{-\sigma\psi}$$ is Lipschitz with divergence $$(D-\sigma\,\xi\cdot\nabla\psi)e^{-\sigma\psi}$$, and its flux through the sphere of radius $$R$$ is at most $$R^{D}e^{-\sigma(cR^\varepsilon-C)}\to0$$. Hence $$\int(D-\sigma\xi\cdot\nabla\psi)e^{-\sigma\psi}d^D\xi=0$$, that is $$D=\sigma\langle\xi\cdot\nabla\psi\rangle_\sigma$$. Subtracting this from $$d_s=2\sigma\langle\psi\rangle_\sigma$$ gives (3). The derivative of $$\psi(t\xi)/t^2$$ at $$t=1$$ is $$\xi\cdot\nabla\psi-2\psi$$, and applying this at the point $$t\xi$$ shows that monotonicity for all $$t$$ fixes the sign of $$\psi-\frac12\xi\cdot\nabla\psi$$ at every point.
</div>

For a rotation-invariant symbol $$\psi=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ the integrand is $$F-xF'=-x^2(F/x)'$$, so $$d_s\ge D$$ whenever $$F(x)/x$$ is nonincreasing. Neither concavity nor monotonicity of $$F$$ is needed. Concave $$F$$ with $$F(0)=0$$, which is the case for the reciprocal of a Stieltjes function, is a special case.

## 3. Positive spectral weight

<div class="theorem" markdown="1">
**Theorem 2** (positivity and the spectral dimension). Under (H1) to (H4), $$d_s(\sigma)\ge D$$ for every $$\sigma>0$$. More precisely,

$$
d_s(\sigma)-D=2\sigma\Big\langle F(x)^2\int\rho(d\mu)\frac{\mu}{(x+\mu)^2}\Big\rangle_\sigma\ \ge0, \tag{4}
$$

so equality holds at some $$\sigma$$, and then at all $$\sigma$$, exactly when $$\rho=0$$, that is for the free propagator $$G=a/x$$.
</div>

The proof is the observation that positivity of the weight makes $$xG(x)$$ nondecreasing, which is the statement that $$F(x)/x=1/(xG)$$ is nonincreasing.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* From (2), $$xG(x)=a+\int\rho(d\mu)\,x/(x+\mu)$$, which is nondecreasing, with derivative $$(xG)'=\int\rho(d\mu)\,\mu/(x+\mu)^2\ge0$$. Hence $$F/x=1/(xG)$$ is nonincreasing, and $$F-xF'=(G+xG')/G^2=(xG)'F^2\ge0$$. The function $$F$$ is increasing, $$F(x)\sim x/a$$ at the origin and $$F'\le1/a$$, so $$\psi(\xi)=F(\lvert\xi\rvert^2)$$ is Lipschitz. Moreover $$F(x)/x\le F(1)$$ for $$x\ge1$$, so $$\psi$$ and $$\xi\cdot\nabla\psi=2xF'\le2F$$ are polynomially bounded. Proposition 1 applies and (3) reduces to (4). If $$d_s(\sigma)=D$$ at some $$\sigma$$, the nonnegative integrand vanishes almost everywhere, so $$(xG)'=0$$ and $$\rho=0$$.
</div>

The reciprocal of a Stieltjes function is a complete Bernstein function [[Schilling–Song–Vondraček]](#ref-ssv), hence concave with $$F(0)=0$$, which gives the same inequality through $$F\ge xF'$$; (4) shows that only the weaker monotonicity of $$xG$$ is needed. The inequality is not an equality away from the free case: a positive propagator can overshoot $$D$$, as the second row of the table shows.

*Examples.* We computed $$d_s(\sigma)$$ in $$D=4$$ from $$d_s=2\sigma\langle F\rangle_\sigma$$ by quadrature in the variable $$\ln x$$ (units $$M=1$$ for the mass scale; the requested relative accuracy of the quadrature is $$10^{-12}$$).

| $$G(x)$$ | $$\sigma=10^{-8}$$ | $$10^{-4}$$ | $$10^{-2}$$ | $$1$$ | $$10$$ | $$10^{2}$$ | $$10^{4}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$1/x$$ | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0000 |
| $$1/x+1/(x+1)$$ | 4.0000 | 4.0000 | 4.0050 | 4.3523 | 4.6784 | 4.1169 | 4.0012 |
| $$1/x-1/(x+1)$$ | 2.0001 | 2.0088 | 2.0865 | 2.7009 | 3.4290 | 3.8941 | 3.9988 |
| $$e^{-x}/x$$ | 0.2464 | 0.5037 | 0.9282 | 2.2596 | 3.3375 | 3.8905 | 3.9988 |

For the second row the right side of (4), with $$\rho=\delta_1$$ and $$(xG)'=1/(x+1)^2$$, agrees with the left side to $$10^{-13}$$ at $$\sigma=10^{-3}$$ and $$1$$ ($$5.0\times10^{-4}$$ and $$0.35226$$). The second row has positive weight and illustrates Theorem 2: $$d_s\ge4$$ everywhere with a maximum of $$4.736$$ (over a grid of $$300$$ values of $$\sigma$$ between $$10^{-8}$$ and $$10^{6}$$). The third row, with a ghost of weight $$-1$$, goes from $$2$$ at short distances to $$4$$; its minimum on the grid is $$2.0001$$. The fourth row, an entire form factor, has no Stieltjes representation and goes to values below one without a plateau at two. In a further test we drew $$40$$ propagators $$G=1/x+\sum_iw_i/(x+\mu_i)$$ with one to four terms, $$w_i\in[0.05,5]$$ and $$\mu_i$$ log-uniform in $$[10^{-3},10^{3}]$$, and evaluated $$d_s$$ at $$25$$ values of $$\sigma$$ between $$10^{-6}$$ and $$10^{6}$$: the minimum over all $$1000$$ values is $$4.00000$$.

### Limits

The limits of $$d_s$$ at the two ends follow from the behaviour of $$F$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 3** (limits). (a) Let $$F\ge0$$ be locally bounded with $$P(\sigma)<\infty$$ for all $$\sigma$$, and suppose $$F(x)/x^\gamma\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)$$ as $$x\to\infty$$ for some $$\gamma>0$$. Then $$d_s(\sigma)\to D/\gamma$$ as $$\sigma\to0^+$$. (b) Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2, $$d_s(\sigma)\to D$$ as $$\sigma\to\infty$$. (c) Under (H4), $$\gamma\le1$$ whenever the limit in (a) exists, so $$D/\gamma\ge D$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* (a) Substitute $$x=\sigma^{-1/\gamma}y$$ in the radial form $$P=\frac12\Omega_D\int x^{D/2-1}e^{-\sigma F(x)}dx$$ and in $$\sigma\langle F\rangle$$, with $$\Omega_D$$ the area of the unit sphere. Pointwise $$\sigma F(\sigma^{-1/\gamma}y)\to\kappa y^\gamma$$. Choose $$x_1$$ with $$F(x)\ge\frac\kappa2x^\gamma$$ for $$x\ge x_1$$. For $$\sigma\le1$$ and $$y\ge x_1$$ both the denominator and the numerator integrand are bounded by a constant times $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-\kappa y^\gamma/4}$$, using $$te^{-t}\le e^{-t/2}$$; on $$y<x_1$$ they are bounded by a constant times $$y^{D/2-1}$$. All are integrable, so by dominated convergence $$2\sigma\langle F\rangle\to2\langle\kappa y^\gamma\rangle$$ in the limit measure $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-\kappa y^\gamma}dy$$. By Proposition 1 applied to the pure power $$\psi=\kappa\lvert\xi\rvert^{2\gamma}$$ at $$\sigma=1$$ this average equals $$D/(2\gamma)$$. (b) Here $$F(x)=x/a\,(1+o(1))$$ as $$x\to0$$; choose $$x_1$$ with $$F\ge x/(2a)$$ on $$(0,x_1)$$. For $$\sigma\ge1$$ split the integrals at $$x_1$$. On $$x>x_1$$, $$F\ge F(x_1)$$ because $$F$$ is increasing, so $$e^{-\sigma F}\le e^{-\sigma F(x_1)/2}e^{-F/2}$$, and this part is bounded by $$e^{-\sigma F(x_1)/2}$$ times a constant, exponentially small against $$P\sim\sigma^{-D/2}$$; the same holds for the numerator of $$\langle F\rangle$$. On $$x<x_1$$ substitute $$x=y/\sigma$$: $$\sigma F(y/\sigma)\to y/a$$, and the integrands are dominated by $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-y/(2a)}$$ times a constant. In the limit $$2\sigma\langle F\rangle\to2\langle y/a\rangle=D$$, the mean of $$y/a$$ under $$y^{D/2-1}e^{-y/a}dy$$ being $$D/2$$. (c) Since $$F/x$$ is nonincreasing, $$F(x)\le xF(1)$$ for $$x\ge1$$, so $$\gamma\le1$$.
</div>

Part (c) is the statement about the ultraviolet value: a positive-weight propagator has $$G\gtrsim1/x$$ at large $$x$$, so it cannot fall off like $$x^{-2}$$. It can fall off more slowly, $$G\sim x^{-1+\eta/2}$$ with $$0<\eta<2$$, which needs infinite total weight and gives $$\gamma=1-\eta/2<1$$, that is $$d_s=D/\gamma>D$$ in the ultraviolet.

## 4. Where a reduction can come from

Dropping any of the four hypotheses allows $$d_s<D$$. We take them in turn: rotation invariance (H3), positivity (H4), and the measure and the clock (H1, H2).

### Anisotropic symbols

If the symbol is not rotation invariant, (3) allows $$d_s<D$$ whenever the symbol grows faster than quadratically in some direction. Hořava's gravity at a Lifshitz point [[Hořava 2009]](#ref-horava) has $$D=4$$ Euclidean dimensions $$(\omega,\vec k)$$ and, in the ultraviolet, the dispersion $$\psi=\omega^2+k^2+k^6$$ with anisotropic scaling $$z=3$$. Then $$\psi-\frac12\xi\cdot\nabla\psi=-2k^6<0$$, and (3) reads $$d_s=4-4\sigma\langle k^6\rangle_\sigma$$. We evaluated both sides of (3) by quadrature: they agree to at least ten digits at $$\sigma=10^{-8}$$ to $$10^{4}$$ (for example $$2.039076$$ at $$\sigma=10^{-2}$$ and $$3.994825$$ at $$\sigma=100$$). The values are

| $$\sigma$$ | $$10^{-8}$$ | $$10^{-4}$$ | $$10^{-2}$$ | $$1$$ | $$10$$ | $$10^{2}$$ | $$10^{4}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$d_s$$ | 2.0000 | 2.0018 | 2.0391 | 2.7038 | 3.7282 | 3.9948 | 4.0000 |

and the limits follow by scaling: $$P\propto\sigma^{-1/2}\sigma^{-1/2}$$ in the ultraviolet, where the $$k^6$$ term dominates, so $$d_s=2$$, and $$P\propto\sigma^{-2}$$ in the infrared. For each fixed $$k$$ the propagator $$1/(\omega^2+k^2+k^6)$$ is a Stieltjes function of $$\omega^2$$, so this is a reduction with positive weight; what it gives up is rotation invariance, the preferred frame whose low-energy leakage is discussed on [Where a preferred frame leaks into low-energy physics](/quantum-gravity/lorentz-violation-leakage/).

### Negative weight

Drop positivity but keep the other hypotheses. Let $$\rho$$ be a signed measure of finite total variation on $$[0,\infty)$$ with $$\rho(\{0\})=1$$, so $$G(x)=\int\rho(d\mu)/(x+\mu)$$ has a massless pole of unit residue.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 4** (sum rule). Put $$M=\int\rho$$, so that $$xG(x)\to M$$ as $$x\to\infty$$. If $$M>0$$ then $$F(x)\sim x/M$$ and, whenever $$F\ge0$$ and $$P$$ is finite, $$d_s(\sigma)\to D$$ as $$\sigma\to0$$. If $$M<0$$ then $$F<0$$ at large $$x$$ and $$P=\infty$$ for every $$\sigma$$. Hence a spectral dimension in the ultraviolet that is defined and different from $$D$$ forces $$M=0$$, so that the negative part of $$\rho$$ on $$(0,\infty)$$ has total mass at least one. For $$\rho=\delta_0+\sum_i\alpha_i\delta_{m_i^2}$$ this reads $$\sum_i\alpha_i=-1$$. If $$M=0$$ and $$\int\mu\lvert\rho\rvert<\infty$$, then $$x^2G(x)\to-\int\mu\,\rho(d\mu)$$, and $$d_s=2$$ in $$D=4$$ with $$F\sim\kappa x^2$$ requires $$\int\mu\,\rho(d\mu)=-1/\kappa$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* $$xG(x)=\int\rho(d\mu)\,x/(x+\mu)$$ with $$\lvert x/(x+\mu)\rvert\le1$$ and pointwise limit $$1$$, so $$xG\to M$$ by dominated convergence. If $$M>0$$ then $$F=1/G\sim x/M$$ and Proposition 3 with $$\gamma=1$$ gives the limit; the ultraviolet limit is then $$D$$. If $$M<0$$ then $$G<0$$ for large $$x$$, $$F\to-\infty$$ like $$x/\lvert M\rvert$$, and $$e^{-\sigma F}$$ grows too fast for $$P$$ to converge. So $$M=0$$, and since $$\rho(\{0\})=1$$ contributes $$+1$$, the positive part has mass at least one and so does the negative part. For $$M=0$$, $$x^2G=x\int\rho\,(1-\mu/(x+\mu))=-\int\rho\,\mu x/(x+\mu)\to-\int\mu\rho$$ when $$\int\mu\lvert\rho\rvert<\infty$$.
</div>

The model case is Stelle's $$G=1/x-1/(x+M^2)=M^2/(x(x+M^2))$$ of the third row of the table, with $$F=x(x+M^2)/M^2$$, $$\gamma=2$$ and $$\kappa=1/M^2$$: it has $$\alpha_1=-1$$, $$\sum\alpha_i=-1$$ and $$\int\mu\rho=-M^2$$, consistent with $$d_s=2$$ in the ultraviolet. Prescriptions for the ghost pole that differ only in the Lorentzian continuation, such as the fakeon prescription [[Anselmi–Piva]](#ref-fakeon) (a diffusion equation with fakeons is studied in [[Calcagni]](#ref-calcagni-fakeon)), leave the Euclidean $$G$$, hence $$d_s$$, unchanged. Positivity of $$\rho$$ in (2) and unitarity of the Lorentzian theory are therefore not the same statement, and Theorem 2 does not say that $$d_s<D$$ is incompatible with unitarity.

### Density of states and the diffusion clock

Dropping (H1) or (H2) is a change of the definition of $$P$$. With a measure $$\nu(dx)\propto x^{D_{\rm eff}/2-1}dx$$ in place of the flat one and $$F=x$$, $$d_s=D_{\rm eff}$$. If the diffusion time is replaced by a function $$\sigma\to s(\sigma)$$ of itself, which is what happens when a scale is identified with an inverse diffusion time in a generator that depends on the scale, $$d_s$$ is multiplied by $$d\ln s/d\ln\sigma$$; for $$s=\sigma^{\gamma}$$ the factor is $$\gamma$$. In both cases $$d_s<D$$ is possible with a positive propagator, and in both the content of the statement lies in the measure or the clock, not in the propagator.

A reported reduction $$d_s\to D'<D$$ therefore has to be read together with the propagator, the measure and the clock it refers to. The autonomous, flat-measure, rotation-invariant description is the situation of Theorem 2, and each of the other three items is a place where the description changes.

## 5. Asymptotic safety

In the asymptotic-safety programme, the spectral dimension $$2$$ of Lauscher and Reuter [[LR]](#ref-lr) arises from the formula $$d_s=2D/(2-\eta)$$, with $$\eta$$ the anomalous dimension of the running Newton coupling. This is Proposition 3(a) with $$\gamma=1-\eta/2$$: a propagator $$G\sim x^{-1+\eta/2}$$ at large $$x$$ has $$F\sim x^{1-\eta/2}$$. The fixed-point value $$\eta=2-D$$ gives $$\gamma=D/2$$, that is $$\gamma=2$$ in $$D=4$$, and $$G\sim x^{-2}$$. By Proposition 3(c) a positive-weight propagator with a massless pole has $$\gamma\le1$$, that is $$\eta\ge0$$, so the value $$2$$ cannot be reached by it.

The Lorentzian graviton spectral function has been computed non-perturbatively and is reported positive, with a massless one-graviton peak and a multi-graviton continuum [[FLPR]](#ref-flpr); the self-consistent computation in an on-shell scheme has unit total spectral weight and a close-to-quadratic decay of the continuum [[PRW]](#ref-prw). To the extent that the reported spectral representation of the transverse-traceless fluctuation propagator is the representation (2), with the pole and without a constant term, Theorem 2 applies to it and gives $$d_s\ge4$$ at every scale for that object. With unit total weight, $$M=1$$, so $$F\sim x$$ in the ultraviolet and $$d_s\to4$$ at both ends (Proposition 3). The number $$2$$ of the Euclidean programme therefore does not belong to this propagator taken as the generator of an autonomous diffusion. In the construction of [[LR]](#ref-lr), as we read it, the scale of the effective average action is identified with the inverse diffusion time, so that the effective generator depends on $$\sigma$$; this is the clock route of section 4. The lemma says nothing about the physical meaning of that construction.

Three limits of this reading are worth stating. The identification of a tensor propagator with a scalar function $$G(p^2)$$ is an assumption: the transverse-traceless part has a definite spectral representation, but the spin-zero part and the gauge dependence are not covered. The reported positivity belongs to the computations cited, which we have not reproduced. And the lemma applies to the flat-space propagator; a calculation of the spectral dimension from the background-field propagator at the identification $$k=p$$ within the same flow has not been done here.

## 6. A ghost and a laboratory bound

The sum rule of Proposition 4 places a ghost of weight $$-1$$ at the mass scale below which $$d_s$$ differs from $$D$$. For a single massive ghost with propagator $$G=1/x-1/(x+M^2)$$ the static potential is the Fourier transform of $$1/k^2-1/(k^2+M^2)$$, so the Newtonian potential is multiplied by $$1-e^{-Mr}$$: finite at $$r=0$$, with a Yukawa correction of relative strength $$\alpha=-1$$ and range $$\lambda=1/M$$. A torsion-balance test of the inverse-square law with detector-attractor separations down to $$52\ \mu$$m limits, at $$95\%$$ confidence, gravitational-strength Yukawa interactions to ranges below $$38.6\ \mu$$m [[Lee et al.]](#ref-eotwash). For $$\lvert\alpha\rvert=1$$ this means $$M\ge\hbar c/38.6\ \mu\text{m}=5.11$$ meV (from $$\hbar c=197.327$$ eV nm). A reduction of the spectral dimension carried by one such ghost therefore cannot set in at lengths above $$38.6\ \mu$$m. For example, a ghost at $$M=2.24$$ meV, a scale of the order of the dark-energy density to the power one quarter, has $$\lambda=88\ \mu$$m and is excluded.

This is an empirical statement about a model, not part of the lemma. It assumes a single Yukawa term of strength $$-1$$. With several ghosts the strengths sum to $$-1$$ and each is bounded as a function of its own strength; for the massive spin-two ghost of quadratic gravity the relative strength is $$-4/3$$ [[Stelle 1978]](#ref-stelle78), so the bound on its mass is at least as strong.

## 7. What the lemma does not exclude

Each item lies outside the hypotheses; the condition under which it applies is given.

- Broken rotation invariance, as in Hořava gravity (section 4). The reduction is then a property of an anisotropic symbol with positive weight and requires a preferred frame.
- Negative weight (ghosts, complex poles, form factors without Stieltjes representation). Proposition 4 gives the sum rule for the ultraviolet value, and section 6 the laboratory bound for one ghost. In a Lorentzian quantisation that restores unitarity with the same Euclidean $$G$$, the spectral dimension computed from $$G$$ is unchanged.
- A measure on momentum space other than the flat one, or a diffusion whose generator depends on time. These change what is computed.
- A constant term $$b>0$$ in the propagator. Then $$d_s$$ as defined here is not finite.
- Non-radial generators with jump (Lévy) parts. Proposition 1 gives a criterion, but whether every symmetric Lévy exponent with a nondegenerate Gaussian part has $$d_s\ge D$$ is not proved here.
- Objects other than the two-point function, for instance the spectral dimension of an ensemble of geometries or of a causal-set random walk, which are averages of positive Markov chains and are not governed by a single autonomous generator.

## 8. Open points

The positive-weight bound is for the scalar function $$G(p^2)$$. For a spin-two propagator with several tensor structures and gauge parameters there is no corresponding statement here. The Lévy question above is open. Whether non-perturbative information on the Lorentzian spectral function beyond the transverse-traceless mode changes the conclusion for the fluctuation propagator is not known to us. Whether a clock identification of the kind used for the Euclidean programme can be phrased as a property of a measurable observable is not addressed.

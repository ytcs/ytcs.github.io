Sakharov suggested that the Einstein–Hilbert term need not be an independent term in the action, because it is generated by the vacuum fluctuations of the matter fields once a cutoff is imposed [[Sakharov]](#ref-sakharov) [[Visser]](#ref-visser). If so, Newton's constant would be fixed by the matter content and the cutoff, and a dimensionless number $$G\Lambda^2$$, or $$G/\ell^2$$ for a discreteness length $$\ell$$, would be a prediction. This page asks whether such a number is determined, and answers in three parts. For free Lorentz-invariant fields with a proper-time regulator common to all species, the induced coefficient is $$\kappa_f\Lambda^2c/16\pi^2$$ plus logarithmic mass terms, where $$c$$ is a rational number fixed by the spins and non-minimal couplings and $$\kappa_f$$ is a number that depends on the shape of the regulator; four admissible regulators give values of $$G\Lambda^2$$ that differ by a factor $$1.56$$ (Proposition 1). When the quadratic divergence cancels, $$G$$ is finite and independent of the regulator but is fixed by the mass spectrum, so it is not a pure number in units of a cutoff. The Standard Model alone supplies the wrong sign and a size short by thirty-three to thirty-five orders of magnitude in the finite case, and requires a cutoff above the Planck mass in the cutoff case (section 3). For the entanglement route, in which $$1/4G$$ is read off from the area coefficient of an entropy, the same question on a causal set is whether the area coefficient of a spectral-state entropy is fixed by the substrate. We prove two identities that control this and report numerical results showing that two truncation schemes give different coefficients in $$2+1$$ dimensions (section 4).

The structure of the induced action is known: the species counting is due to Larsen and Wilczek [[LW]](#ref-lw) and Kabat [[Kabat]](#ref-kabat) for the entropy and to Frolov, Fursaev and Zelnikov [[FFZ]](#ref-ffz) for the finite case; the matter combination $$12c=N_S+2N_D-4N_V$$ appears in the asymptotic-safety literature [[DEP]](#ref-dep); the relation between the entropy of a horizon and the renormalisation of $$G$$ goes back to Susskind and Uglum [[SU]](#ref-su) and Jacobson [[Jac94]](#ref-jac94). Sorkin and Yazdi [[SY]](#ref-sy) evaluated Sorkin's spacetime entropy [[Sor12]](#ref-sor12) in the Sorkin–Johnston state [[ASS12]](#ref-ass12) on causal sets and found that truncating the Pauli–Jordan operator restores an area law in $$1+1$$ dimensions; Belenchia, Benincasa, Letizia and Liberati [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll) extended this to $$2+1$$ and $$3+1$$ dimensions. What is added here is the explicit regulator dependence with the Standard Model numbers, an algebraic statement on how a density-graded extraction of the area coefficient treats logarithms, and two identities with numerical tests that show how the area coefficient of a truncated spectral entropy on causal sets depends on the surrounding region. The place of this question among other approaches is mapped on [The landscape of quantum-gravity theories](/quantum-gravity/landscape/).

## 1. The induced Einstein–Hilbert term

*The picture.* Integrating out a free field on a curved background produces an effective action, and the part linear in the scalar curvature $$R$$ has the form of the Einstein–Hilbert term. Its coefficient is a sum over species of a number from the heat kernel, times an integral over the proper time that is regulated in the ultraviolet.

*Setting.* In Euclidean signature a free field with a Laplace-type operator $$\Delta=-\Box-E+m^2$$ contributes to the effective action $$\Gamma=-w\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{s}f(\Lambda^2s)\,\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\Delta}$$, where $$w=\tfrac12$$ for a real boson, $$w=-\tfrac12$$ for a Dirac fermion (with $$\Delta=\mathcal D^2+m^2$$, the square of the Dirac operator $$\mathcal D$$), and $$f$$ is a regulator function common to all species with $$f\to1$$ for $$u=\Lambda^2s\to\infty$$ and $$f(u)=O(u^{1+\eta})$$, $$\eta>0$$, as $$u\to0$$. The small-$$s$$ expansion of the heat kernel on a four-dimensional manifold is $$\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\Delta}=(4\pi s)^{-2}e^{-sm^2}\int\sqrt g\,\big[\mathrm{tr}\,a_0+s\,\mathrm{tr}\,a_1+O(s^2)\big]$$ with $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_0=\dim$$ and $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_1=\mathrm{tr}(\tfrac R6+E)$$ [[Vass]](#ref-vass). Matching the term linear in $$R$$ with $$\Gamma\supset-\frac1{16\pi G}\int\sqrt g\,R$$ gives, for a set of species labelled by $$i$$,

$$
\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\sum_ic_i\,\Lambda^2J\!\Big(\frac{m_i^2}{\Lambda^2}\Big),\qquad J(\epsilon)=\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{u^2}f(u)e^{-\epsilon u},\qquad c_i=w_i\,\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,a_1}{R}.\tag{1}
$$

The number $$c_i$$ is the *species character*. It is computed from the operator of the field:

| field | operator | $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_1/R$$ | $$w$$ | $$c$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real scalar, coupling $$\xi R\phi^2/2$$ | $$-\Box+\xi R$$ | $$\tfrac16-\xi$$ | $$\tfrac12$$ | $$\tfrac12(\tfrac16-\xi)$$ |
| Dirac fermion | $$-\Box+\tfrac R4$$ on 4 components | $$4(\tfrac16-\tfrac14)=-\tfrac13$$ | $$-\tfrac12$$ | $$\tfrac16$$ |
| Weyl fermion | half of Dirac | | | $$\tfrac1{12}$$ |
| Maxwell field | $$-\Box\delta^\mu_\nu+R^\mu{}_\nu$$ on vectors, with ghosts | $$\tfrac46-1=-\tfrac13$$ | $$\tfrac12$$ | $$-\tfrac16$$ from the vector, $$-\tfrac16$$ from the ghosts, total $$-\tfrac13$$ |
| Proca field | Maxwell plus one real scalar with $$\xi=0$$ | | | $$-\tfrac13+\tfrac1{12}=-\tfrac14$$ |

For $$\xi=0$$ these give $$12c=N_S+2N_D-4N_V$$ for $$N_S$$ real scalars, $$N_D$$ Dirac fermions and $$N_V$$ gauge fields. As a check of the heat-kernel coefficients we summed the exact spectra on the four-sphere of unit radius ($$R=12$$, volume $$8\pi^2/3$$). For the scalar Laplacian, with eigenvalues $$l(l+3)$$ and multiplicities $$(2l+3)(l+1)(l+2)/6$$, the combination $$[(4\pi s)^2K(s)/\mathrm{Vol}-1]/s$$ equals $$2.0391,\,2.0195,\,2.0097$$ at $$s=0.02,\,0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$R/6=2$$; with the conformal coupling $$\xi=1/6$$ (eigenvalues $$l(l+3)+2$$) it equals $$-6.7\times10^{-4}$$ and $$-3.3\times10^{-4}$$ at $$s=0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$0$$. For the Dirac operator, with eigenvalues $$\pm(n+2)$$ and multiplicity $$4(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)/6$$ for each sign, the corresponding combination for the squared operator, normalised per spinor component, is $$-0.9982$$ and $$-0.9991$$ at $$s=0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$\tfrac14\mathrm{tr}\,a_1=\tfrac16R-\tfrac14R=-\tfrac1{12}R=-1$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 1** (Induced coefficient with a regulator). Let $$f:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]$$ be measurable, with $$f(u)=O(u^{1+\eta})$$ as $$u\to0$$ for some $$\eta>0$$, $$f(u)\to1$$ as $$u\to\infty$$, and $$\int_1^\infty(1-f(u))\,du/u<\infty$$. Put $$\kappa_f=\int_0^\infty u^{-2}f(u)\,du$$ and $$A_f=\int_0^\infty u^{-1}\big(f(u)-\theta(u-1)\big)du$$. Then as $$\epsilon\to0$$,

$$
J(\epsilon)=\kappa_f+\epsilon\ln\epsilon+C_f\,\epsilon+o(\epsilon),\qquad C_f=\gamma_E-1-A_f,\tag{2}
$$

and consequently, with $$c=\sum_ic_i$$ and $$M_1=\sum_ic_im_i^2$$,

$$
\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\Big[\kappa_f\,c\,\Lambda^2-\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln\frac{\Lambda^2}{m_i^2}+C_fM_1\Big]+o(1)\qquad(\Lambda\to\infty).\tag{3}
$$

(i) If $$c\neq0$$, then $$G\Lambda^2=\pi/(\kappa_fc)$$ to leading order, which depends on $$f$$ through $$\kappa_f$$; only the sign and the zero set of $$c$$ are independent of $$f$$ for $$\kappa_f>0$$.

(ii) If $$c=0$$ and $$M_1=0$$, then $$\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln m_i^2+o(1)$$, finite and independent of $$f$$ and of $$\Lambda$$.
</div>

The idea is that $$J(\epsilon)$$ has a universal non-analytic term $$\epsilon\ln\epsilon$$, with coefficient $$1$$, and that everything else, namely the value $$\kappa_f$$ at $$\epsilon=0$$ and the coefficient of the term linear in $$\epsilon$$, depends on the shape of $$f$$. The calculation is routine.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The integral $$\kappa_f$$ converges at $$u\to0$$ by the assumption on $$f$$ and at infinity because $$f\le1$$. Differentiating, $$J'(\epsilon)=-\int_0^\infty u^{-1}f(u)e^{-\epsilon u}du$$. Write $$f=\theta(u-1)+(f-\theta(u-1))$$. The first part gives $$-E_1(\epsilon)=\ln\epsilon+\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)$$. The second part has an integrable integrand $$(f-\theta(u-1))/u$$, by the assumptions at $$u\to0$$ and $$u\to\infty$$, so it tends to $$-A_f$$ by dominated convergence. Hence $$J'(\epsilon)=\ln\epsilon+\gamma_E-A_f+o(1)$$, and integrating from $$0$$, where $$J(0)=\kappa_f$$, gives $$J(\epsilon)=\kappa_f+\epsilon\ln\epsilon+(\gamma_E-A_f-1)\epsilon+o(\epsilon)$$. Inserting (2) in (1) with $$\epsilon_i=m_i^2/\Lambda^2$$ gives (3). Part (i) is the leading term of (3), $$1/16\pi G=\kappa_fc\Lambda^2/16\pi^2$$. For part (ii), if $$c=0$$ and $$M_1=0$$ the terms $$\kappa_fc\Lambda^2$$ and $$C_fM_1$$ vanish and $$-\sum c_im_i^2\ln(\Lambda^2/m_i^2)=\sum c_im_i^2\ln m_i^2-\ln\Lambda^2\,M_1$$ with the last term zero. $$\square$$
</div>

*Check.* Four regulators satisfy the assumptions: the sharp cutoff $$f=\theta(u-1)$$, with $$\kappa_f=1$$; $$f=e^{-1/u}$$, with $$\kappa_f=1$$; $$f=e^{-1/u^2}$$, with $$\kappa_f=\sqrt\pi/2=0.8862$$; and $$f=(1-e^{-u})^2$$, with $$\kappa_f=2\ln2=1.3863$$. Numerical quadrature reproduces these values to six digits, and for each of them the difference $$(J(\epsilon)-\kappa_f-\epsilon\ln\epsilon-C_f\epsilon)/\epsilon$$ is below $$0.03$$ in absolute value at $$\epsilon=10^{-2}$$ and below $$5\times10^{-4}$$ at $$\epsilon=10^{-4}$$, with $$C_f=-0.4228,\,0.1544,\,-0.1342,\,-0.3069$$. The ratio of the largest to the smallest $$\kappa_f$$ is $$1.564$$, which is the factor by which $$G\Lambda^2$$ varies.

The $$f$$-dependence in (i) is, in part, a statement about conventions: rescaling $$\Lambda^2\to\kappa_f\Lambda^2$$ turns every such regulator into one with $$\kappa_f=1$$. What is not a convention is that a number such as $$G\Lambda^2$$ requires a prescription that defines $$\Lambda$$ independently of $$G$$, for example the mass of a Pauli–Villars regulator field or a lattice spacing, and that different prescriptions give different numbers. In case (ii) there is no cutoff to define, and $$G$$ is a function of the mass spectrum alone. The vanishing of $$c$$ and $$M_1$$ is the sum-rule mechanism of Frolov, Fursaev and Zelnikov [[FFZ]](#ref-ffz). It makes $$G\,m_*^2$$ a function of the ratios of masses, so that $$G$$ is set by the overall mass scale $$m_*$$ of the species in the sum and is not a pure number in units of a cutoff.

## 2. Counting species

With a universal cutoff the value of $$c$$ decides the sign of $$1/G$$. For the Standard Model with 45 Weyl fermions, four real Higgs components (the Higgs doublet, which includes the three would-be Goldstone modes of the massive vectors) and twelve gauge fields, $$c=2(\tfrac16-\xi)+\tfrac{45}{12}-4$$, which is $$\tfrac1{12}$$ at $$\xi=0$$ and $$-\tfrac14$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$. With three right-handed neutrinos, 48 Weyl fermions, $$c=\tfrac13$$ at $$\xi=0$$ and $$c=0$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$: the four conformally coupled scalars contribute $$0$$, the fermions $$4$$ and the gauge fields $$-4$$. This vanishing is arithmetic; no mechanism that keeps it at higher order is claimed here. The sign of $$c$$ is the sign of $$1/G$$ in the cutoff case, so a matter content with $$c<0$$ gives a negative induced coefficient.

## 3. The Standard Model as the only source

*The cutoff case.* With $$c=\tfrac13$$ (Standard Model with three right-handed neutrinos, $$\xi=0$$) or $$c=\tfrac1{12}$$ (Standard Model alone), the leading term of (3) matches the observed value $$1/16\pi G=M_{\rm P}^2/16\pi=2.97\times10^{36}$$ GeV$$^2$$, with $$M_{\rm P}=G^{-1/2}=1.221\times10^{19}$$ GeV, when $$\Lambda=\sqrt{\pi/(\kappa_fc)}\,M_{\rm P}$$. The four regulators give:

| regulator | $$\kappa_f$$ | $$\Lambda/M_{\rm P}$$ for $$c=\tfrac13$$ | $$\Lambda/M_{\rm P}$$ for $$c=\tfrac1{12}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| sharp, $$e^{-1/u}$$ | $$1$$ | $$3.07$$ | $$6.14$$ |
| $$e^{-1/u^2}$$ | $$0.886$$ | $$3.26$$ | $$6.52$$ |
| $$(1-e^{-u})^2$$ | $$1.386$$ | $$2.61$$ | $$5.22$$ |

The required cutoff is above the Planck mass for every regulator, and its value is set by the regulator.

*The finite case.* If $$c=0$$ (with $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ and three right-handed neutrinos) the cutoff term is absent and (3) gives $$\frac1{16\pi G}=-\frac1{16\pi^2}\big[M_1\ln\Lambda^2-\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln m_i^2-C_fM_1\big]$$, where $$M_1=\sum c_im_i^2$$ does not vanish; Proposition 1(ii) therefore does not apply, and $$G$$ depends on $$\Lambda$$ logarithmically. The Higgs, being conformally coupled, contributes $$0$$. The sum runs over the massive fermions and the massive vectors, counted as Proca fields. With rounded masses (top $$172.6$$, bottom $$4.18$$, charm $$1.27$$, strange $$0.093$$, down $$0.0047$$, up $$0.0022$$, tau $$1.777$$, muon $$0.1057$$, electron $$0.000511$$, $$W$$ $$80.38$$, $$Z$$ $$91.19$$ GeV, neutrinos massless), with $$c=\tfrac12$$ for each coloured quark ($$N_c/6$$), $$\tfrac16$$ for each charged lepton, $$-\tfrac12$$ for the $$W^\pm$$ pair and $$-\tfrac14$$ for the $$Z$$,

$$
M_1=9.6\times10^3\ \mathrm{GeV}^2,\tag{4}
$$

dominated by the top quark and reduced by the vectors. The logarithmic term of (3) then has the value $$1/16\pi G=-166,\,-446,\,-2.1\times10^3,\,-4.7\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$ for $$\Lambda=10^3,\,10^4,\,10^{10},\,1.2\times10^{19}$$ GeV. This is negative, and smaller in modulus than the observed value by $$5.6\times10^{-35}$$, $$1.5\times10^{-34}$$, $$7.2\times10^{-34}$$ and $$1.6\times10^{-33}$$. The regulator-dependent constant $$C_fM_1/16\pi^2$$ is at most $$26$$ GeV$$^2$$ in modulus for the four regulators of section 1, which is $$16\%$$ of the value at $$\Lambda=1$$ TeV and negligible at the larger cutoffs. These are one-loop evaluations with the structure of Proposition 1 and rounded masses; they assume free fields with the masses of the broken phase and the species character of section 1.

In both cases the Standard Model fixes $$1/G$$ only through a cutoff placed above the Planck mass by hand, or not at all. A sector that supplies the observed $$1/G$$ in the finite case needs $$\sum c_im_i^2\ln(\ldots)\sim16\pi^2M_{\rm P}^2/16\pi$$, so masses of order $$10^{18}$$ to $$10^{19}$$ GeV; $$G$$ is then set by whatever is heaviest.

## 4. The area coefficient of a causal-set entropy

*The question.* In the entanglement picture the coefficient of $$A/4G$$ in the entropy of a horizon is renormalised by the same fluctuations that renormalise $$1/G$$ [[SU]](#ref-su) [[Jac94]](#ref-jac94) [[LW]](#ref-lw), and for a minimally coupled scalar the divergent area term of the entanglement entropy equals $$A/4G$$ with the induced $$G$$ [[SU]](#ref-su) [[LW]](#ref-lw). On a causal set the density $$\rho$$ supplies the cutoff. If the area coefficient of the entropy of a region is a fixed number times $$\rho^{(d-2)/d}A$$, this would define $$G$$ in units of the discreteness scale. We examine whether that coefficient is fixed.

*The entropy.* For a free scalar on a sprinkled causal set let $$K$$ be the retarded Green function, a matrix supported on related pairs, and $$i\Delta=i(K-K^T)$$ the Pauli–Jordan operator, Hermitian and imaginary. The Sorkin–Johnston state has two-point function $$W$$ equal to the positive part of $$i\Delta$$. For a region $$R$$ with restrictions $$W_R$$ and $$(i\Delta)_R$$, the spacetime entanglement entropy of Sorkin [[Sor12]](#ref-sor12) is $$S=\sum_\lambda\lambda\ln\lvert\lambda\rvert$$ over the generalised eigenvalues $$\lambda$$ of $$W_Rv=\lambda\,(i\Delta)_Rv$$, restricted to the range of $$(i\Delta)_R$$. For a single mode with symplectic eigenvalue $$\nu\ge\tfrac12$$ the pair $$\lambda=\tfrac12\pm\nu$$ gives $$(\nu+\tfrac12)\ln(\nu+\tfrac12)-(\nu-\tfrac12)\ln(\nu-\tfrac12)$$, the Bose–Einstein entropy, which is why the formula is the entropy of the Gaussian state. The untruncated Sorkin–Johnston entropy obeys a volume law in $$1+1$$ dimensions [[SY]](#ref-sy); an area law is recovered if the modes of $$i\Delta$$ with eigenvalues below a threshold are projected out of $$W$$ and $$i\Delta$$, once for the whole causal set and once for the region (the double truncation of [[SY]](#ref-sy) [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll)). Different rules for the thresholds are different schemes.

### 4.1 Logarithms and the extraction of the area coefficient

Let $$F(\rho)$$ be the mean over sprinklings of density $$\rho$$ of an observable of a fixed region, and let $$\theta=\rho\,\partial_\rho$$. A thinning of a sprinkling is a sprinkling of lower density, so $$\theta$$ acts on $$F$$ as the generator of density dilations, and the powers $$\rho^\beta$$ are its eigenvectors. A power times a logarithm, $$\rho^\beta\ln\rho$$, is not an eigenvector but a generalised one, and $$(\theta-\beta)$$ maps it to $$\rho^\beta$$. The coefficient of the area term of an entropy of a $$(d-1)$$-dimensional boundary is the component of $$F$$ at the exponent $$\beta_A=(d-2)/d$$. It is extracted by a function $$P(\theta)$$ of $$\theta$$ that vanishes on the exponents of the other terms and equals $$1$$ at $$\beta_A$$ (the extraction of powers of the density from combinations of thinned counts is treated on the page [Horizon counts: which weights give the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/)).

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 2** (Extraction of a coefficient in the presence of logarithms). Let $$Z$$ be a finite set of exponents with $$\beta_A\notin Z$$, and let

$$
F(\rho)=\sum_{\beta\in Z}\rho^\beta(a_\beta+b_\beta\ln\rho)+\rho^{\beta_A}(b\ln\rho+e).
$$

Let $$P$$ be an entire function with $$P(\beta)=0$$ for $$\beta\in Z$$ and $$P(\beta_A)=1$$. Then

$$
P(\theta)F=\sum_{\beta\in Z}P'(\beta)\,b_\beta\,\rho^\beta+\rho^{\beta_A}\big(b\ln\rho+e+P'(\beta_A)\,b\big).\tag{5}
$$

Consequently: (a) $$P(\theta)$$ removes a term $$\rho^\beta\ln\rho$$, $$\beta\in Z$$, if and only if $$P'(\beta)=0$$ as well, that is $$\beta$$ is a double zero of $$P$$; (b) the coefficient $$b$$ of $$\rho^{\beta_A}\ln\rho$$ in $$P(\theta)F$$ is the same for every admissible $$P$$; (c) the constant $$e$$ of the area term shifts by $$P'(\beta_A)\,b$$ and is therefore determined only up to the choice of $$P$$ when $$b\neq0$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* $$\theta\rho^\beta=\beta\rho^\beta$$, so $$P(\theta)\rho^\beta=P(\beta)\rho^\beta$$. Because $$\rho^\beta\ln\rho=\partial_\beta\rho^\beta$$ and $$\theta$$ commutes with $$\partial_\beta$$, $$P(\theta)\rho^\beta\ln\rho=\partial_\beta\big[P(\beta)\rho^\beta\big]=P'(\beta)\rho^\beta+P(\beta)\rho^\beta\ln\rho$$. Insert $$P(\beta)=0$$ on $$Z$$ and $$P(\beta_A)=1$$ to obtain (5). Statements (a) to (c) read off the coefficients; for (c), replacing $$P(\beta)$$ by $$P(\beta)(1+\lambda(\beta-\beta_A))$$ keeps the admissibility conditions and changes $$P'(\beta_A)$$ by $$\lambda$$. $$\square$$
</div>

The identity was also checked symbolically (the residual of (5) is zero).

<div class="corollary" markdown="1">
**Corollary 3** (Two dimensions). In $$d=2$$ the area exponent is $$\beta_A=0$$: the boundary of a region is a set of points. Suppose each of $$N_{\rm sp}$$ massless scalars contributes $$\tfrac{c_{\rm cft}}{12}\ln\rho$$ to the entropy per boundary point, with $$c_{\rm cft}=1$$, as the entropy $$\tfrac{c_{\rm cft}}3\ln(L/\varepsilon)$$ of an interval of length $$L$$ in a conformal field theory does for a short-distance cutoff $$\varepsilon\propto\rho^{-1/2}$$. Define $$1/4G_{\rm eff}$$ as the area-degree component of the entropy per boundary point. Then $$1/4G_{\rm eff}=a_g+\tfrac{N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}}{12}\ln(\rho\,\ell_{\rm IR}^2)$$ for an infrared scale $$\ell_{\rm IR}$$ and a constant $$a_g$$, and the slope $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}/12$$ is the same for every admissible $$P$$. Newton's constant in two dimensions runs with the density, and there is no density-independent induced $$G$$ unless $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}=0$$.
</div>

This is a derivation under a stated physical input, not a theorem: the input is the form of the entropy of the sprinkled theory. The proof of the consequence is Proposition 2(b): a term in $$\rho^0\ln\rho$$ sits at the area exponent and cannot be removed by any $$P$$.

*Numerical check of the input in $$1+1$$ dimensions.* We computed the Sorkin spacetime entropy of the causal diamond $$\lvert t\rvert+\lvert x\rvert<\tfrac12$$ inside the diamond $$\lvert t\rvert+\lvert x\rvert<1$$, with the massless retarded function $$K=\tfrac12$$ on related pairs, for nested sprinklings with $$N=250,354,500,707,1000,1414,2000$$ points (the same points thinned), with two truncation schemes: the keep-the-largest-modes scheme A, which retains the $$k\sqrt N$$ positive modes globally and $$k\sqrt n$$ in the region of $$n$$ points, and the absolute-threshold scheme C, which applies to the region the threshold equal to the smallest retained global eigenvalue. The slope $$dS/d\ln N$$ expected from $$c_{\rm cft}=1$$ and two endpoints is $$\tfrac16=0.167$$. The fitted slopes (mean over six seeds of the slope of each seed, with the standard error) are $$0.151\pm0.016$$ for A and $$0.153\pm0.016$$ for C with the knee factor $$k=0.5$$, and $$0.159\pm0.019$$ and $$0.183\pm0.017$$ with $$k=1$$. With $$k=2$$ scheme A retains so many modes that the entropy no longer grows ($$0.011\pm0.029$$), while C gives $$0.205\pm0.032$$. The constant terms depend on the scheme: the mean entropies at $$N=2000$$ are $$0.92$$ and $$0.66$$ for $$k=0.5$$, $$1.29$$ and $$1.00$$ for $$k=1$$, and $$2.13$$ and $$1.66$$ for $$k=2$$ (A and C). The logarithmic coefficient is therefore reproduced within the errors where the truncation is not too weak, and the constants are scheme dependent, 

### 4.2 Dilation identities in $$2+1$$ dimensions

Consider a sprinkling in a causal diamond $$D(L)=\{\lvert t\rvert+r<L\}$$ in $$2+1$$ dimensions and the concentric sub-diamond $$R_\ell=\{\lvert t\rvert+r<\ell\}$$, whose edge is the circle of length $$2\pi\ell$$. An area law would read $$\langle S\rangle=2\pi s_1\,\ell\,\rho^{1/3}+c_0$$ with $$s_1$$ a property of the substrate. Assume a scheme that depends on the order, on the numbers $$N$$ and $$n$$ of points in the diamond and in the region, and on nothing else; all the schemes below are of this type.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 4** (Dilation invariance and the outer-diamond identity). Under this assumption $$\langle S\rangle=F(x,y)$$ with $$x=\ell\rho^{1/3}$$ and $$y=\ell/L$$. Define $$s_1^\ell=(2\pi\rho^{1/3})^{-1}\partial_\ell\langle S\rangle\vert_{\rho,L}$$ and $$s_1^\rho=(2\pi\ell)^{-1}\partial_{\rho^{1/3}}\langle S\rangle\vert_{\ell,L}$$. Then

$$
s_1^\ell-s_1^\rho=-\frac1{2\pi\,\ell\,\rho^{1/3}}\;\frac{\partial\langle S\rangle}{\partial\ln L}\bigg\vert_{\ell,\rho}.\tag{6}
$$

</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The dilation $$x\mapsto\lambda x$$ maps a sprinkling of density $$\rho$$ in $$D(L)$$ to one of density $$\lambda^{-3}\rho$$ in $$D(\lambda L)$$, the sub-diamond $$R_\ell$$ to $$R_{\lambda\ell}$$, and preserves the order, hence the retarded function up to a constant factor and the numbers $$N,n$$. The matrices $$W$$ and $$i\Delta$$ scale together, the generalised eigenvalues do not change, and $$\langle S\rangle$$ is invariant under $$(\ell,L,\rho)\to(\lambda\ell,\lambda L,\lambda^{-3}\rho)$$. The invariants are $$x$$ and $$y$$. Now $$\partial_\ell\langle S\rangle=\rho^{1/3}F_x+F_y/L$$, $$\partial_{\rho^{1/3}}\langle S\rangle=\ell F_x$$ and $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L=-yF_y$$. Hence $$2\pi(s_1^\ell-s_1^\rho)=F_y/(L\rho^{1/3})=-\partial_{\ln L}\langle S\rangle/(\ell\rho^{1/3})$$. $$\square$$
</div>

So the area coefficient read from the dependence on the size of the region ($$s_1^\ell$$) equals the one read from the dependence on the density ($$s_1^\rho$$) exactly when the entropy of a fixed region does not depend on the diamond that contains it. A stronger statement holds for a decomposition of the entropy into an area term and a term that does not depend on the region.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 5** (Area-plus-background decomposition). Under the assumption above, the following are equivalent: (a) $$\langle S\rangle=2\pi s_1\,\ell\rho^{1/3}+h(L,\rho)$$ for all $$(\ell,L,\rho)$$ with $$s_1$$ constant; (b) $$F(x,y)=2\pi s_1x+g(x/y)$$ for a function $$g$$. In that case $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L\vert_{\ell,\rho}=L\rho^{1/3}g'(L\rho^{1/3})$$ does not depend on $$\ell$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* At fixed $$(L,\rho)$$, varying $$\ell$$ moves $$(x,y)$$ along the ray $$x/y=L\rho^{1/3}$$. Statement (a) says that $$F-2\pi s_1x$$ is independent of $$\ell$$ at fixed $$(L,\rho)$$, that is constant on each ray, which is (b), with $$h(L,\rho)=g(L\rho^{1/3})$$. Differentiating $$h$$ with respect to $$\ln L$$ at fixed $$\rho$$ gives the last statement. $$\square$$
</div>

### 4.3 Numerical results in $$2+1$$ dimensions

*Method.* We took sprinklings in $$D(1)$$ with $$\rho=3N/2\pi$$ and computed $$\langle S\rangle$$ for the concentric sub-diamonds $$\ell=0.30,0.375,0.45,0.525,0.60$$ with the kernel $$K_{ij}=C_{ij}(n_{ij}+1)^{-1/3}$$ on related pairs, where $$n_{ij}$$ is the number of elements between $$i$$ and $$j$$ (the discretised retarded function of [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll)). The overall constant drops out of $$S$$. Three schemes: U, the untruncated state; A, the global truncation to $$k_N=\mathrm{round}(N^{2/3})$$ positive modes of $$i\Delta$$ (and their negative partners) followed by a truncation of the restricted operator to $$\mathrm{round}(n^{2/3})$$ positive modes; C, the absolute threshold $$\sigma^*$$ equal to the $$k_N$$-th positive eigenvalue of the global $$i\Delta$$, applied to the restricted operator. The coefficient $$s_1^\ell$$ is the slope of the least-squares line through the five values of $$\langle S\rangle$$ against $$2\pi\ell\rho^{1/3}$$. Six sprinklings of $$2000$$ points and the same six thinned to $$1414$$ points were used; the means and standard errors are:

| scheme | $$s_1^\ell$$, $$N=1414$$ | $$s_1^\ell$$, $$N=2000$$ |
|---|---|---|
| U | $$7.57\pm0.13$$ | $$9.39\pm0.10$$ |
| A | $$0.068\pm0.017$$ | $$0.063\pm0.017$$ |
| C | $$0.168\pm0.007$$ | $$0.188\pm0.004$$ |

The untruncated entropy is of volume type: its mean for $$N=1414$$ is $$26,56,87,118,151$$ for the five values of $$\ell$$, about $$0.5$$ to $$0.7$$ per point of the region, and the fitted $$s_1$$ is meaningless as an area coefficient. The two truncated schemes differ by a factor $$2.8$$ at $$N=1414$$ and $$3.0$$ at $$N=2000$$, and the standard errors are too small to attribute this to noise. In scheme A the mean entropy $$9.8,10.9,11.5,11.4,11.0$$ at $$N=2000$$ rises and saturates with $$\ell$$, so a straight line in $$\ell$$ is not a good description; scheme C is closer to linear ($$0.8,1.2,2.1,3.2,4.2$$).

*Density direction.* From the paired values at $$N=2000$$ and $$N=1414$$, $$\rho^{1/3}=9.85$$ and $$8.77$$, the coefficient read from the density dependence at fixed $$\ell$$, by a least-squares fit through the origin of $$\Delta S$$ against $$2\pi\ell\,\Delta\rho^{1/3}$$, is $$s_1^\rho=0.461\pm0.020$$ for A and $$0.160\pm0.020$$ for C. For A the two directions disagree by a factor seven; for C they agree within the error.

*Outer-diamond dependence.* To test (6) directly we sprinkled $$D(1.26)$$ at the density of $$N\approx1414$$ points in $$D(1)$$, so that $$D(1.26)$$ contains about twice as many points, and computed the entropy of the same sub-diamond as a region of $$D(1)$$ and of $$D(1.26)$$ (four sprinklings; $$N=1378$$ to $$1493$$ and $$2759$$ to $$2896$$). The finite-difference derivative $$\Delta\langle S\rangle/\ln1.26$$ is:

| scheme | $$\ell=0.30$$ | $$\ell=0.45$$ | $$\ell=0.60$$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $$2.7\pm0.6$$ | $$7.9\pm0.4$$ | $$15.6\pm0.3$$ |
| C | $$-1.7\pm1.1$$ | $$-1.2\pm0.2$$ | $$-2.4\pm0.3$$ |

The entropy of a fixed region depends on the enclosing diamond by several nats in both schemes. By Proposition 5, the dependence on $$\ell$$ of this derivative shows that the decomposition into an area term and a term independent of $$\ell$$ fails for A; for C the dependence on $$\ell$$ is weaker but the variation between $$\ell=0.45$$ and $$0.60$$ is about four standard errors. Equation (6) is satisfied roughly: for A at $$\ell=0.45$$ the left side is $$0.068-0.461=-0.39$$ and the right side is $$-7.9/(2\pi\cdot0.45\cdot8.77)=-0.32$$; for C the left side is $$0.008\pm0.02$$ and the right side $$+0.05$$. The two sides use different averaging in $$\ell$$ and finite differences, which is why we compare them only in sign and size.

These results are for one kernel, the scheme definitions above, up to $$2900$$ points, and regions of boundary length $$2\pi\ell\rho^{1/3}=16$$ to $$37$$ in units of the discreteness length. They show that in these schemes the coefficient $$s_1$$ of an area law is not a property of the sprinkling alone; they do not show that no scheme exists for which it is.

## 5. What the results do not exclude

* Finite induced gravity with a heavy sector. If $$c=M_1=0$$ for a spectrum with positive norms and masses of order $$10^{18}$$ to $$10^{19}$$ GeV, $$G$$ is finite and regulator independent (Proposition 1(ii)); it is then a function of mass ratios and of one overall mass, which is a modulus.
* Entanglement-based definitions in which the ultraviolet entanglement density is taken to equal $$1/4G$$ as an input [[Jac15]](#ref-jac15) [[BM]](#ref-bm). Newton's constant is then a parameter of the construction.
* Truncation schemes or states other than those tested. A state defined by local Hadamard conditions or by a different regularisation of the Pauli–Jordan operator might give an area coefficient independent of the diamond; Propositions 4 and 5 give the criterion.
* The density-graded extraction of Proposition 2 applied to the untruncated entropy. If the volume term of the untruncated state is an eigenvector at the exponent $$1$$ with no accompanying logarithm, a suitable $$P$$ removes it in the mean; this has not been tested here, and the variance of the untruncated entropy is not controlled by the mean.
* Corollary 3 does not apply if the entropy of the sprinkled theory has no logarithm in two dimensions, or if $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}=0$$.
* The Standard Model plus new heavy states: Proposition 1 and section 3 concern the Standard Model content alone.
* Regulators built from Pauli–Villars fields, which carry an indefinite spectral weight: the state-based entropy of such a regulated theory is the subject of [Entropy of states in indefinite-metric theories](/quantum-gravity/indefinite-metric-entropy/).

## 6. Open points

Whether the zero of $$c$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ for the Standard Model with three right-handed neutrinos survives at two loops, where $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ is no longer protected, would decide whether it is structural or accidental. For the causal-set entropy, the region-size dependence of the truncated entropy has been tested for two schemes in $$2+1$$ dimensions at a few thousand points; the trend with $$N$$ of the discrepancy between the schemes, which decreases only slowly with the sizes used, is not settled. Whether a scheme exists for which $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L\to0$$ as $$L\rho^{1/3}\to\infty$$ for a fixed region is not known. The form assumed for the entropy in Corollary 3 has been checked here numerically in $$1+1$$ dimensions only to the accuracy stated.

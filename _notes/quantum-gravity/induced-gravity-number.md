Sakharov proposed that the Einstein–Hilbert term need not be an independent term in the action, because it is generated by the vacuum fluctuations of the matter fields once a cutoff is imposed [[Sakharov]](#ref-sakharov) [[Visser]](#ref-visser). If so, the dimensionless number $$G\Lambda^2$$, with $$\Lambda$$ the cutoff, would be fixed by the matter content. For free Lorentz-invariant fields with a proper-time regulator common to all species the leading term gives $$G\Lambda^2=\pi/(\kappa_fc)$$, where $$c$$ is a rational number fixed by the spins and non-minimal couplings and $$\kappa_f$$ depends on the shape of the regulator; four admissible regulators differ by a factor $$1.56$$ (Proposition 1), so the number has content only relative to a prescription that defines $$\Lambda$$. For the Standard Model content $$c=\tfrac1{12}-2\xi$$, with $$\xi$$ the non-minimal coupling of the Higgs doublet, and the cutoff that reproduces the observed $$G$$ lies above the Planck mass unless $$\xi$$ is below about $$-1.1$$ (section 3). If the couplings are chosen so that $$c=0$$, $$G$$ depends logarithmically on $$\Lambda$$ unless also $$\sum_ic_im_i^2=0$$, which the Standard Model spectrum does not satisfy; its induced $$1/G$$ is then negative and smaller in modulus than the observed value by a factor between $$10^{-33}$$ and $$10^{-35}$$. Section 4 asks the corresponding question for the entropy route, in which $$1/4G$$ is read off from the area coefficient of an entropy: whether the area coefficient of a spectral-state entropy on a causal set is fixed by the sprinkling. We prove two identities that control its extraction, with a conditional result on the running of $$G$$ in two dimensions, and report tests in $$2+1$$ dimensions. The entropy of the mode-count truncation saturates with the size of the region and depends strongly on the enclosing diamond, so it has no area-plus-background form; the threshold truncation has one, with an area coefficient of about $$0.19$$ read from the size of the region and $$0.14$$ read from the density, and a dependence on the enclosing diamond that does not vary with the region.

The species counting is due to Larsen and Wilczek [[LW]](#ref-lw) and Kabat [[Kabat]](#ref-kabat) for the entropy, and the combination $$12c=N_S+2N_D-4N_V$$ appears in the asymptotic-safety literature [[DEP]](#ref-dep). Frolov, Fursaev and Zelnikov [[FFZ]](#ref-ffz) showed that in a model with heavy constituents the induced constant is fixed by their masses and related it to the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy; the relation between the entropy of a horizon and the renormalisation of $$G$$ goes back to Susskind and Uglum [[SU]](#ref-su) and Jacobson [[Jac94]](#ref-jac94). Sorkin and Yazdi [[SY]](#ref-sy) evaluated Sorkin's spacetime entropy [[Sor12]](#ref-sor12) in the Sorkin–Johnston state [[ASS12]](#ref-ass12) on causal sets and found that truncating the Pauli–Jordan operator restores an area law in $$1+1$$ dimensions; Belenchia, Benincasa, Letizia and Liberati [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll) extended this to $$2+1$$ and $$3+1$$ dimensions. What is added here is the explicit regulator dependence with Standard Model numbers, a statement on how logarithms enter the density-graded extraction of an area coefficient, and two identities with numerical tests of how the area coefficient of a truncated spectral entropy depends on the surrounding region. Other approaches to the same question are surveyed on the page [on quantum-gravity approaches](/quantum-gravity/landscape/).

## 1. The induced Einstein–Hilbert term

Integrating out a free field on a curved background gives an effective action whose part linear in the scalar curvature $$R$$ has the form of the Einstein–Hilbert term. Its coefficient is a sum over species of a heat-kernel number times a proper-time integral that is cut off in the ultraviolet. In Euclidean signature a free field with a Laplace-type operator $$\Delta=-\Box-E+m^2$$ contributes $$\Gamma=-w\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{s}f(\Lambda^2s)\,\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\Delta}$$ to the effective action, where $$w=\tfrac12$$ for a real boson, $$w=-\tfrac12$$ for a Dirac fermion (with $$\Delta=\mathcal D^2+m^2$$, the square of the Dirac operator $$\mathcal D$$), and $$f$$ is a regulator function common to all species with $$f\to1$$ for $$u=\Lambda^2s\to\infty$$ and $$f(u)=O(u^{1+\eta})$$, $$\eta>0$$, as $$u\to0$$. The small-$$s$$ expansion of the heat kernel on a four-dimensional manifold is $$\mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-s\Delta}=(4\pi s)^{-2}e^{-sm^2}\int\sqrt g\,\big[\mathrm{tr}\,a_0+s\,\mathrm{tr}\,a_1+O(s^2)\big]$$ with $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_0=\dim$$ and $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_1=\mathrm{tr}(\tfrac R6+E)$$ [[Vass]](#ref-vass). Matching the term linear in $$R$$ with $$\Gamma\supset-\frac1{16\pi G}\int\sqrt g\,R$$ gives, for a set of species labelled by $$i$$,

$$
\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\sum_ic_i\,\Lambda^2J\!\Big(\frac{m_i^2}{\Lambda^2}\Big),\qquad J(\epsilon)=\int_0^\infty\frac{du}{u^2}f(u)e^{-\epsilon u},\qquad c_i=w_i\,\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,a_1}{R}.\tag{1}
$$

The number $$c_i$$, which we call the species character, is computed from the operator of the field:

| field | operator | $$\mathrm{tr}\,a_1/R$$ | $$w$$ | $$c$$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real scalar, coupling $$\xi R\phi^2/2$$ | $$-\Box+\xi R$$ | $$\tfrac16-\xi$$ | $$\tfrac12$$ | $$\tfrac12(\tfrac16-\xi)$$ |
| Dirac fermion | $$-\Box+\tfrac R4$$ on 4 components | $$4(\tfrac16-\tfrac14)=-\tfrac13$$ | $$-\tfrac12$$ | $$\tfrac16$$ |
| Weyl fermion | half of Dirac | | | $$\tfrac1{12}$$ |
| Maxwell field | $$-\Box\delta^\mu_\nu+R^\mu{}_\nu$$ on vectors, with ghosts | $$\tfrac46-1=-\tfrac13$$ | $$\tfrac12$$ | $$-\tfrac16$$ from the vector, $$-\tfrac16$$ from the ghosts, total $$-\tfrac13$$ |
| Proca field | Maxwell plus one real scalar with $$\xi=0$$ | | | $$-\tfrac13+\tfrac1{12}=-\tfrac14$$ |

For $$\xi=0$$ these give $$12c=N_S+2N_D-4N_V$$ for $$N_S$$ real scalars, $$N_D$$ Dirac fermions and $$N_V$$ gauge fields. The heat-kernel coefficients can be tested on the four-sphere of unit radius ($$R=12$$, volume $$8\pi^2/3$$), where the spectra are known. For the scalar Laplacian, with eigenvalues $$l(l+3)$$ and multiplicities $$(2l+3)(l+1)(l+2)/6$$, the combination $$[(4\pi s)^2K(s)/\mathrm{Vol}-1]/s$$ of the summed heat kernel $$K(s)$$ equals $$2.0391,\,2.0195,\,2.0097$$ at $$s=0.02,\,0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$R/6=2$$; with the conformal coupling $$\xi=1/6$$ (eigenvalues $$l(l+3)+2$$) it equals $$-6.7\times10^{-4}$$ and $$-3.3\times10^{-4}$$ at $$s=0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$0$$. For the squared Dirac operator, with eigenvalues $$\pm(n+2)$$ and multiplicity $$4(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)/6$$ for each sign, the corresponding combination per spinor component is $$-0.9982$$ and $$-0.9991$$ at $$s=0.01,\,0.005$$, approaching $$\tfrac14\mathrm{tr}\,a_1=\tfrac16R-\tfrac14R=-\tfrac1{12}R=-1$$.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 1** (Induced coefficient with a regulator). Let $$f:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]$$ be measurable, with $$f(u)=O(u^{1+\eta})$$ as $$u\to0$$ for some $$\eta>0$$, $$f(u)\to1$$ as $$u\to\infty$$, and $$\int_1^\infty(1-f(u))\,du/u<\infty$$. Put $$\kappa_f=\int_0^\infty u^{-2}f(u)\,du$$ and $$A_f=\int_0^\infty u^{-1}\big(f(u)-\theta(u-1)\big)du$$. Then as $$\epsilon\to0$$,

$$
J(\epsilon)=\kappa_f+\epsilon\ln\epsilon+C_f\,\epsilon+o(\epsilon),\qquad C_f=\gamma_E-1-A_f,\tag{2}
$$

and consequently, with $$c=\sum_ic_i$$ and $$M_1=\sum_ic_im_i^2$$, for fixed masses as $$\Lambda\to\infty$$ (terms with $$m_i=0$$ are omitted from the logarithmic sum),

$$
\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\Big[\kappa_f\,c\,\Lambda^2-\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln\frac{\Lambda^2}{m_i^2}+C_fM_1\Big]+o(1).\tag{3}
$$

(i) If $$c\neq0$$, then $$G\Lambda^2=\pi/(\kappa_fc)$$ to leading order, which depends on $$f$$ through $$\kappa_f$$; only the sign and the zero set of $$c$$ are independent of $$f$$ for $$\kappa_f>0$$.

(ii) If $$c=0$$ and $$M_1=0$$, then $$\frac1{16\pi G}=\frac1{16\pi^2}\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln m_i^2+o(1)$$, finite and independent of $$f$$ and of $$\Lambda$$.

(iii) If $$c=0$$ and $$M_1\neq0$$, then $$1/G$$ depends on $$\ln\Lambda$$ with coefficient $$-M_1/8\pi^2$$ and on $$f$$ through the constant $$C_fM_1/16\pi^2$$.
</div>

The proof isolates the one non-analytic term of $$J$$, $$\epsilon\ln\epsilon$$, whose coefficient does not depend on $$f$$; the constants $$\kappa_f$$ and $$C_f$$ carry all the dependence on the shape of the regulator.

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The integral $$\kappa_f$$ converges at $$u\to0$$ by the assumption on $$f$$ and at infinity because $$f\le1$$. Differentiating, $$J'(\epsilon)=-\int_0^\infty u^{-1}f(u)e^{-\epsilon u}du$$. Write $$f=\theta(u-1)+(f-\theta(u-1))$$. The first part gives $$-E_1(\epsilon)=\ln\epsilon+\gamma_E+O(\epsilon)$$. The second part has an integrable integrand $$(f-\theta(u-1))/u$$, by the assumptions at $$u\to0$$ and $$u\to\infty$$, so it tends to $$-A_f$$ by dominated convergence. Hence $$J'(\epsilon)=\ln\epsilon+\gamma_E-A_f+o(1)$$, and integrating from $$0$$, where $$J(0)=\kappa_f$$, gives $$J(\epsilon)=\kappa_f+\epsilon\ln\epsilon+(\gamma_E-A_f-1)\epsilon+o(\epsilon)$$. Inserting (2) in (1) with $$\epsilon_i=m_i^2/\Lambda^2$$ gives (3); the remainder is $$\Lambda^2o(\epsilon_i)=o(1)$$ at fixed $$m_i$$. Part (i) is the leading term of (3), $$1/16\pi G=\kappa_fc\Lambda^2/16\pi^2$$. For parts (ii) and (iii) the term $$\kappa_fc\Lambda^2$$ vanishes, and $$-\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln(\Lambda^2/m_i^2)=\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln m_i^2-M_1\ln\Lambda^2$$; if $$M_1=0$$ this and $$C_fM_1$$ vanish as well. $$\square$$
</div>

Four regulators satisfy the assumptions: the sharp cutoff $$f=\theta(u-1)$$, with $$\kappa_f=1$$; $$f=e^{-1/u}$$, with $$\kappa_f=1$$; $$f=e^{-1/u^2}$$, with $$\kappa_f=\sqrt\pi/2=0.8862$$; and $$f=(1-e^{-u})^2$$, with $$\kappa_f=2\ln2=1.3863$$. The last is the regulator of two Pauli–Villars fields, of squared masses $$\Lambda^2$$ and $$2\Lambda^2$$ and weights $$-2$$ and $$+1$$. Their constants are $$C_f=-0.4228,\,0.1544,\,-0.1342,\,-0.3069$$. Direct quadrature of $$J$$ confirms (2): the remainder $$(J(\epsilon)-\kappa_f-\epsilon\ln\epsilon-C_f\epsilon)/\epsilon$$ has modulus below $$0.03$$ at $$\epsilon=10^{-2}$$ and below $$6\times10^{-4}$$ at $$\epsilon=10^{-4}$$ for each of them. The ratio of the largest to the smallest $$\kappa_f$$ is $$1.564$$, which is the factor by which $$G\Lambda^2$$ varies among these four.

Rescaling $$\Lambda^2\to\kappa_f\Lambda^2$$ turns every such regulator into one with $$\kappa_f=1$$, so the $$f$$-dependence in (i) concerns the definition of $$\Lambda$$. A value of $$G\Lambda^2$$ has content only once $$\Lambda$$ is defined by a prescription that does not refer to $$G$$, such as the mass of a Pauli–Villars field or a lattice spacing, and different prescriptions give different values. Proposition 1 does not show that no such prescription exists. In case (ii) there is no cutoff to define: $$G\,m_*^2$$ is a function of the ratios of the masses, so that $$G$$ is set by the overall mass scale $$m_*$$ of the species in the sum and is not a number in units of a cutoff.

## 2. Counting species

The character $$c$$ of a matter content is the sum of the rows of the table and does not depend on masses. For the Standard Model with 45 Weyl fermions, twelve gauge fields (with ghosts) and a Higgs doublet of four real components with coupling $$\xi R\,H^\dagger H$$, each real component contributes $$\tfrac12(\tfrac16-\xi)$$, and
$$c=2(\tfrac16-\xi)+\tfrac{45}{12}-4=\tfrac1{12}-2\xi .$$
This equals $$\tfrac1{12}$$ at $$\xi=0$$, $$-\tfrac14$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$, and vanishes at $$\xi=\tfrac1{24}$$. With three right-handed neutrinos, 48 Weyl fermions, $$c=\tfrac13-2\xi$$, which is $$\tfrac13$$ at $$\xi=0$$ and $$0$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$: the four conformally coupled scalars contribute $$0$$, the fermions $$4$$ and the gauge fields $$-4$$. The vanishing is arithmetic; no mechanism that keeps it at higher order is claimed. In the cutoff case the sign of $$c$$ is the sign of $$1/G$$, so a matter content with $$c<0$$ has a negative induced coefficient.

## 3. The Standard Model content as the source of $$G$$

In the cutoff case the leading term of (3) equals the observed value $$1/16\pi G=M_{\rm P}^2/16\pi=2.97\times10^{36}$$ GeV$$^2$$, with $$M_{\rm P}=G^{-1/2}=1.221\times10^{19}$$ GeV, when $$\Lambda=\sqrt{\pi/(\kappa_fc)}\,M_{\rm P}$$. This requires $$c>0$$, that is $$\xi<\tfrac1{24}$$ for the Standard Model alone and $$\xi<\tfrac16$$ with three right-handed neutrinos. At $$\xi=0$$ the four regulators give:

| regulator | $$\kappa_f$$ | $$\Lambda/M_{\rm P}$$ for $$c=\tfrac13$$ | $$\Lambda/M_{\rm P}$$ for $$c=\tfrac1{12}$$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| sharp, $$e^{-1/u}$$ | $$1$$ | $$3.07$$ | $$6.14$$ |
| $$e^{-1/u^2}$$ | $$0.886$$ | $$3.26$$ | $$6.52$$ |
| $$(1-e^{-u})^2$$ | $$1.386$$ | $$2.61$$ | $$5.22$$ |

The required cutoff is above the Planck mass for every regulator, and its value is set by the regulator. A cutoff $$\Lambda\le M_{\rm P}$$ needs $$c\ge\pi/\kappa_f$$, which for the Standard Model alone means $$\xi\le(\tfrac1{12}-\pi/\kappa_f)/2=-1.53,\,-1.73,\,-1.09$$ for $$\kappa_f=1,\,0.886,\,1.386$$; three right-handed neutrinos raise each of these thresholds by $$\tfrac18$$.

In the finite case $$c=0$$, so the cutoff term of (3) is absent. We take the Standard Model with three right-handed neutrinos at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$, where the unbroken-phase count of section 2 gives $$c=0$$. Then (3) reads $$\frac1{16\pi G}=-\frac1{16\pi^2}\big[M_1\ln\Lambda^2-\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln m_i^2-C_fM_1\big]$$, and Proposition 1(iii) applies whenever $$M_1\neq0$$.

In the broken phase the list of species with masses, and with it the evaluation of $$M_1$$, depends on the gauge. In the 't Hooft–Feynman gauge each massive vector together with its ghosts has $$c=-\tfrac13$$ (vector $$-\tfrac16$$, ghosts $$-\tfrac16$$), the three Goldstone modes carry the coupling $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ of the doublet and have $$c=0$$, and the Higgs boson has $$c=0$$; the total is $$c=0$$, as in the unbroken phase. In unitary gauge the $$W^\pm$$ and $$Z$$ are Proca fields with $$c=-\tfrac14$$ each, the Goldstone modes are absent, and the total is $$4-3-\tfrac34=\tfrac14$$: a Proca field has a minimally coupled longitudinal mode, with $$c=\tfrac1{12}$$ instead of $$0$$, and three of them add $$\tfrac14$$. The quadratic divergence therefore cancels in the first gauge and not in the second, the off-shell induced action being gauge dependent. We use the first, because $$c=0$$ is the condition that defines the case. A tree-level contribution $$-\xi v^2/2$$ to $$1/16\pi G$$ from the Higgs vacuum value $$v=246$$ GeV, equal to $$-5.0\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$, is not part of Proposition 1; it has the same sign as the loop term and is also far below the observed value.

With the masses (GeV) top $$172.6$$, bottom $$4.18$$, charm $$1.27$$, strange $$0.093$$, down $$0.0047$$, up $$0.0022$$, tau $$1.777$$, muon $$0.1057$$, electron $$0.000511$$, $$W$$ $$80.38$$, $$Z$$ $$91.19$$, neutrinos massless, the 't Hooft–Feynman gauge in which ghost masses equal the vector masses, and $$c=\tfrac12$$ for each coloured quark ($$N_c/6$$), $$\tfrac16$$ for each charged lepton, $$-\tfrac23$$ for the $$W^\pm$$ pair and $$-\tfrac13$$ for the $$Z$$,

$$
M_1=7.8\times10^3\ \mathrm{GeV}^2,\tag{4}
$$

the fermions contributing $$+14.9\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$, almost all of it from the top quark, and the vectors $$-7.1\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$. The logarithmic term of (3) then has the value $$1/16\pi G=-110,\,-339,\,-1.7\times10^3,\,-3.8\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$ for $$\Lambda=10^3,\,10^4,\,10^{10},\,1.2\times10^{19}$$ GeV. It is negative, and smaller in modulus than the observed value by $$3.7\times10^{-35}$$, $$1.1\times10^{-34}$$, $$5.8\times10^{-34}$$ and $$1.3\times10^{-33}$$. The regulator-dependent constant $$C_fM_1/16\pi^2$$ is at most $$21$$ GeV$$^2$$ in modulus for the four regulators of section 1, which is $$19\%$$ of the value at $$\Lambda=1$$ TeV and negligible at the larger cutoffs. For the Standard Model alone at $$\xi=\tfrac1{24}$$, where $$c=0$$ with the Higgs boson and the Goldstone modes each contributing $$\tfrac1{16}$$ (Higgs mass $$125.25$$ GeV, Goldstone masses equal to the vector masses in this gauge), $$M_1=10.1\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$ and the logarithmic term runs from $$-178$$ to $$-4.9\times10^3$$ GeV$$^2$$ over the same range of $$\Lambda$$. These are one-loop evaluations with the structure of Proposition 1; they assume free fields with the masses of the broken phase, the species character of section 1, and the gauge stated above.

Consequently the Standard Model content fixes $$1/G$$ only through a cutoff placed above the Planck mass (for $$\xi>-1.09$$ and each of the four regulators), or, with the quadratic divergence cancelled, at a value 33 to 35 orders of magnitude below the observed one. A sector that supplies the observed $$1/G$$ in the finite case needs $$\sum_ic_im_i^2\ln(\ldots)\sim16\pi^2M_{\rm P}^2/16\pi$$, so masses of order $$10^{18}$$ to $$10^{19}$$ GeV; $$G$$ is then set by whatever is heaviest.

## 4. The area coefficient of a causal-set entropy

In the entanglement picture the coefficient of $$A/4G$$ in the entropy of a horizon is renormalised by the same fluctuations that renormalise $$1/G$$ [[SU]](#ref-su) [[Jac94]](#ref-jac94) [[LW]](#ref-lw), and for a minimally coupled scalar the divergent area term of the entanglement entropy equals $$A/4G$$ with the induced $$G$$ [[SU]](#ref-su) [[LW]](#ref-lw). On a causal set the density $$\rho$$ supplies the cutoff. If the area coefficient of the entropy of a region is a fixed number times $$\rho^{(d-2)/d}A$$, this defines $$G$$ in units of the discreteness length $$\ell=\rho^{-1/d}$$. We examine whether that coefficient is fixed.

For a free scalar on a sprinkled causal set let $$K$$ be the retarded Green function, a matrix supported on related pairs, and $$i\Delta=i(K-K^T)$$ the Pauli–Jordan operator, Hermitian and imaginary. The Sorkin–Johnston state has two-point function $$W$$ equal to the positive part of $$i\Delta$$. For a region $$\mathcal R$$ with restrictions $$W_{\mathcal R}$$ and $$(i\Delta)_{\mathcal R}$$, the spacetime entanglement entropy of Sorkin [[Sor12]](#ref-sor12) is $$S=\sum_\lambda\lambda\ln\lvert\lambda\rvert$$ over the generalised eigenvalues $$\lambda$$ of $$W_{\mathcal R}v=\lambda\,(i\Delta)_{\mathcal R}v$$, restricted to the range of $$(i\Delta)_{\mathcal R}$$. For a single mode with symplectic eigenvalue $$\nu\ge\tfrac12$$ the pair $$\lambda=\tfrac12\pm\nu$$ gives $$(\nu+\tfrac12)\ln(\nu+\tfrac12)-(\nu-\tfrac12)\ln(\nu-\tfrac12)$$, the Bose–Einstein entropy, which is why the formula is the entropy of the Gaussian state. The untruncated Sorkin–Johnston entropy obeys a volume law in $$1+1$$ dimensions [[SY]](#ref-sy). An area law is recovered if the modes of $$i\Delta$$ with small eigenvalues are projected out of $$W$$ and $$i\Delta$$, once for the whole causal set and once for the region (the double truncation of [[SY]](#ref-sy) [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll)). Rules for the two truncations are called schemes. Throughout, $$N$$ is the number of points of the causal set, $$n$$ the number in the region, $$d$$ the dimension, and a knee factor $$k$$ sets the number of retained modes. The schemes used below are the untruncated state (U); the mode-count scheme (M), which retains the $$k_N=\mathrm{round}(kN^{(d-1)/d})$$ largest positive modes of $$i\Delta$$ and their negative partners globally, and then $$\mathrm{round}(kn^{(d-1)/d})$$ positive modes (and partners) of the restricted operator; and the threshold scheme (T), which retains the same $$k_N$$ modes globally and applies to the restricted operator the absolute threshold equal to the smallest retained global eigenvalue.

### 4.1 Logarithms and the extraction of the area coefficient

Let $$F(\rho)$$ be the mean over sprinklings of density $$\rho$$ of an observable of a fixed region, and let $$\theta=\rho\,\partial_\rho$$. A thinning of a sprinkling is a sprinkling of lower density, so $$\theta$$ acts on $$F$$ as the generator of density dilations, and the powers $$\rho^\beta$$ are its eigenvectors. A power times a logarithm, $$\rho^\beta\ln\rho$$, is a generalised eigenvector: $$(\theta-\beta)$$ maps it to $$\rho^\beta$$. The coefficient of the area term of the entropy of a boundary of dimension $$d-1$$ is the component of $$F$$ at the exponent $$\beta_A=(d-2)/d$$. It is extracted by a function $$P(\theta)$$ that vanishes on the exponents of the other terms and equals $$1$$ at $$\beta_A$$; the extraction of powers of the density from combinations of thinned counts is treated on the page on [horizon counts and the area law](/quantum-gravity/area-projector/). The question here is what such an extraction returns when some terms carry a logarithm.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 2** (Extraction of a coefficient in the presence of logarithms). Let $$Z$$ be a finite set of exponents with $$\beta_A\notin Z$$, and let

$$
F(\rho)=\sum_{\beta\in Z}\rho^\beta(a_\beta+b_\beta\ln\rho)+\rho^{\beta_A}(b\ln\rho+e).
$$

Let $$P$$ be an entire function with $$P(\beta)=0$$ for $$\beta\in Z$$ and $$P(\beta_A)=1$$. Then

$$
P(\theta)F=\sum_{\beta\in Z}P'(\beta)\,b_\beta\,\rho^\beta+\rho^{\beta_A}\big(b\ln\rho+e+P'(\beta_A)\,b\big).\tag{5}
$$

Consequently: (a) $$P(\theta)$$ removes the whole term $$\rho^\beta(a_\beta+b_\beta\ln\rho)$$, $$\beta\in Z$$, if and only if $$P'(\beta)=0$$ as well, that is $$\beta$$ is a double zero of $$P$$ (or $$b_\beta=0$$); (b) the coefficient $$b$$ of $$\rho^{\beta_A}\ln\rho$$ in $$P(\theta)F$$ is the same for every admissible $$P$$; (c) the constant $$e$$ of the area term shifts by $$P'(\beta_A)\,b$$ and is therefore determined only up to the choice of $$P$$ when $$b\neq0$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* $$\theta\rho^\beta=\beta\rho^\beta$$, so $$P(\theta)\rho^\beta=P(\beta)\rho^\beta$$. Because $$\rho^\beta\ln\rho=\partial_\beta\rho^\beta$$ and $$\theta$$ commutes with $$\partial_\beta$$, $$P(\theta)\rho^\beta\ln\rho=\partial_\beta\big[P(\beta)\rho^\beta\big]=P'(\beta)\rho^\beta+P(\beta)\rho^\beta\ln\rho$$. Insert $$P(\beta)=0$$ on $$Z$$ and $$P(\beta_A)=1$$ to obtain (5). Statements (a) to (c) read off the coefficients; for (c), replacing $$P(\beta)$$ by $$P(\beta)(1+\lambda(\beta-\beta_A))$$ keeps the admissibility conditions and changes $$P'(\beta_A)$$ by $$\lambda$$. $$\square$$
</div>

In two dimensions the area exponent is $$\beta_A=0$$, because the boundary of a region is a set of points, and a logarithm of the density is expected on general grounds. The entropy of an interval of length $$L$$ in a conformal field theory with central charge $$c_{\rm cft}$$ is $$\tfrac{c_{\rm cft}}3\ln(L/\varepsilon)$$ for a short-distance cutoff $$\varepsilon$$; with two endpoints and $$\varepsilon\propto\rho^{-1/2}$$ this is $$\tfrac{c_{\rm cft}}{12}\ln\rho$$ per boundary point plus terms independent of $$\rho$$. Proposition 2 then gives the following conditional statement, which rests on an assumed form of the entropy of the sprinkled theory and is not a theorem about it.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 3** (Two dimensions, conditional on the form of the entropy). Suppose the mean entropy per boundary point of a region of a sprinkled theory of $$N_{\rm sp}$$ massless scalars ($$c_{\rm cft}=1$$ each) in $$1+1$$ dimensions has the form of $$F$$ in Proposition 2 with $$\beta_A=0$$ and $$b=N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}/12$$, that is $$\tfrac{N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}}{12}\ln(\rho\,\ell_{\rm IR}^2)$$ for an infrared scale $$\ell_{\rm IR}$$, plus a constant and powers $$\rho^\beta$$, $$\beta\in Z$$, possibly with logarithms. Define $$1/4G_{\rm eff}$$ as the exponent-$$0$$ component of the entropy per boundary point, extracted by an admissible $$P$$. Then $$1/4G_{\rm eff}=a_P+\tfrac{N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}}{12}\ln(\rho\,\ell_{\rm IR}^2)$$, where the constant $$a_P$$ depends on $$P$$ and the slope $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}/12$$ does not. In particular $$1/4G_{\rm eff}$$ depends on the density, and there is no density-independent induced $$G$$ unless $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}=0$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* This is Proposition 2(b) and (c) with $$\beta_A=0$$: a term in $$\rho^0\ln\rho$$ sits at the area exponent and no admissible $$P$$ removes it, while the constant $$a_P=a+P'(0)\,b$$, with $$a$$ the constant part of $$F$$ at exponent $$0$$, shifts with $$P$$. $$\square$$
</div>

The assumption is tested numerically in $$1+1$$ dimensions. We computed the spacetime entropy of the causal diamond $$\lvert t\rvert+\lvert x\rvert<\tfrac12$$ inside the diamond $$\lvert t\rvert+\lvert x\rvert<1$$, with the massless retarded function $$K=\tfrac12$$ on related pairs, for nested sprinklings with $$N=250,354,500,707,1000,1414,2000$$ points (the same points thinned), in schemes M and T. The slope $$dS/d\ln N$$ expected from $$c_{\rm cft}=1$$ and two endpoints is $$\tfrac16=0.167$$.

| knee factor $$k$$ | slope, scheme M | slope, scheme T | $$\langle S\rangle$$ at $$N=2000$$, M | $$\langle S\rangle$$ at $$N=2000$$, T |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$0.5$$ | $$0.158\pm0.009$$ | $$0.160\pm0.017$$ | $$0.92$$ | $$0.66$$ |
| $$1$$ | $$0.156\pm0.012$$ | $$0.193\pm0.017$$ | $$1.31$$ | $$1.00$$ |
| $$2$$ | $$0.057\pm0.042$$ | $$0.182\pm0.023$$ | $$2.18$$ | $$1.68$$ |

The slopes are fits of the entropy against $$\ln N$$ for each of six independent sprinklings, with mean and standard error. For $$k=0.5$$ and $$k=1$$ both schemes give slopes within about $$1.5$$ standard errors of $$\tfrac16$$. With $$k=2$$ scheme M retains so many modes that the entropy no longer grows with $$N$$, while T stays near $$\tfrac16$$. The constant term depends on the scheme and on $$k$$: the mean entropy at $$N=2000$$ changes by more than a factor $$2$$ between $$k=0.5$$ and $$k=2$$. The logarithmic coefficient assumed in Proposition 3 is therefore reproduced where the truncation is not too weak, and the constant is not a property of the sprinkling alone.

### 4.2 Dilation identities in $$2+1$$ dimensions

Consider a sprinkling in a causal diamond $$D(L)=\{\lvert t\rvert+r<L\}$$ in $$2+1$$ dimensions and the concentric sub-diamond $$R_h=\{\lvert t\rvert+r<h\}$$, whose edge is the circle of length $$2\pi h$$. An area law would read $$\langle S\rangle=2\pi s_1\,h\,\rho^{1/3}+c_0$$ with $$s_1$$ a property of the substrate. The statements below use a hypothesis on the scheme which holds for U, M and T: the scheme depends on the causal order (the retarded function used below depends on the order alone) and on the numbers $$N$$ and $$n$$, and on nothing else.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 4** (Dilation invariance and the outer-diamond identity). If the scheme depends only on the causal order and on $$N$$ and $$n$$, then $$\langle S\rangle=F(x,y)$$ with $$x=h\rho^{1/3}$$ and $$y=h/L$$. Define $$s_1^h=(2\pi\rho^{1/3})^{-1}\partial_h\langle S\rangle\vert_{\rho,L}$$ and $$s_1^\rho=(2\pi h)^{-1}\partial_{\rho^{1/3}}\langle S\rangle\vert_{h,L}$$. Then

$$
s_1^h-s_1^\rho=-\frac1{2\pi\,h\,\rho^{1/3}}\;\frac{\partial\langle S\rangle}{\partial\ln L}\bigg\vert_{h,\rho}.\tag{6}
$$

</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* The dilation $$x\mapsto\lambda x$$ maps a sprinkling of density $$\rho$$ in $$D(L)$$ to one of density $$\lambda^{-3}\rho$$ in $$D(\lambda L)$$, the sub-diamond $$R_h$$ to $$R_{\lambda h}$$, and preserves the order, hence the retarded function up to a constant factor and the numbers $$N,n$$. The matrices $$W$$ and $$i\Delta$$ scale together, the generalised eigenvalues do not change, and $$\langle S\rangle$$ is invariant under $$(h,L,\rho)\to(\lambda h,\lambda L,\lambda^{-3}\rho)$$. The invariants are $$x$$ and $$y$$. Now $$\partial_h\langle S\rangle=\rho^{1/3}F_x+F_y/L$$, $$\partial_{\rho^{1/3}}\langle S\rangle=hF_x$$ and $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L=-yF_y$$. Hence $$2\pi(s_1^h-s_1^\rho)=F_y/(L\rho^{1/3})=-\partial_{\ln L}\langle S\rangle/(h\rho^{1/3})$$. $$\square$$
</div>

So the area coefficient read from the dependence on the size of the region ($$s_1^h$$) equals the one read from the dependence on the density ($$s_1^\rho$$) exactly when the entropy of a fixed region does not depend on the diamond that contains it. A stronger statement holds for a decomposition of the entropy into an area term and a term that does not depend on the region.

<div class="proposition" markdown="1">
**Proposition 5** (Area-plus-background decomposition). Under the hypothesis of Proposition 4, the following are equivalent: (a) $$\langle S\rangle=2\pi s_1\,h\rho^{1/3}+B(L,\rho)$$ for all $$(h,L,\rho)$$ with $$s_1$$ constant; (b) $$F(x,y)=2\pi s_1x+g(x/y)$$ for a function $$g$$. In that case $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L\vert_{h,\rho}=L\rho^{1/3}g'(L\rho^{1/3})$$ does not depend on $$h$$.
</div>

<div class="proof" markdown="1">
*Proof.* At fixed $$(L,\rho)$$, varying $$h$$ moves $$(x,y)$$ along the ray $$x/y=L\rho^{1/3}$$. Statement (a) says that $$F-2\pi s_1x$$ is independent of $$h$$ at fixed $$(L,\rho)$$, that is constant on each ray, which is (b), with $$B(L,\rho)=g(L\rho^{1/3})$$. Differentiating $$B$$ with respect to $$\ln L$$ at fixed $$\rho$$ gives the last statement. $$\square$$
</div>

### 4.3 Numerical results in $$2+1$$ dimensions

We took sprinklings of $$N=2000$$ points in $$D(1)$$ and the same sprinklings thinned to $$1414$$ points, so that $$\rho=3N/2\pi$$ (the volume of $$D(1)$$ is $$2\pi/3$$) and $$\rho^{1/3}=9.85$$ and $$8.77$$. The entropy $$\langle S\rangle$$ was computed for the concentric sub-diamonds $$h=0.30,0.375,0.45,0.525,0.60$$, whose boundary lengths $$2\pi h\rho^{1/3}$$ range from $$16$$ to $$37$$ in units of $$\ell$$, with the kernel $$K_{ij}=C_{ij}(n_{ij}+1)^{-1/3}$$ on related pairs, where $$C_{ij}=1$$ if $$i$$ precedes $$j$$ and $$n_{ij}$$ is the number of elements between them (the discretised retarded function of [[BBLL]](#ref-bbll); the overall constant drops out of $$S$$). Schemes M and T use $$k=1$$. The coefficient $$s_1^h$$ is the slope of the least-squares line through the five values of $$\langle S\rangle$$ against $$2\pi h\rho^{1/3}$$, computed for each sprinkling; the table gives the mean and standard error over eight sprinklings.

| scheme | $$s_1^h$$, $$N=1414$$ | $$s_1^h$$, $$N=2000$$ |
|---|---|---|
| U | $$7.73\pm0.13$$ | $$9.42\pm0.11$$ |
| M | $$0.046\pm0.013$$ | $$0.072\pm0.012$$ |
| T | $$0.179\pm0.005$$ | $$0.187\pm0.007$$ |

The untruncated entropy is of volume type: its mean at $$N=1414$$ is $$27,56,90,117,156$$ for the five values of $$h$$, which is $$0.5$$ to $$0.75$$ per point of the region, and the fitted $$s_1^h$$ is not an area coefficient. In scheme M the mean entropy at $$N=2000$$, $$9.7,11.0,11.7,11.6,11.1$$, rises and saturates with $$h$$, so a straight line in $$h$$ does not describe it. In scheme T the means $$0.69,1.24,2.16,3.06,4.13$$ are close to linear in $$h$$.

The coefficient read from the density dependence at fixed $$h$$, by a least-squares fit through the origin of $$\Delta S$$ between $$N=2000$$ and $$N=1414$$ against $$2\pi h\,\Delta\rho^{1/3}$$, is $$s_1^\rho=0.424\pm0.024$$ for M and $$0.137\pm0.014$$ for T. The difference $$s_1^h-s_1^\rho$$, which by (6) measures the dependence of the entropy on the enclosing diamond, is $$-0.35\pm0.03$$ for M and $$+0.05\pm0.02$$ for T (errors treated as independent, using $$N=2000$$ for $$s_1^h$$).

To test (6) directly we sprinkled $$D(1.26)$$ at the density of $$1414$$ points in $$D(1)$$, so that $$D(1.26)$$ holds about twice as many points ($$2770$$ to $$2930$$ points against $$1360$$ to $$1480$$ in $$D(1)$$), and computed the entropy of the same sub-diamond as a region of $$D(1)$$ (the points of the sprinkling inside $$D(1)$$) and of $$D(1.26)$$. The finite difference $$\Delta\langle S\rangle/\ln1.26$$, an estimate of $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L$$, has mean and standard error over $$13$$ sprinklings:

| scheme | $$h=0.30$$ | $$h=0.45$$ | $$h=0.60$$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| M | $$2.0\pm0.3$$ | $$8.0\pm0.3$$ | $$15.8\pm0.2$$ |
| T | $$-2.4\pm0.5$$ | $$-2.0\pm0.4$$ | $$-1.95\pm0.12$$ |

For M the entropy of a fixed region changes by $$0.5$$, $$1.9$$ and $$3.6$$ nats when the enclosing diamond is enlarged by $$1.26$$, and the derivative grows with $$h$$; by Proposition 5 the decomposition into an area term and a term independent of $$h$$ fails. For T the change is about $$-0.5$$ nats at each $$h$$, and the three derivatives are consistent with one value, $$-1.98\pm0.11$$ ($$\chi^2=0.8$$ for two degrees of freedom), so the decomposition of Proposition 5 holds within errors, with $$s_1=s_1^h\approx0.19$$ and a background term that depends on $$L$$ and $$\rho$$; the density-direction value $$s_1^\rho$$ differs from $$s_1^h$$ because of that term. Equation (6) is satisfied in sign and size: at $$h=0.45$$ the right side is $$-8.0/(2\pi\cdot0.45\cdot8.77)=-0.32$$ for M against the left side $$-0.35\pm0.03$$, and $$+0.08$$ for T against $$+0.05\pm0.02$$. The two sides use different finite differences in $$\rho$$ and $$L$$, so they are compared only in sign and rough size.

These results are for one kernel, the schemes defined above, up to $$2930$$ points, and regions of boundary length $$16$$ to $$37$$ in units of $$\ell$$. They show that in the mode-count scheme the entropy has no area-plus-background form in this range, and that in the threshold scheme the value of $$s_1$$ depends on how it is read: $$0.19$$ from the size of the region, $$0.14$$ from the density. They do not show that no scheme exists for which $$s_1$$ is a property of the sprinkling alone, and the trend of these numbers with $$N$$ is within errors between $$1414$$ and $$2000$$ points.

## 5. Cases not covered and open points

The results leave the following cases open, each with its own conditions.

* Finite induced gravity with a heavy sector. If $$c=M_1=0$$ for a spectrum with positive norms and masses of order $$10^{18}$$ to $$10^{19}$$ GeV, $$G$$ is finite and regulator independent (Proposition 1(ii)); it is then a function of mass ratios and of one overall mass.
* The Higgs coupling. The thresholds of section 3 depend on $$\xi$$, and the vanishing of $$c$$ at $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ with three right-handed neutrinos holds at one loop; whether it survives at two loops, where $$\xi=\tfrac16$$ is not protected, is not known. The broken-phase count depends on the gauge, as stated in section 3, and the gauge-independent content of $$M_1$$ has not been determined.
* Regulators built from Pauli–Villars fields, which carry an indefinite spectral weight: the state-based entropy of such a regulated theory is the subject of the page on [entropy of states in indefinite-metric theories](/quantum-gravity/indefinite-metric-entropy/).
* Entanglement-based definitions in which the ultraviolet entanglement density is taken to equal $$1/4G$$ as an input [[Jac15]](#ref-jac15) [[BM]](#ref-bm). Newton's constant is then a parameter of the construction.
* Truncation schemes or states other than those tested. A state defined by local Hadamard conditions or by a different regularisation of the Pauli–Jordan operator might give an area coefficient independent of the diamond; Propositions 4 and 5 give the criterion. Whether a scheme exists for which $$\partial\langle S\rangle/\partial\ln L\to0$$ as $$L\rho^{1/3}\to\infty$$ for a fixed region is not known.
* The density-graded extraction of Proposition 2 applied to the untruncated entropy. If the volume term of the untruncated state is an eigenvector at the exponent $$1$$ with no accompanying logarithm, a suitable $$P$$ removes it in the mean; this has not been tested, and the variance of the untruncated entropy is not controlled by the mean.
* Proposition 3 does not apply if the entropy of the sprinkled theory has no logarithm in two dimensions, or if $$N_{\rm sp}c_{\rm cft}=0$$; the form assumed there has been tested in $$1+1$$ dimensions only to the accuracy stated in section 4.1.
